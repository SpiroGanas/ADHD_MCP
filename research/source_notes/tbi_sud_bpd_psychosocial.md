## Q1. Traumatic brain injury (including mild TBI and blast): VA effect sizes, prevalence, EHR signals, and the overlap with ADHD

### Takeaway
VA national cohort data consistently show that a TBI diagnosis carries about 1.5–2.2 times the hazard of suicide death. The estimates are aHR 1.55 (Brenner 2011), aHR 1.71 (Hostetter 2019) and MRR 1.56 among post-9/11 veterans (Howard 2023). The risk persists after psychiatric adjustment, also applies to mild TBI, and is partly mediated by new-onset mental health conditions (Brenner 2023). Comorbid TBI/PTSD carries about 4 times the odds of a recorded intentional self-harm diagnosis. TBI symptoms (inattention, slowed processing, irritability, impulsivity) overlap heavily with ADHD and PTSD, and no veteran-validated method separates pre-injury ADHD from post-injury deficits. Distinguishing them rests on documented childhood onset and pre-injury records.

### Cited Findings
**Effect sizes (VA/DoD cohorts)**
- Brenner LA, Ignacio RV, Blow FC. "Suicide and traumatic brain injury among individuals seeking Veterans Health Administration services." *J Head Trauma Rehabil* 2011;26(4):257-264, doi:10.1097/HTR.0b013e31821fdb6e. In this VHA cohort with National Death Index (NDI) linkage, veterans with a TBI history were **1.55 times (95% CI 1.24–1.92)** more likely to die by suicide than those without TBI. Check the full text for the exact adjustment set. — [PubMed 21734509](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21734509/); [CU Anschutz news](https://news.cuanschutz.edu/medicine/veterans-with-traumatic-brain-injuries-have-higher-suicide-risk)
- Hostetter TA, Hoffmire CA, Forster JE, Adams RS, Stearns-Yoder KA, Brenner LA (2019). "Suicide and TBI among individuals seeking VHA services between fiscal years 2006 and 2015." The cohort was about 1.4 million VHA users with NDI linkage. The suicide hazard was **2.19 (95% CI 2.02–2.37) unadjusted and 1.71 (1.56–1.87) adjusted** for TBI compared with no TBI. Among suicide decedents, **moderate/severe TBI was associated with firearm as the method (OR 2.39; 1.48–3.87)**. NOTE: the request cited this paper as "JAMA Psychiatry". - A related national study is titled "A national study of TBI and risk of suicide and unintended death by overdose and firearms". It looked at unintentional overdose and firearm deaths as well as suicide. Numbers were not retrieved. — [PubMed 31822136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31822136/)
- Brenner LA, Forster JE, Gradus JL, et al. "Associations of Military-Related Traumatic Brain Injury With New-Onset Mental Health Conditions and Suicide Risk." *JAMA Netw Open* 2023;6(7):e2326296. The cohort was 860,892 post-deployment soldiers with DoD data linked to the NDI. Soldiers with military TBI had higher rates of new-onset PTSD, alcohol use disorder, mood disorders and combinations of these. TBI was associated with suicide **both directly and indirectly, through the new-onset diagnoses**. News coverage reported that soldiers with TBI died by suicide **21.3% sooner** after adjustment **(verify; the figure comes from news coverage)**. An invited commentary appeared at e2327644. — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2807787); [Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20230803/rates-of-newonset-mental-health-conditions-higher-in-us-soldiers-with-history-of-tbi)
- Howard JT, et al. "Trends in Suicide Rates Among Post-9/11 US Military Veterans With and Without TBI From 2006-2020." *JAMA Neurology* 2023. Over 15 years, veterans with TBI had suicide rates **56% higher** than veterans without TBI (**MRR 1.56; 95% CI 1.46–1.67**) and **3 times** the US adult rate. Annual percentage change was 14.8% with TBI and 14.4% without. In 2020 the rates were **90.81 and 56.65 per 100,000 person-years** (with and without TBI). — [JAMA Neurology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2808953); [Military.com](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2023/08/28/suicide-rates-surged-among-post-9-11-veterans-even-deaths-remained-steady-general-population-study.html)
- Howard JT, et al. "Suicide Rate Trends for Post–September 11, 2001, US Military Veterans" (*JAMA Netw Open* 2025). The suicide rate for post-9/11 veterans with TBI peaked at about **100 per 100,000**, against 61 per 100,000 without TBI. From 2020 to 2022, rates fell 12% a year with TBI and 7.5% a year without. — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2838445); [Univ. of Utah news](https://attheu.utah.edu/health-medicine/veteran-suicide-rates-are-declining-but-remain-high-for-those-with-brain-injuries-research-shows/)
- Howard JT, Stewart IJ, Amuan M, Janak JC, Pugh MJ. "Association of TBI With Mortality Among Military Veterans Serving After September 11, 2001." *JAMA Netw Open* 2022. Age-adjusted mortality rose with TBI severity. Veterans with **even mild TBI**, as well as moderate/severe TBI, were more likely to die by suicide, accident or homicide than veterans without TBI. Moderate/severe TBI accounted for 33.6% of excess deaths. — [PubMed 35147684](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35147684/); [PMC8837911](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8837911/)
- Rawat BPS, Reisman J, Pogoda TK, et al. "Intentional Self-Harm Among US Veterans With TBI or PTSD: Retrospective Cohort Study 2008–2017." *JMIR Public Health Surveill* 2023, doi:10.2196/42803. The cohort was about 6.7 million VHA users. Before the index date, 93,866 had TBI only, 892,420 PTSD only and 102,549 comorbid TBI/PTSD. Prevalence of an intentional self-harm diagnosis was **11.63% with comorbid TBI/PTSD versus 1.92% with neither**. Among high-utilization veterans, comorbid TBI/PTSD carried an **aOR of 4.26 (95% CI 4.15–4.38)**. — [PMC10407646](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10407646/); [JMIR](https://publichealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e42803)
- The Chronic Effects of Neurotrauma Consortium (CENC) published a retrospective cohort examining deployment, suicide and overdose across comorbidity phenotypes after mild TBI. Phenotype-level numbers were not retrieved. — [PMC6754132](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6754132/)

**VA TBI screening pathway (EHR-recordable)**
- Under VHA Directives 2007-013 and 2010-012, the national **TBI Clinical Reminder screen** is given to OEF/OIF/OND-era veterans who have no prior TBI diagnosis. It has 4 sequential sections: (1) an event that may increase TBI risk (blast, vehicle crash, fragment, fall, blow to the head); (2) immediate symptoms such as loss or alteration of consciousness, or amnesia; (3) new or worsening symptoms after the event; (4) current symptoms. A positive answer in each section makes a positive screen, which triggers referral for the **Comprehensive TBI Evaluation (CTBIE)** in polytrauma/TBI clinics. — [VHA Directive 2010-012](https://www.va.gov/optometry/docs/vha_directive_2010-012_screening_and_evaluation_of_possible_tbi_in_oef-oif_veterans.pdf); [VHA Directive 2007-013](https://www.va.gov/optometry/docs/vha_directive_2007-013_screening_possible_tbi.pdf); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00852527](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00852527)
- The TBI screen has **high sensitivity (about 94%) but only moderate specificity (about 59%)** for historical TBI. — [search extract of PMC validation studies, e.g., PMC4055523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4055523)

**TBI and ADHD overlap**
- Chronic mild TBI can produce cognitive and behavioral problems that resemble ADHD. In veterans with TBI, stimulant use that started before the TBI diagnosis may reflect pre-existing symptoms that surfaced through screening. — [Am J Med 2025, stimulant use in veterans with TBI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002934325007028)
- In a VA Polytrauma clinic, combat veterans with and without confirmed TBI shared attention and concentration complaints. 73 patients met ADHD criteria, and those with ADHD reported more attention problems but not more overall distress. — [PMC6363329, "ADHD in a VA Polytrauma Clinic"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6363329/)
- In post-9/11 veterans the **ASRS-v1.1 may not distinguish adult ADHD symptoms from PTSD symptoms**, and the two sets of symptoms overlap heavily. — [Military Medicine 2025;190(5-6):e1106](https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/190/5-6/e1106/7911917)
- Civilian, pediatric evidence: no acute clinical features predicted "secondary ADHD" after mild TBI in children. — [PMC12702464](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12702464/)

### Inferences
- **Prevalence:** in Rawat 2023's VHA cohort, roughly 196,000 of about 6.7 million VHA users (about 3%) had a TBI diagnosis, with or without PTSD. Over half of veterans with TBI also had PTSD, so in this population TBI should be treated as a "comorbidity-cluster" signal rather than an isolated one. This is arithmetic on the [Rawat 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10407646/) counts.
- **Proposed EHR signals that should trigger referral for mental health and/or neuropsychological screening.** These are not drawn from a validated rule. Codes are not verified in session unless marked:
  - ICD-10-CM: S06.0X- (concussion, for example S06.0X0A/S06.0X1A); S06.1–S06.9 intracranial injury, including S06.2X- diffuse and S06.3- focal; 7th character "S" for sequela; **Z87.820** personal history of TBI; **F07.81** postconcussional syndrome; G44.3- post-traumatic headache; R41.840 attention/concentration deficit; R41.844 executive function deficit; external-cause Y36.-/Y37.- (war/military operations, including explosions) and W40.- (explosion). Add **Z91.82** personal history of military deployment and **Z91.85** personal history of military service (both verified in session, [CMS FY2027 tables](https://www.cms.gov/icd10m/FY2027-fr-v44-fullcode-cms/fullcode_cms/P0996.html)).
  - VA-specific: a positive TBI Clinical Reminder; a completed CTBIE note with Neurobehavioral Symptom Inventory (NSI) scores; Polytrauma/TBI clinic stop codes; a TBI diagnosis plus PTSD (F43.1-), which per Rawat is about a 4-fold self-harm signal.
  - Escalation flags: TBI combined with firearm-related history, since moderate/severe TBI is linked to firearm as the method (Hostetter); TBI with a new-onset alcohol use disorder, mood disorder or PTSD in the first years after injury (Brenner 2023 mediation); and TBI with stimulant prescriptions or an ADHD diagnosis first recorded after the injury, which calls for neuropsychological evaluation to separate pre-existing ADHD from TBI sequelae.
- **Distinguishing pre-injury ADHD from TBI sequelae:** the most defensible EHR evidence is documentation of symptom onset before age 12, as DSM-5 requires, and before the injury. Sources include school or childhood records, prior ADHD diagnoses or stimulant prescriptions, DoD accession/MEPS records, and collateral history. New attention or impulsivity symptoms that start after a documented injury, together with PTSD and sleep disturbance, favor TBI/PTSD sequelae. No veteran-validated algorithm was found, so this is a clinical inference built on the overlap findings above.

### Gaps
- Severity-stratified suicide HRs (mild versus moderate/severe) from the 2023 JAMA Neurology and 2022 JAMA Netw Open papers could not be extracted because full text was blocked. Blast-specific suicide HRs were not found.
- No VA/DoD study was found that validates a method for distinguishing pre-injury ADHD from post-concussive attention deficits. Evidence on ADHD as a pre-injury risk factor for TBI in service members was not retrieved.
- The Brenner 2011 adjustment set, and whether 1.55 is the fully adjusted estimate, could not be confirmed. Whether NSI scores have LOINC codes was not found.
- The VA/DoD mTBI CPG (2021) recommendations were not retrieved in this session.

## Q2. Substance use disorders, chronic pain, long-term opioid therapy and tapering/discontinuation: VA effect sizes and EHR signals

### Takeaway
In VHA data every SUD type is associated with higher suicide mortality. Unadjusted HRs run from 1.35 (cocaine, men) to 4.74 (sedatives, men) and from 3.89 (cannabis, women) to 11.36 (sedatives, women), and the associations are stronger in women. Any SUD remains associated after adjustment (HR about 1.5 in men and 1.9 in women in a model that includes tobacco use disorder). Among chronic pain patients, higher opioid dose shows a dose-response association with suicide (HR 2.15 at ≥100 mg MED/day versus ≤20). Stopping long-term opioids is associated with overdose/suicide death, and the risk rises with longer prior treatment (HRs about 1.67 to 6.77, from Oliva 2020 BMJ). That study has been criticized for immortal-time bias. Buprenorphine treatment for OUD is associated with about 65% lower suicide mortality. Several pain diagnoses (back pain, migraine, psychogenic pain) independently predict suicide.

### Cited Findings
**SUDs**
- Bohnert KM, Ilgen MA, Louzon S, McCarthy JF, Katz IR. "Substance use disorders and the risk of suicide mortality among men and women in the US Veterans Health Administration." *Addiction* 2017;112(7):1193-1201, doi:10.1111/add.13774. Any current SUD and each specific SUD (alcohol, cocaine, cannabis, opioid, amphetamine, sedative) were associated with suicide. **Unadjusted HRs for men ranged from 1.35 (cocaine) to 4.74 (sedative); for women from 3.89 (cannabis) to 11.36 (sedative).** Associations for any SUD, alcohol, cocaine and opioid use disorders were **stronger in women**. — [PubMed 28301070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28301070/); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.13774)
- In the same cohort the suicide rate was **75.6 per 100,000 with any SUD versus 34.7 per 100,000 for VHA users overall**. Women with SUD had more than 5 times the suicide rate of women without SUD. The rate was highest with sedative use disorder, at **171.4 per 100,000**. — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/03/170316092949.htm); [Michigan Medicine](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/drug-and-alcohol-problems-linked-vet-suicides-especially-women)
- In a reply letter, a model further adjusted for tobacco use disorder gave **any-SUD HRs of about 1.5 for men and 1.9 for women**. — [Bohnert response, Addiction 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.13937); critique: [Aubin et al., Addiction 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.13919)
- Watts BV, Gottlieb DJ, Riblet NB, Gui J, Shiner B. "Association of Medication Treatment for Opioid Use Disorder With Suicide Mortality." *Am J Psychiatry* 2022, doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.21070700. The cohort was more than 60,000 VA patients who received MOUD from 2003 to 2017: about 50% buprenorphine, 25% methadone, 25% naltrexone. **Buprenorphine was the only medication significantly associated with lower suicide mortality in adjusted models, about 65–66% lower.** Methadone was associated only in unadjusted models, and naltrexone not at all. The accompanying editorial reports a greater than 50% reduction in suicide mortality during stable MOUD treatment. — [AJP](https://ajp.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.21070700); [Psychiatric News alert](https://alert.psychnews.org/2022/04/medications-for-opioid-use-disorder-may.html); [editorial](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.20220173)
- Problematic opioid use identified through NLP of VA clinical notes captures veterans that ICD diagnosis codes miss. This supports adding note-based signals for OUD. — [arXiv 2401.12996](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12996)

**Chronic pain**
- Ilgen MA, et al. "Noncancer Pain Conditions and Risk of Suicide." *JAMA Psychiatry* 2013. VHA records were linked to the NDI for FY2005–2008, with suicide deaths tracked FY2006–2008. After adjustment for psychiatric comorbidity, **psychogenic pain (HR 1.58), migraine (HR 1.34) and back pain (HR 1.13; 99% CI 1.03–1.24)** remained associated with suicide. Arthritis and neuropathy were not significant. — [MedicalXpress](https://m.medicalxpress.com/news/2013-05-pain-conditions-linked-suicide.html); [News24](https://www.news24.com/life/wellness/body/condition-centres/pain-management/Migraine-chronic-back-pain-a-suicide-risk-20130523)
- In a VHA case-control study, specific pain conditions remained associated with **suicide attempts** after adjustment for covariates. — [Pain Conditions and Suicide Attempts in Military Veterans: A Case-Control Design (Boska et al. 2021)](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4N2PNgY4)

**Long-term opioid therapy (LTOT): dose, discontinuation and tapering**
- Ilgen MA, et al. "Opioid dose and risk of suicide." *Pain* 2016. The cohort was 123,946 VA patients with chronic pain receiving opioids in FY2004–2005, followed to 2009. Compared with ≤20 mg morphine-equivalent per day, the suicide HRs were **1.48 (1.25–1.75) for 20 to <50, 1.69 (1.33–2.14) for 50 to <100, and 2.15 (1.64–2.81) for ≥100 mg/day**. The results were similar for suicide by intentional overdose. — [IU ScholarWorks record](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/ae6747b8-2f72-472e-b253-a9c5a49719ab); [PMC4939394](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4939394)
- Oliva EM, Bowe T, Manhapra A, et al. "Associations between stopping prescriptions for opioids, length of opioid treatment, and overdose or suicide deaths in US veterans: observational evaluation." *BMJ* 2020;368:m283, doi:10.1136/bmj.m283. The cohort was **1,394,102 VHA patients** with an outpatient opioid prescription in FY2013–2014. Stopping opioids was associated with higher risk of overdose or suicide death across every treatment-length band, and the risk grew with longer treatment (interaction P<0.001). The reported **HRs were 1.67 (≤30 days), 2.80 (31–90), 3.95 (91–400) and 6.77 (>400 days)** **(verify; HRs from a secondary ResearchGate page)**. — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Associations-between-stopping-prescriptions-for-of-Oliva-Bowe/4625e0ff26f3901789302a4a782344f518be4a48); [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356313490_The_Opioid_Safety_Initiative_and_Veteran_Suicides)
- Critique: a National Academies review rated this study at **critical risk of immortal-time bias**. — [NASEM, *Veterans, Prescription Opioids and Benzodiazepines, and Mortality, 2007–2019*](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28584/chapter/14)
- Demidenko MI, Dobscha SK, Morasco BJ, et al. (*Gen Hosp Psychiatry* 2017). Among **509 VHA patients whose clinicians discontinued LTOT** in 2012, **9.2% had suicidal ideation only and 2.4% had suicidal self-directed violence** within 12 months. Dose, SUD, pain diagnoses and benzodiazepines were not associated with these outcomes. — [PubMed 28807135](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28807135/); [QxMD](https://read.qxmd.com/read/28807135/suicidal-ideation-and-suicidal-self-directed-violence-following-clinician-initiated-prescription-opioid-discontinuation-among-long-term-opioid-users)
- **Civilian evidence:** Agnoli A, et al. *JAMA* 2021 (OptumLabs). Among 113,618 patients on stable high-dose LTOT, tapering (dose reduction of at least 15%) was associated with **overdose (aIRR 1.68) and mental health crisis (aIRR 2.28)**. Each 10% increase in maximum monthly taper velocity raised the aIRR for overdose by 1.09 and for mental health crisis by 1.18. A 2022 follow-up found elevated risk persisted 12–24 months after taper initiation (aIRR 1.57 for overdose/withdrawal, 1.52 for mental health crisis). — [PMC8335575](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8335575); [UC Davis news](https://health.ucdavis.edu/health-news/research/is-reducing-opioids-for-pain-patients-linked-to-higher-rates-of-overdose-and-mental-health-crisis/2021/08); [JAMA 2022 follow-up](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2793293)
- **VA STORM** (Stratification Tool for Opioid Risk Mitigation) is a provider dashboard that uses VHA EHR data to predict the risk of **overdose or suicide-related serious adverse events** in patients prescribed opioids. It displays risk factors such as benzodiazepines, prior adverse events and diagnoses, together with mitigation strategies. A stepped-wedge randomized program evaluation required case reviews for high-risk tiers. Sources describe the tiers as top 1% or top 10%, so the definitions vary. — [STORM evaluation protocol, PMC6339438](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6339438/); [AHRQ PSNet innovation profile](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/innovation/veterans-health-administration-stratification-tool-opioid-risk-mitigation-storm-shows); [JGIM 2022](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11606-022-07617-y.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Proposed EHR signals for referral (ICD-10-CM codes from author knowledge; verify):**
  - SUD diagnoses: F10.- (alcohol), F11.- (opioid), F12.- (cannabis), F13.- (sedative/hypnotic; the highest-risk SUD in Bohnert 2017), F14.- (cocaine), F15.- (other stimulants, including methamphetamine), F16.-/F18.-/F19.-; remission codes (for example F10.21, F11.21) still indicate history.
  - Intoxication or overdose events: T40.0X–T40.6X (opioids, including heroin T40.1X-, methadone T40.3X-, synthetic opioids/fentanyl T40.4-), T40.5X- (cocaine), T43.6- (psychostimulants, including amphetamines and methamphetamine **(verify subcodes)**), T42.4X- (benzodiazepines), T51.0X- (ethanol). The **6th character "2" = intentional self-harm, "4" = undetermined**, and undetermined poisonings should be treated as possible self-harm. Add R78.0 (alcohol in blood) and Y90.- (blood alcohol level).
  - Screening scores: AUDIT-C (75626-2) ≥4 in men or ≥3 in women is the conventional positive threshold; scores ≥8 suggest likely AUD. A positive single-item drug screen or DAST-10 ≥3 is also a signal. These thresholds are conventional and were not verified here.
  - Medication and utilization: buprenorphine, methadone (OTP) or naltrexone starts and stops, since a gap in buprenorphine removes a protective association (Watts 2022); **naloxone dispensing**, a marker of recognized overdose risk; ED/urgent care visits or admissions for poisoning; detox or residential SUD treatment stop codes; STORM high-risk tier.
  - Chronic pain and opioids: G89.2-/G89.4 (chronic pain/chronic pain syndrome); M54.5- (low back pain); G43.- (migraine); F45.41/F45.42 (pain disorder with psychological factors, the "psychogenic pain" category with HR 1.58); **Z79.891 long-term (current) use of opiate analgesic**; PEG (91148-7) scores.
  - Opioid-specific triggers for proactive screening:
    - daily MME ≥50, and especially ≥100 (Ilgen 2016);
    - concurrent benzodiazepines;
    - **any discontinuation, or a dose reduction of ≥15% (the Agnoli definition), in a patient on LTOT longer than 90 days**, with the highest risk after more than 400 days (Oliva 2020);
    - rapid taper velocity;
    - opioid discontinuation together with an SUD diagnosis.
    Re-screen for suicide (C-SSRS) and overdose risk at taper start and over the following 12–24 months.
- Women veterans with any SUD deserve a lower threshold for referral, because of the larger relative hazards reported in Bohnert 2017.

## Q3. Borderline and other personality disorders, and eating disorders: self-harm risk in veterans and diagnostic overlap with ADHD, bipolar disorder and complex PTSD

### Takeaway
In the VHA, a personality disorder (PD) diagnosis carries more than 3 times the crude suicide rate (156.5 vs 46.7 per 100,000 person-years), and Cluster B, which includes BPD, has the highest rate (178.5). PDs are under-diagnosed in the VHA relative to community samples, so EHR-based identification is insensitive. BPD is strongly linked to non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI), which in turn predicts suicide attempts. Eating disorders are common by self-report: about 33% of women and 19% of men screened positive for a probable DSM-5 eating disorder. They are almost never diagnosed in the VHA EHR (about 0.1–0.2%), and VA identifies them as associated with NSSI, suicidal ideation and attempts. BPD overlaps substantially with ADHD (an estimated 18–34% comorbidity in adults) and with complex PTSD. That evidence is civilian.

### Cited Findings
**Personality disorders**
- Nelson SM, Griffin CA, Hein TC, Bowersox N, McCarthy JF. "Personality disorder and suicide risk among patients in the Veterans Affairs health system." *Personality Disorders: Theory, Research, and Treatment* 2022;13(6):563-571, doi:10.1037/per0000521. The cohort was 5,517,024 VHA patients with at least 2 encounters in 2012–2013, followed through 2017. Suicide rates were **156.5 per 100,000 person-years with any PD versus 46.7 without**. The rate was highest for **Cluster B (borderline, antisocial etc.) at 178.5**, followed by PD-NOS at 152.6 and Cluster C at 121.4. Proportional hazards models were adjusted for age, sex, veteran status, region and other mental health diagnoses, and PD **remained associated** with suicide. **PD prevalence in VHA is lower than in community samples.** — [NYU IFP abstract listing](https://ifp.nyu.edu/?p=1655582); [BVS record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/bvsdip/resource/pt/mdl-34726449)
- In 124 veterans with mental health diagnoses, BPD was associated with lifetime suicide attempts in unadjusted analysis (χ²=11.1). In a multivariable model, BPD was associated with **NSSI disorder (aOR 7.67, p<0.001)**, and NSSI was the independent correlate of attempts. The sample is small and the estimates imprecise. — [PMC9004586](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9004586/); [PMC7855413](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7855413/)
- One veteran cohort described in a review found NSSI (48%) to be the sole independent correlate of lifetime suicide attempts (**adjusted OR 4.9**). **Civilian/mixed:** adult BPD samples average about 80% lifetime ideation, 52% lifetime attempts and 6% suicide deaths. — [PMC13441801](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13441801/)
- PD diagnoses and health-system use among older VHA veterans are examined in a separate study, which also points to under-recognition. — [PMC7741531](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7741531/)

**Eating disorders**
- Masheb RM, et al. "DSM-5 eating disorder prevalence, gender differences, and mental health associations in United States military veterans." *Int J Eat Disord* 2021;54(7):1171-1180. **32.8% of women and 18.8% of men screened positive for any probable DSM-5 eating disorder**, using a self-report screen whose validation status in veterans is questioned. — [PMC11802485 (review citing Masheb 2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11802485)
- VHA EHR data give **1-year and 5-year eating disorder diagnosis prevalence of about 0.1% and 0.2%** among post-9/11 veterans, which the authors attribute to the absence of routine eating disorder screening in VHA. Interview-based VHA studies give 3–4.7% (DSM-IV criteria). — [PMC11802485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11802485)
- Binge eating disorder symptoms are reported by 1.2–5.2% of women and 0.3–2.9% of men veterans. 35% of women veterans in one study reported binge episodes, and more than 78% of over 45,000 MOVE! weight-management participants reported binge-eating behavior. — [DoD PHCoE "Binge Eating in the Military"](https://health.mil/Military-Health-Topics/Centers-of-Excellence/Psychological-Health-Center-of-Excellence/Clinicians-Corner-Blog/Special-Populations-and-Topics/Binge-Eating-in-the-Military); [VA news](https://www.va.gov:443/HEALTH/NewsFeatures/2014/December/2015-New-Years-Resolution-End-Binge-Eating.asp)
- The VA Office of Mental Health and Suicide Prevention fact sheet on women veterans (04/2026) states that eating disorders are associated with greater risk of **NSSI, suicidal ideation and suicide attempts** and **may be associated with suicide death**. It notes that prevalence among veterans is at least as high as in the general population and recommends assessing veterans for eating-disordered behaviors. — [VA FSTP: Suicide Among Women Veterans (04/2026)](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/suicide_prevention/docs/FSTP-Suicide-Among-Women-Veterans.pdf)

**Diagnostic overlap (civilian evidence unless stated)**
- **BPD vs complex PTSD (ICD-11):** both involve affect dysregulation, negative self-concept and relational difficulties. Prolonged or repeated trauma is required for CPTSD but not for BPD. BPD is usually the more severe presentation because of suicidality and self-injury. A 2023 *Br J Psychiatry* clinical guide recommends that a shared symptom (for example emotional volatility) count toward both diagnoses while they are being considered, but not be double-counted once a primary diagnosis is made. — [BJP 2023 guide (Cambridge)](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/abs/distinguishing-between-icd11-complex-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-and-borderline-personality-disorder-clinical-guide-and-recommendations-for-future-research/ECE472B009FE51D8D11C315EC0475068); [Cardiff ORCA author copy](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/160166/1/CPTSD%20vs%20BPD%20Final%20BJP%20R2%20clear.pdf)
- A latent class analysis in a highly traumatized sample supported CPTSD as a separate construct, while BPD features overlapped heavily. — [PMC8103648](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8103648)
- **BPD vs ADHD:** adult comorbidity is estimated at about **18–34%** (15–35% in another review), and about one-third of BPD patients have ADHD. Part of this may be an artifact of shared symptoms (impulsivity, emotional dysregulation, interpersonal impairment). ADHD shows more severe impulsivity, and BPD more severe emotion-regulation difficulty. BPD impulsivity tends to show as self-harm, ADHD impulsivity as behaviors such as interrupting. A meta-analysis of 13 studies (N=2,535) found emotional lability in adult ADHD versus controls at **Hedges g = 1.20**. — [PMC9567630](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9567630/); [PMC11862682](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11862682/); [PMC5960499](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5960499/); [PMC12437113](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12437113/)

### Inferences
- **Proposed EHR signals (codes not verified in session):**
  - Personality disorders: F60.3 (BPD); F60.2 (antisocial); other F60.x and F21 (schizotypal).
  - Recurrent self-harm without suicidal intent: R45.88 (nonsuicidal self-harm); Z91.52 (personal history of nonsuicidal self-harm); S-codes for self-inflicted lacerations with X78.- (sharp object, intentional).
  - Self-harm history: **Z91.51 personal history of suicidal behavior** (verified in session, [CMS table](https://www.cms.gov/icd10m/FY2024-version41-fullcode-cms/fullcode_cms/P0969.html)).
  - Utilization pattern of frequent crisis or ED visits; DBT program enrollment.
  - Eating disorders: F50.0- anorexia; F50.2- bulimia; **F50.81- binge eating disorder**; F50.82 ARFID; F50.89/F50.9. Severity subcodes were added in FY2025 **(verify)**.
  - Eating disorder proxies: R63.4 abnormal weight loss; R63.6 underweight; Z68.1 BMI ≤19.9; E87.6 hypokalemia with no other cause; E44–E46 malnutrition; MOVE! enrollment with binge eating documented in notes.
- Because PDs and eating disorders are recorded far less often than they occur, **NLP of notes** is needed for real sensitivity. Useful note terms include "cutting", "self-injury", "splitting", "fear of abandonment", "DBT", "binge", "purging" and "restricting". Women veterans who screen positive for MST, IPV or PTSD are a high-yield group for eating disorder screening.
- On ADHD overlap: when attention, impulsivity or affective-instability complaints appear in a veteran with trauma exposure, a single EHR diagnosis (ADHD, BPD, bipolar or PTSD/CPTSD) should prompt **structured differential assessment** rather than a label taken at face value. Childhood-onset history points to ADHD. Episodic, sustained mood elevation points to bipolar disorder. Chronic interpersonal instability with self-harm points to BPD. Trauma-linked disturbances in self-organization point to CPTSD.

### Gaps
- No VHA cohort was found that reports a **BPD-specific** HR for suicide death or attempt. Nelson 2022 reports Cluster B as a group, and its adjusted HRs were not extracted.
- No veteran study of **suicide mortality in eating disorders** was found. The VA position rests on non-veteran evidence and the fact sheet.
- **BPD vs bipolar** differentiation evidence was not retrieved (searches returned nothing), and no veteran-specific BPD–ADHD comorbidity data were found.

## Q4. Psychosocial and military risk factors: MST, homelessness and housing instability, justice involvement, the military-to-civilian transition, firearm access, IPV, social isolation and financial strain

### Takeaway
Among VA-screened veterans, MST is associated with HR 1.69 for suicide in men and 2.27 in women. Housing instability carries about 1.75–1.80 times the adjusted suicide hazard, and VA homeless program use is associated with about 20% lower suicide hazard. The year after military separation is a high-risk window: rates peak 6–12 months after separation, and separation overall carries HR 1.63. Firearms were involved in 73.3% of veteran suicides in 2023, about 45% of veterans own firearms, and a third of veteran owners store at least one gun loaded and unlocked. In NLP-enriched VHA data, legal problems (aOR about 2.6), violence (about 2.3) and social problems (about 1.9) are each independently associated with suicide death. Women screening positive for IPV have about 3.8 times the odds of suicidal ideation or behavior.

### Cited Findings
**Military sexual trauma (MST)**
- Kimerling R, Makin-Byrd K, Louzon S, Ignacio RV, McCarthy JF. "Military Sexual Trauma and Suicide Mortality." *Am J Prev Med* 2016;50(6):684-691, doi:10.1016/j.amepre.2015.10.019. The cohort was VHA users screened for MST in FY2007–2011: 5,991,080 men and 360,774 women, with 9,017 suicides. **MST prevalence by screen was 1.1% in men and 21.2% in women.** MST was associated with suicide at **HR 1.69 (95% CI 1.45–1.97) in men and 2.27 (1.76–2.94) in women**, adjusted for age, rurality, medical morbidity and, in further models, psychiatric conditions. MST may "negate the protective effect of female gender". — [SPRC summary](https://sprc.org/?p=13217); [Loughborough record with DOI](https://vufind.lboro.ac.uk/PrimoRecord/cdi_unpaywall_primary_10_1016_j_amepre_2015_10_019)
- VA has run **universal MST screening of men and women since 1999**. The screen is a **two-item clinical reminder**: (1) unwanted sexual attention or harassment; (2) sexual contact under force or threat of force. Results are stored in the EHR, and the pop-up reminder stays until the clinician responds. Early data showed about 22% of women and 1% of men screening positive, with screening completed in about 70% of patients. More recent work estimates about 1 in 3 women veterans endorse MST in VHA screening. — [Kimerling et al. 2008, Psychiatr Serv, doi:10.1176/ps.2008.59.6.635](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/ps.2008.59.6.635); [PMC2089100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2089100); [PMC9949350](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949350)

**Homelessness and housing instability**
- In about 5.85 million VHA patients screened for housing instability (2012–2016), **suicide aHR was 1.75 for unstably housed veterans and 1.80 for those at imminent risk of housing instability**, against all-cause mortality aHRs of 1.69 and 1.48. Using at least one VHA homeless program was associated with **20% lower suicide hazard**, and using 4 or more programs with **78% lower** hazard (an observational dose-response finding). — [VA HSR&D CIDER bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USVHACENTER/bulletins/2788f15); [HSR&D pubbrief "Use of VHA Homeless Programs Decreases Suicide"](https://www.hsrd.research.va.gov/research/citations/pubbriefs/articles.cfm?RecordID=1072)
- Schinka JA, Leventhal KC, Lapcevic WA, Casey R. "Mortality and Cause of Death in Younger Homeless Veterans." *Public Health Rep* 2018, doi:10.1177/0033354918755709. The **suicide HR was about 2.7** for homeless versus non-homeless veterans **(verify; from a search extract)**. — [Public Health Reports](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033354918755709)
- A 2025 VA OMHSP fact sheet reports that in 2022 the suicide rate among recent VHA users experiencing homelessness was **110.2% higher** **(verify; the excerpt was truncated and the comparison group unclear)**. — [VA FSTP: Housing Instability and Homelessness (03/2025)](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/suicide_prevention/docs/FSTP-Housing-Instability-and-Homelessness-A-Risk-Factor-for-Suicide-Among-Veterans-508.pdf)
- A latent class analysis identified the subgroups of homeless VHA users at highest suicide risk. Risk was concentrated among those with high psychiatric burden. — [PubMed 35905795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35905795/); [VA NCHAV brief (Holliday 2022)](https://www.va.gov/HOMELESS/nchav/docs/Research_Brief-Holliday-2022-Combining_Medical_Social_Determinants_of_Health_and_Suicide_Data-FINAL-508.pdf)

**Justice involvement**
- Among criminal justice-involved veterans (JIVs), age-specific mortality was **2–5 times** that of the general male veteran and US adult populations. Suicide and homicide are among the leading causes of death in JIVs. — [Health & Justice 2026, doi:10.1186/s40352-026-00456-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40352-026-00456-7)
- A latent class analysis of **197,737 justice-involved veterans** in VHA justice programs found suicide risk concentrated in high-psychiatric-burden classes. — [Holliday et al., BMC Psychiatry 2023, doi:10.1186/s12888-023-04725-9](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04725-9)
- Among post-9/11 veterans, justice involvement was associated with more PTSD and depressive symptoms, recent suicidal ideation and lifetime suicide attempts. — [Holliday et al., VA-hosted PDF](https://www.va.gov/files/2023-06/Associations%20Between%20Justice%20Involvement%20and%20PTSD%20and%20Depressive%20Symptoms,%20Suicidal%20Ideation,%20and%20Suide%20Attempt%20Among%20Post-911%20Veterans.pdf)
- **Conflicting evidence:** in a 2014–2019 VHA cohort, *type* of social determinant (housing instability, unemployment or justice involvement) did **not** predict suicide mortality in adjusted models, though it did predict all-cause mortality. Use of Veterans Justice Programs was protective for all-cause mortality. — [Pandit et al., Am J Addict 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ajad.70158); [PMC13485320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485320/)
- More frequent receipt of social services for housing instability, justice involvement and unemployment was **positively associated with a documented suicide attempt**. This likely reflects need and contact rather than harm. — [PMC12474729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12474729)

**Military-to-civilian transition**
- Ravindran C, Morley SW, Stephens BM, Stanley IH, Reger MA. "Association of Suicide Risk With Transition to Civilian Life Among US Military Service Members." *JAMA Netw Open* 2020;3(9):e2016261. The cohort was **1,868,970 separated service members**, followed for suicide up to 6 years after separation. Suicide rates were time-dependent and **generally peaked 6–12 months after separation**, declining only modestly afterwards. HRs were **male sex 3.13 (2.68–3.69); shorter service 1.26 (1.11–1.42); Marine Corps 1.55 (1.36–1.78); Army 1.48 (1.31–1.67)**. — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2770538); [Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20200915/military-service-member-suicide-rates-increase-after-separation-and-vary-by-demographics)
- Reger MA, Smolenski DJ, Skopp NA, et al. *JAMA Psychiatry* 2015;72(6):561-569, doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2014.3195. The cohort was about 3.9 million service members. **Deployment was not associated with suicide (HR 0.96; 99% CI 0.87–1.05)**. **Separation was associated with suicide (HR 1.63; 99% CI 1.50–1.77)** whether or not the person had deployed. **Separation after less than 4 years of service and a discharge that is not honorable** were additional risk factors. — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/04/150401115738.htm); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/808389)

**Firearm access**
- **Firearms were involved in 73.3% of veteran suicides in 2023**, against 52.9% among non-veteran adults. Veteran firearm suicide rates rose 67.0% from 2001 to 2023. The firearm suicide rate in 2023 was 168.3% higher for female veterans than for female non-veterans, and 70.5% higher for male veterans than for male non-veterans. — [VA 2025 National Veteran Suicide Prevention Annual Report, Part 2](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/docs/data-sheets/2025/2025_Annual_Report_Part_2_508.pdf); [Part 1](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/docs/data-sheets/2025/2025_National_Veteran_Suicide_Prevention_Annual_Report_PART_1_FINAL.pdf); [Military.com](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2026/02/12/va-releases-newest-veteran-suicide-data-heres-what-they-found.html)
- **44.9% (95% CI 41.3–48.6%) of veterans own firearms**, against about 20% of non-veterans (2015 National Firearms Survey). — [Cleveland et al., Inj Epidemiol 2017, doi:10.1186/s40621-017-0130-y](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5735043/)
- Among veteran gun owners, **33.3% stored at least one gun loaded and unlocked**, 66% stored at least one unlocked, and 46.7% stored at least one loaded. — [Simonetti et al. 2018, PubMed 29658142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29658142/); [Harvard Injury Control](https://hsph.harvard.edu/injury-control/news/one-third-of-veterans-store-their-guns-loaded-and-unlocked)
- The 2024 VA/DoD Suicide CPG (May 2024) covers universal screening, risk stratification, predictive analytics and **lethal means safety**. It has 8 new recommendations, 12 reviewed and replaced, 3 amended and 1 unchanged. — [Health.mil news on the 2024 CPG](https://health.mil/News/Dvids-Articles/2024/06/07/news473280)

**Intimate partner violence (IPV)**
- VHA began universal IPV screening of women in 2014 using the **E-HITS (Extended Hurt-Insult-Threaten-Scream)**. In a JAMA Netw Open analysis of VHA screening data, a positive IPV screen in women was associated with **suicidal ideation and/or behavior at aOR 3.80 (95% CI 2.10–6.90)** **(verify the comparison group)**. — [JAMA Netw Open 2020 (fullarticle/2764599)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2764599)
- In female VHA patients the 4-item HITS (cut-point 6) accurately detected past-year IPV against the CTS-2. — [PubMed 23824907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23824907/); [PMC3785669](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3785669)
- Lifetime physical or sexual IPV is 33.0% among women veterans against 23.8% among civilian women. — [JGIM, doi:10.1007/s11606-013-2534-x](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11606-013-2534-x.pdf)
- VHA also screens for IPV **use** (perpetration). — [Portnoy et al., PMC10576210](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10576210/)
- VA OMHSP states that IPV and suicidal ideation or behavior commonly co-occur. The IPV Assistance Program supports screening for both. — [VA FSTP: IPV (archived)](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20240510233729/https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/suicide_prevention/docs/FSTP-Intimate-Partner-Violence.pdf)

**Social isolation, financial strain and other SDOH (EHR-based)**
- Mitra A, Pradhan R, Melamed RD, et al. "Associations Between NLP-Enriched Social Determinants of Health and Suicide Death Among US Veterans." *JAMA Netw Open* 2023, doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.3079. This nested case-control study had **8,821 suicide decedents and 35,284 matched controls**. In fully adjusted models, NLP-extracted SDOH gave **legal problems aOR 2.62 (2.38–2.89), violence aOR 2.34 (2.17–2.52) and social problems aOR 1.94 (1.83–2.06)**. Models adjusted for sociodemographics, psychiatric symptoms, substance use, pain, disability, comorbidity and the other SDOH. The text also cites a maximum aOR of 3.44 for legal problems/violence, which conflicts with the table **(verify)**. — [PubMed 36920391](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36920391/); [PMC10018322](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10018322/); [arXiv 2212.05546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05546)
- Blosnich JR, et al. "Social Determinants and Military Veterans' Suicide Ideation and Attempt: a Cross-sectional Analysis of EHR Data." *J Gen Intern Med* 2020;35:1759-1767, doi:10.1007/s11606-019-05447-z. SDOH documented in the VHA EHR (violence, housing instability, employment, legal, social and family problems, and others) were **strongly associated with suicidal ideation and attempt even after adjusting for mental health diagnoses**. Factor-level ORs were not retrieved. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-019-05447-z); [U.S. Medicine](https://www.usmedicine.com/non-clinical-topics/technology/ehr/researcher-uses-va-emr-to-track-social-factors-linked-to-veteran-suicide/)
- In the National Health and Resilience in Veterans Study (NHRVS, 2019–2022, N=2,430), lower resilience, **lower social support** and lower purpose in life explained most of the variance in high chronic suicide risk. Only about **35.5% of veterans with current suicidal ideation were in mental health treatment** (2019–2020, n=4,069). — [J Clin Psychiatry 2025 PDF](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/suicide-specific-cognitions-US-military-veterans-24m03821.pdf); [J Clin Psychiatry NHRVS 2019–2020](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/mental/other-mental-disorders/prevalence-correlates-treatment-suicidal-behavior-us-military-veterans-results-2019-2020-national-health-resilience-veterans-study/)

### Inferences
- **Proposed EHR signals for referral to formal mental health screening (codes not verified in session unless marked):**
  - **MST:**
    - a positive VA MST clinical-reminder screen, recorded as a health factor;
    - MST-related treatment encounters;
    - T74.21X-/T76.21X- (adult sexual abuse, confirmed/suspected);
    - Z91.410 (personal history of adult physical and sexual abuse);
    - Z56.81 (sexual harassment on the job).
    Prioritize women: an MST-positive woman has a suicide risk approaching that of the average male VHA user (Kimerling 2016).
  - **Housing:**
    - a positive Homeless Screening Clinical Reminder (HSCR);
    - **Z59.0- homelessness**: Z59.00, Z59.01 sheltered, Z59.02 unsheltered (Z59.0/Z59.1 used in the VHA definition, verified via the [PLOS ONE paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0279973));
    - Z59.1- inadequate housing;
    - **Z59.81- housing instability, housed** (Z59.811 with risk of homelessness, Z59.812 homelessness in past 12 months);
    - homeless program clinic stop codes (HUD-VASH, GPD, HCHV, SSVF referrals);
    - HOMES records.
  - **Justice:**
    - Z65.0 (conviction without imprisonment); Z65.1 (imprisonment/incarceration); **Z65.2 (problems related to release from prison)**; Z65.3 (other legal circumstances);
    - Veterans Justice Outreach (VJO) / Health Care for Re-entry Veterans (HCRV) encounters;
    - NLP "legal problems" in notes, the strongest NLP SDOH at aOR 2.62.
  - **Transition:**
    - date of separation (DoD/VA Defense Enrollment Eligibility Reporting System (DEERS)/VA Profile) within the past 12 months, peak 6–12 months;
    - fewer than 4 years of service;
    - other-than-honorable discharge;
    - Army or Marine Corps;
    - **Z91.82** personal history of military deployment; **Z91.85** personal history of military service (both verified in session);
    - Z56.82 military deployment status;
    - Z60.0 problems of adjustment to life-cycle transitions.
  - **Firearms:**
    - no ICD-10-CM code exists for firearm access;
    - capture through the Comprehensive Suicide Risk Evaluation (CSRE), Safety Plan and lethal-means-counseling documentation (note titles/health factors), or NLP of notes ("gun", "firearm", "weapon at home");
    - prior firearm injury: W32–W34 accidental, X72–X74 intentional self-harm, Y22–Y24 undetermined;
    - treat any veteran with another risk factor plus documented access as needing lethal-means counseling.
  - **IPV:**
    - positive E-HITS or IPV-use screen;
    - Z69.11 (encounter for mental health services for victim of spousal/partner abuse); Z69.12 (perpetrator);
    - T74.11X-/T74.31X- (adult physical/psychological abuse, confirmed);
    - Y07.0- (spouse/partner as perpetrator);
    - Z63.0 (problems in relationship with spouse/partner).
  - **Social isolation:**
    - Z60.2 (problems related to living alone); Z60.4 (social exclusion and rejection);
    - Z63.4 (bereavement); Z63.5 (disruption of family by separation/divorce);
    - marital status change in demographics.
  - **Financial strain:**
    - Z59.86 (financial insecurity); Z59.87 (material hardship); Z59.5 (extreme poverty); Z59.6 (low income); Z59.41 (food insecurity);
    - Z56.0 (unemployment);
    - recent loss of VA disability compensation or an employment program referral.
- Because Mitra 2023 found NLP-extracted SDOH independently associated with suicide death, a screening rule that uses only structured Z-codes will under-identify at-risk veterans.

### Gaps
- No VA cohort HR for suicide death specific to **social isolation/loneliness** or **financial strain** was found. Mitra's "social problems" category is the closest proxy. The Elbogen financial-strain studies were not retrieved.
- The comparison group behind the IPV aOR of 3.80 is unconfirmed, and no VA cohort estimate of suicide **death** after a positive IPV screen was found. No study linking male veterans' IPV victimization to suicide was found.
- The original paper behind the housing instability aHRs of 1.75/1.80 was identified only through VA summary bulletins. Its authors and journal were not confirmed in session.
- Justice involvement shows inconsistent associations with suicide death once adjusted, as Pandit 2026 conflicts with the other studies. Most justice evidence covers ideation, attempts or all-cause mortality.

## Q5. Which social-determinant and risk data elements are reliably captured in VA EHRs (structured vs NLP of notes), and which VA screens and codes can be operationalized

### Takeaway
VA captures several risk factors through **national clinical reminders**: TBI (OEF/OIF), MST (universal), homelessness (HSCR, annual two-part), IPV (E-HITS for women), AUDIT-C, depression/PTSD/suicide screens, and the STORM and REACH VET predictive models. These give structured, reliable flags. Z-codes for SDOH are inconsistently used. In the VHA housing definition, ICD-10 codes alone identified only 41–62% of homeless veterans that a combination of stop codes, HSCR and HOMES records found. Eating disorders and personality disorders are heavily under-coded, and firearm access, social isolation, legal problems and financial strain largely sit in free-text notes. VA's NLP work (Mitra/Yu; Blosnich) shows that note-derived SDOH add independent predictive information for suicide.

### Cited Findings
- In the VHA operational definition of homelessness and housing instability, the data sources were ICD-10 Z59.0 (and Z59.1) codes, outpatient and inpatient clinic stop codes, a positive **part 2 of the two-part annual Homeless Screening Clinical Reminder (HSCR)** and HOMES records. **ICD-10 codes alone captured 41%–62% of homeless veterans** per VISN. Stop codes identified the most veterans: 153,484 first identified by stop codes, plus 33,951 added by ICD-10 codes. A 2025 correction removed stop codes 507, 522 and 530 from the homelessness row. — [PLOS ONE 2023, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0279973](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0279973); [PMC9803152](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9803152/); [Correction PMC12021128](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12021128/)
- The HSCR is one of VHA's 71 national clinical reminders. A 2025 VA OIG audit assessed whether facilities complete its follow-up procedures, which matters for how reliable it is as a flag. — [VA OIG report 25-00077-215 (Dec 2025)](https://www.vaoig.gov/sites/default/files/reports/2025-12/vaoig-25-00077-215-final.pdf)
- In Mitra 2023, structured SDOH were derived from ICD codes and VHA stop codes. An NLP system extracted SDOH from unstructured notes. **SDOH from both structured and NLP sources were independently associated with suicide death**, and NLP-extracted SDOH "measurably enhance" structured data. — [PMC10018322](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10018322/); [Companion JAMA Netw Open editorial/paper 2802475](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2802475)
- A follow-up applied NLP-extracted social and behavioral determinants of health (SBDH) to about **2.99 million veterans**. It extracted 12 SBDH from 9 note types, including social isolation, housing insecurity, unemployment, legal problems, violence, alcohol, smoking and illicit drugs. NLP-enriched SBDH improved post-discharge suicide prediction at 7, 30, 90 and 180 days. — [npj Mental Health Research 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00120-2); [PMC11846906](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11846906/); [PMC11092830](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11092830/)
- More recent VA NLP work extracts **life events** (for example relationship breakdown, legal and financial events) from notes to predict acute suicide risk. Another study combines SDOH with transformer models to predict suicide after psychiatric hospitalization. — [JAMIA 2026;33(4):812](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/33/4/812/8526275); [medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.03.25321612v1.full); [PMC12698767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12698767)
- NLP has also been used to identify eviction status in VA notes. — [arXiv 2212.02762](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.02762)
- Blosnich 2020 measured how often SDOH were documented in the VHA EHR and showed that they can be tracked with EHR data alone. — [JGIM 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-019-05447-z)

### Inferences
- **Reliability tiers for building a referral rule (synthesis):**
  1. **Most reliable (structured, nationally mandated in VA):**
     - TBI Clinical Reminder / CTBIE (OEF/OIF/OND only);
     - MST 2-item screen;
     - HSCR;
     - AUDIT-C;
     - E-HITS (women);
     - C-SSRS / CSRE (VA Risk ID);
     - pharmacy data: opioid MME, start/stop dates, benzodiazepines, buprenorphine, naloxone;
     - encounter data: ED and inpatient poisoning/injury codes, specialty program stop codes (homeless, justice, SUD, polytrauma);
     - predictive-model flags (STORM, REACH VET).
  2. **Moderately reliable:** ICD-10 diagnoses for SUD, PTSD, TBI and chronic pain.
  3. **Unreliable or under-coded:** Z-codes for SDOH (Z59/Z60/Z63/Z65); personality disorders (F60.x); eating disorders (F50.x); firearm access, which has no code; social isolation; financial strain; IPV in men. Use NLP or patient-reported SDOH screens for these.
- **Unverified LOINC codes to check before use:**
  - PHQ-9 item 9 (self-harm thoughts), believed to be 44260-8;
  - housing status (PRAPARE), believed to be 71802-3;
  - Hunger Vital Sign, believed to be 88124-3.
- For veterans who receive care outside VA (community care or non-VA EHRs), the VA-only screens (TBI reminder, MST, HSCR) will not exist. Z91.82/Z91.85, the separation date and SDOH Z-codes become the main portable signals.

### Gaps
- REACH VET model variables, its top-0.1% threshold and its evaluation results (for example McCarthy et al. 2021) were not retrieved or verified in this session.
- No LOINC codes were confirmed for DAST-10, E-HITS/HITS, the VA HSCR items, the VA MST screen items, the TBI Clinical Reminder or the NSI. These VA instruments are generally stored as **VistA health factors/reminder dialogs** rather than LOINC-coded observations, so check VA CDW documentation.
- Quantitative completeness of the VA MST, IPV and TBI reminders in recent years (post-2020) was not found. The 70% completion figure for MST dates from about 2003.
- The ICD-10-CM codes listed under Inferences that are not marked as verified (for example Z59.81-, Z59.86, Z59.87, Z65.x, Z60.x, Z69.1x, F50 FY2025 expansions, T43.65-) were not verified in this session. Check them against the current CDC/NCHS ICD-10-CM release.
