## Key Question 1: How was VA REACH VET built, how does it perform, what is the top-0.1% tier approach, what changed in REACH VET 2.0, and what are the critiques?

### Takeaway
REACH VET is a penalized logistic regression on structured VA Corporate Data Warehouse data (original proof-of-concept 381 candidate predictors → operational 61-predictor model) that flags roughly the top 0.1% of predicted risk monthly per facility for clinician care review and outreach; it concentrates risk (≈30-fold elevated suicide rate in the top 0.1%) but captures only ≈2% of all VA suicides, and published quasi-experimental evaluations show process gains and fewer documented suicide attempts but **no detectable reduction in suicide or all-cause mortality**. REACH VET 2.0 (Peluso et al., 2026; system-wide since June 2025) improved discrimination (C 0.76 vs 0.69), added military sexual trauma (MST) and intimate partner violence (IPV), and removed race/ethnicity as inputs after a 2024 investigation showed the original model favored white men.

### Cited Findings

**Original model development (McCarthy 2015; Kessler 2017) [VA]**

- Kessler RC, Hwang I, Hoffmire CA, McCarthy JF, et al. "Developing a practical suicide risk prediction model for targeting high-risk patients in the Veterans Health Administration." *Int J Methods Psychiatr Res* 2017;26(3):e1575. doi:10.1002/mpr.1575. - Operational rule: the model flags veterans above a predefined threshold — approximately the **top 0.1% of risk each month at each facility**; VHA policy requires each veteran in the top 0.1% tier to have their care **evaluated and be contacted by a VHA provider** (REACH VET coordinator + provider review, outreach, safety planning, care enhancement). The original model went live in **2017**. — [Psychiatric Services: "The Veterans Health Administration REACH VET Program: Suicide Predictive Modeling in Practice," doi:10.1176/appi.ps.202100629](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/full/10.1176/appi.ps.202100629); [Peluso et al. - Real-world PPV of the top tier (2025 legal-involvement analysis): among patients in the top 0.1%, an average of **324.8 per month attempted or died by suicide (PPV ≈ 0.052 for the combined attempt-or-death outcome)**; the original model contains **no predictors for criminal-legal involvement** despite high suicide rates in that group. — [npj Mental Health Research 2025, "Evaluating the accuracy of the VHA's REACH VET suicide prediction model for legal involved veterans" (PMC12535588)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12535588/); [npj Mental Health Research 2025, "Retraining the VHA's REACH VET suicide risk prediction model for patients involved in the legal system" (PMC12246187)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00143-9)

**Critiques [VA]**
- 2024 Fuller Project investigation: the original algorithm treated **being a white male (and divorced male)** as stronger indicators of self-harm risk than factors that primarily or fully affect women; **MST and IPV — both linked to elevated suicide risk among women veterans — were not included**. — [Fuller Project](https://fullerproject.org/story/artificial-intelligence-veteran-suicide-prevention-algorithm-favors-men/); [Yahoo syndication](https://www.yahoo.com/news/vas-veteran-suicide-prevention-algorithm-114319692.html)
- Low sensitivity by design: top 0.1% tier ≈2.2% sensitivity → >97% of suicides occur among non-flagged patients; REACH VET is therefore a complement to universal screening (VA Risk ID / C-SSRS), not a replacement. — [Kessler 2017 via citing paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00143-9)
- Missing risk domains: no criminal-legal involvement predictors in the original model. — [PMC12535588](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12535588/)
- No mortality effect in two evaluations (2021, 2025). — [McCarthy 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8524305/); [Dent 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2836124)
- Broader scoping review of ML for suicide in military/veteran populations (2025) for additional VA/DoD model inventory. — [arXiv 2505.12220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12220)
- NASEM workshop proceedings "Identifying and Managing Veteran Suicide Risk" (NAP 27195) discuss REACH VET in policy context. — [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/27195/chapter/5)

### Inferences
- The REACH VET design pattern is "**rank-and-review**," not "classify-and-treat": a fixed-capacity top-k (≈0.1%/facility/month) list sized to clinician bandwidth, paired with a low-harm, multi-component intervention. For a new VA model, threshold selection should be capacity-constrained and justified with decision-curve / net-benefit analysis at very low threshold probabilities.
- Because flagged patients receive intervention, **post-deployment outcomes in the flagged tier are treatment-contaminated** (performative); retraining REACH VET 2.0 on 2018 data (post-2017 deployment) implies the training labels were already partially affected by the intervention — a design consideration for any 3.0 retraining (consider propensity/inverse-probability weighting for intervention exposure or restricting to pre-deployment or non-flagged strata).
- The 2.0 update shows that removing race/ethnicity and adding gender-relevant risk factors (MST, IPV) can coexist with improved discrimination; however, removing protected attributes does not guarantee group calibration — subgroup audits remain necessary.
- Mortality null results with attempt reductions suggest the program may better serve attempters than decedents; models targeting **suicide death** specifically (e.g., firearm access proxies, older male veterans with low engagement) may need separate features/outcomes.

## Key Question 2: What do other leading suicide-risk EHR models report (MHRN/Kaiser, Vanderbilt VSAIL, Partners/Mass General Brigham, Army STARRS, Kessler), and what is the low-PPV problem?

### Takeaway
Structured-EHR suicide models consistently achieve AUCs of ~0.76–0.86 (higher at short horizons), but because suicide death is rare (base rates ~0.01–0.1%/year), PPVs for suicide death are almost always ≤1%; attempt/ideation outcomes yield more usable PPVs (e.g., 5.4% 90-day attempt risk in MHRN's top 5%; NNS 23 for ideation in VSAIL). Performance degrades across sites and is markedly worse for Black and American Indian/Alaska Native patients in at least one major model.

### Cited Findings

**Mental Health Research Network (MHRN; Kaiser Permanente + HealthPartners + Henry Ford, etc.) [CIVILIAN]**
- Simon GE, Johnson E, Lawrence JM, et al. "Predicting Suicide Attempts and Suicide Deaths Following Outpatient Visits Using Electronic Health Records." *Am J Psychiatry* 2018;175(10):951–960. doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17101167. Mental health specialty visits: **C-statistic 0.851 (95% CI 0.848–0.853) for 90-day suicide attempt; 0.861 (0.848–0.875) for suicide death**. Primary care visits: **0.853 (0.849–0.857) attempt; 0.833 (0.813–0.853) death**. Top 5% of risk scores (MH specialty) accounted for **43% of subsequent attempts and 48% of suicide deaths**; among those in the top 5%, **5.4% attempted and 0.26% died by suicide within 90 days**. Authors suggest predicted 90-day attempt risk >10% (≈99th percentile in MH specialty) should prompt safety planning and lethal-means counseling. — [Psychiatry Online](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17101167); [PDF](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/pdf/10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17101167)
- Simon 2018 strongest predictors: prior suicide attempt, MH and substance use diagnoses, medical diagnoses, psychiatric medications dispensed, inpatient/ED care, and routinely administered depression questionnaires (PHQ-9, esp. item 9); method was penalized (LASSO) logistic regression on ~20 million visits by ~3 million patients across 7 health systems. **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]** — [Psychiatry Online](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17101167)
- Critique response: Simon, Shortreed and Coley argued Belsher et al. were too pessimistic — some suicide-attempt models have PPVs equal to or exceeding widely accepted risk tools, and a 5% 90-day attempt risk can be identified accurately among MH outpatient visits. — [JAMA Psychiatry letter, "Positive Predictive Values and Potential Success of Suicide Prediction Models"](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/article-abstract/2737195)
- Methodological note from MHRN: visit-level evaluation inflates/biases metrics when patients with many visits contribute many observations ("informative cluster size"); person-level evaluation is recommended. — [ResearchGate: "Clinical risk prediction models and informative cluster size: Assessing the performance of a suicide risk prediction algorithm"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351843122_Clinical_risk_prediction_models_and_informative_cluster_size_Assessing_the_performance_of_a_suicide_risk_prediction_algorithm)

| REACH VET 2.0 [VA] | All VHA users | suicide death | C 0.76 | Top 0.1%: 5.6 vs 3.6 deaths/monthly set |
| MHRN (Simon 2018) [CIV] | MH specialty visits | 90-day attempt / death | 0.851 / 0.861 | Top 5%: 43%/48% captured; 5.4% / 0.26% event rate |
| VSAIL (Walsh 2021) [CIV] | Inpt/ED/amb. surgery | ideation / attempt | — | Top quantile NNS 23 / 271 |
| Barak-Corren 2020 [CIV] | 5 systems, 3.7M pts | first attempt | — | 38% sensitivity @ 90% specificity; PPV ≈3.6% |
| Army STARRS 2015 [DoD] | Post-psych discharge | 12-mo suicide | — | Top 5%: 52.9% of suicides; 3,824/100k PY |

### Inferences
- For suicide **death**, any population-level model will have PPV ≲1–5% even in top 0.1%; operational value must come from cheap, low-harm, broadly beneficial interventions (caring contacts, safety planning, lethal-means counseling, care coordination) and capacity-sized top-k lists — not from binary "high-risk" labels.
- Ideation/attempt outcomes (more common, more modifiable) give more actionable PPV; consider a composite or multi-task outcome (ideation, attempt, death, overdose, external-cause death) as REACH VET's tier also has elevated non-suicide mortality.
- Short-horizon (7–30 day) models are better discriminators (VSAIL) and align with acute-care triggers; long-horizon models (12 mo) align with population outreach.
- Site/setting variation (VSAIL, Barak-Corren) argues for local recalibration and mandatory pre-deployment silent-mode validation.
- Visit-level vs person-level evaluation and informative cluster size must be addressed in any VA re-analysis.

### Gaps
- Exact per-site AUCs in Barak-Corren 2020 and Coley 2021 subgroup sensitivities could not be verified.
- Kaiser Permanente Northern California deployment results (e.g., suicide-attempt models embedded in KPNC workflows) were not retrieved.
- Walsh 2017 AUC range, Barak-Corren 2017 sensitivity, Ross 2021 thresholds are from prior knowledge and need full-text confirmation.

## Key Question 3: What computable phenotypes or ML models exist for identifying undiagnosed adult ADHD, autism, PTSD, bipolar disorder, depression, or TBI from EHR/claims data, and how do they perform?

### Takeaway
Two distinct tasks are conflated in the literature: (1) **phenotyping** already-diagnosed cases with high PPV (eMERGE/PheKB, MVP algorithms — PPV often 0.80–0.98) and (2) **predicting future/undiagnosed** cases (prediction AUCs ~0.75–0.80 for adult ADHD; sensitivity usually <75%). Validated VA algorithms exist for PTSD (MVP Lasso, AUC 0.95) and TBI (TBI-PheCAP, AUC 0.92); adult autism prediction from EHR is essentially an evidence gap.

### Cited Findings

**Adult ADHD [CIVILIAN; Sweden]**
- Hamed et al. "Early detection of adults ADHD using electronic health records: A machine learning study." *European Psychiatry* (2025/2026). Swedish regional EHR; **2,973 adults with ADHD vs 4,447 adults referred to psychologists without ADHD**; only pre-index data used. **6-month window: AUC 0.79 (95% CI 0.76–0.81), F1 0.79, sensitivity 0.80; 12-month AUC 0.76 (0.73–0.79); 18-month AUC 0.75 (0.71–0.78)**. Predictive signals included substance use and frequent healthcare visits. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/early-detection-of-adults-adhd-using-electronic-health-records-a-machine-learning-study/C84C0CA747621B55238E6ED384120176); [PMC13122517](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13122517); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/a-new-ai-tool-spots-hidden-signs-of-adult-adhd-months-before-a-formal-diagnosis/)
- Review: "Predicting neurodevelopmental disorders using machine learning models and electronic health records" (*J Neurodev Disord* 2024) — **sensitivity <75% in most studies while AUC >75%**; better models used deep neural networks or tree-based ML with subject-level representations of past medical history. — [Springer, s11689-024-09579-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s11689-024-09579-0)
- Stacked ML for identifying non-adult ADHD using administrative population registers in a universal healthcare system. — [PMC10933630](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10933630/)

**Autism [CIVILIAN; mostly pediatric]**
- Lingren T, Chen P, Bochenek J, Doshi-Velez F, Manning-Courtney P, Bickel J, et al. "Electronic Health Record Based Algorithm to Identify Patients with Autism Spectrum Disorder." *PLoS ONE* 2016;11(7):e0159621. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0159621. eMERGE; ICD-9 codes alone "notoriously unreliable." **Rule-based PPV 0.885 (Boston Children's), 0.840 (Cincinnati Children's)**; ML version 0.780/0.799; external test PPV fell to **0.66** (development 0.894, overtraining); independent CHOP validation **PPV 0.848**. Algorithm + pseudocode on PheKB. — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0159621); [Harvard PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/finale/files/electronic_health_record_based_algorithm_to_identify_patients_with_autism_spectrum_disorder.pdf)
- Autism prediction from well-baby electronic data (pediatric screening use case). — [PMC11049145](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11049145/)
- VA CIPHER (Centralized Interactive Phenomics Resource) knowledgebase indexes VA phenotype definitions searchable by condition (e.g., autism). — [VA CIPHER](https://phenomics.va.ornl.gov/web/cipher/search?search=autism)

**PTSD [VA]**
- Harrington KM, Quaden R, Stein MB, et al. "Validation of an Electronic Medical Record–Based Algorithm for Identifying Posttraumatic Stress Disorder in U.S. Veterans." *J Trauma Stress* 2019;32(2):226–237. MVP; chart review of 500 records as gold standard; **Lasso probability score, cut-point 0.7**. Lasso vs ICD rule: agreement 80% vs 75%, **sensitivity 0.95 vs 0.84, AUC 0.95 vs 0.90**; final algorithm sensitivity 0.99, specificity 0.99, **PPV 0.95**, NPV 1.00 (possible-PTSD and likely-not grouped). Used in MVP GWAS (e.g., 11,920 algorithm-defined cases in African-ancestry sample). — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Validation-of-an-Electronic-Medical-Record-Based-in-Harrington-Quaden/4641923a6ab5ae437c94f10e7742f4a4491ae89e); [DeepDyve](https://www.deepdyve.com/lp/wiley/validation-of-an-electronic-medical-record-based-algorithm-for-x6orS0YtyP); [bioRxiv MVP PTSD GWAS](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/764001.full.pdf)
- Moshier et al. 2022 evaluated ICD diagnostic codes for identifying veterans with PTSD. — [J Trauma Stress, doi:10.1002/jts.22844](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.22844)
- "Misleading Results in PTSD Predictive Models Using EHR Data: Algorithm Validation Study" (*JMIR* 2024): EHR PTSD models had AUC 0.85–0.90, but **VA visit frequency drove results** (healthcare-utilization leakage) — a key warning for undiagnosed-condition prediction. — [JMIR doi:10.2196/63352](https://doi.org/10.2196/63352)
- Canadian primary-care EMR PTSD classification (Zafari et al. 2021). — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/14604582211053259)

**Bipolar disorder [CIVILIAN; Partners Healthcare]**
- Castro VM, Minnier J, Murphy SN, et al. "Validation of Electronic Health Record Phenotyping of Bipolar Disorder Cases and Controls." *Am J Psychiatry* 2015;172(4):363–372. Against SCID-IV interviews: **coded-broad algorithm PPV 0.80**; **NLP algorithm (trained to 95% specificity) PPV 0.85**. Source: Partners system >4.6 million patients over 20 years. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274401943_Validation_of_Electronic_Health_Record_Phenotyping_of_Bipolar_Disorder_Cases_and_Controls); [Smoller commentary, Am J Psychiatry](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2015.15010043)
- Chen CY, et al. "Genetic validation of bipolar disorder identified by automated phenotyping using electronic health records." *Transl Psychiatry* 2018;8:86 — 3 of 4 case definitions PPV up to **0.86**; control algorithm PPV **1.0**; NLP-combined algorithm gave strongest SNP-heritability signal. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-018-0133-7); [PMC5904248](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5904248/)

**TBI [VA]**
- "Development and validation of an electronic health record-based algorithm for identifying TBI in the VA: A VA Million Veteran Program study." *Brain Injury* 2024;38(13). doi:10.1080/02699052.2024.2373920. 200 chart-reviewed gold-standard labels; semi-supervised **TBI-PheCAP: AUC 0.92; sensitivity 84%, PPV 98% at specificity 90%**; equal or better than rules-based TBI algorithms and MVP survey self-report. — [Brain Injury](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699052.2024.2373920)
- MVP comparison of TBI data sources vs ICD reference: sensitivity ranged **0.23 (MVP baseline survey TBI item) to 0.77 (VA Comprehensive TBI Evaluation, CTBIE)**; CTBIE judged the best sensitivity/specificity balance. — [PMC13477427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13477427/)
- Civilian acute-TBI retrospective EHR protocol (κ = 0.74 after reweighting); authors note adaptation needed for VHA. — [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ned/advance-article/doi/10.1159/ned/adwag047/9116367)

**Phenotyping methods relevant to under-diagnosed targets**
- PheCAP (semi-supervised high-throughput phenotyping with chart-review training sets) — Zhang Y, et al. *Nat Protoc* 2019;14:3426–3444. doi:10.1038/s41596-019-0227-6. **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]** — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41596-019-0227-6)
- PheNorm (unsupervised, normalizes surrogate counts such as ICD/NLP mention counts by healthcare utilization) — Yu S, et al. *JAMIA* 2018;25(1):54–60. doi:10.1093/jamia/ocx111. **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]** — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocx111)
- ssROC: semi-supervised ROC analysis for evaluating phenotyping algorithms with limited gold-standard labels. — [arXiv 2305.01709](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.01709)

### Inferences
- For "who should be referred for screening," the target is a **pre-diagnosis prediction** problem, not a phenotyping problem; use phenotype algorithms (PTSD-MVP, TBI-PheCAP, bipolar NLP) to construct **labels and exclusion criteria**, then train prediction models on data strictly before first diagnosis (index-date design as in Hamed et al.).
- Utilization confounding (JMIR PTSD study) is the central threat: diagnosis requires contact; normalize for visit counts (PheNorm-style), use time-matched controls with similar utilization (Hamed used psychologist-referred controls), and report performance stratified by utilization decile.
- For veterans, adult ADHD/autism are likely heavily under-coded; outcome labels from ICD codes alone will have low sensitivity, so expect PU-learning settings and use chart-review or structured screening (ASRS-v1.1 for ADHD; AQ/RAADS for autism) on a sampled subset as a gold standard.
- TBI and PTSD have strong VA-specific structured sources (CTBIE, PCL-5, PC-PTSD-5 screens) that can serve as silver labels; depression has PHQ-2/PHQ-9 in VA clinical reminders.

### Gaps
- No published EHR model for **undiagnosed adult autism** (or adult autism in veterans) was found.
- No VA-specific adult ADHD prediction/phenotype study was found; PheKB ADHD phenotype details (e.g., CHOP eMERGE algorithm PPV) were not retrieved.
- Depression and bipolar *prediction-of-undiagnosed* models in VA, and validated VA depression phenotypes, were not searched due to tool budget.
- Whether ADHD/autism models perform comparably in veterans (older, male-predominant, high PTSD/TBI comorbidity that mimics ADHD symptoms) is unknown.

## Key Question 4: Which data standards and common data models are used, and which feature categories matter?

### Takeaway
VA models run on the Corporate Data Warehouse (CDW) via VINCI (with an OMOP-transformed instance available), research cohorts like MVP add genomics and surveys, and civilian multisite work uses OMOP (OHDSI) or PCORnet CDMs; FHIR R4/US Core and Bulk FHIR are the deployment-time interoperability layer. Predictive signal in published suicide models comes mainly from prior self-harm, MH/SUD diagnoses, psychiatric medications, acute-care utilization, and screening instruments (PHQ-9 item 9), with clinical-note NLP and social determinants (legal involvement, MST, IPV) as the main recent additions.

### Cited Findings
- REACH VET uses VA electronic health record data (CDW) and was developed/enhanced in collaboration with Oak Ridge National Laboratory; RV 2.0 was trained on 2018 VHA data. — [Peluso et al., OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/3375750-predictive-analytics-direct-clinical-attention-complex-patients-elevated-suicide-risk-enhancement-veterans-health-administration-reach-vet-model)
- VA Informatics and Computing Infrastructure (VINCI) provides researcher access to CDW data (including an OMOP CDM instance) in a secure enclave. **[prior knowledge, documentation not fetched]** — [VA HSR&D VINCI](https://www.hsrd.research.va.gov/for_researchers/vinci/)
- Million Veteran Program (MVP) links EHR, genomics, and surveys; it hosts validated EHR phenotypes for PTSD and TBI. — [Harrington 2019](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Validation-of-an-Electronic-Medical-Record-Based-in-Harrington-Quaden/4641923a6ab5ae437c94f10e7742f4a4491ae89e); [TBI-PheCAP 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699052.2024.2373920); [MVP site](https://www.research.va.gov/mvp/)
- VA CIPHER is VA's phenotype library (definitions searchable by condition). — [VA CIPHER](https://phenomics.va.ornl.gov/web/cipher/search?search=autism)
- OMOP CDM (OHDSI; current v5.4) standardizes condition, drug, procedure, measurement, observation, visit, note, and NLP tables onto standard vocabularies (SNOMED CT, RxNorm, LOINC). **[prior knowledge, documentation not fetched]** — [OHDSI CDM docs](https://ohdsi.github.io/CommonDataModel/); [Book of OHDSI](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/)
- PCORnet CDM is used by PCORnet clinical research networks for distributed queries. **[prior knowledge, documentation not fetched]** — [PCORnet data](https://pcornet.org/data/)
- FHIR R4 US Core profiles (aligned with USCDI, which includes SDOH assessment/goal/problem elements in recent versions) and SMART/HL7 Bulk Data ($export, NDJSON) are the standard mechanisms for population-level extraction and real-time scoring. **[prior knowledge, documentation not fetched]** — [US Core IG](https://hl7.org/fhir/us/core/); [Bulk Data IG](https://hl7.org/fhir/uv/bulkdata/); [USCDI](https://www.healthit.gov/isa/united-states-core-data-interoperability-uscdi)
- Barak-Corren 2020's portable approach used only structured diagnoses, labs, procedure codes and medications across 5 heterogeneous systems — demonstrating that a CDM-level feature set transfers. — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2763237)
- MHRN predictors: prior suicide attempt, MH/SUD diagnoses, medical diagnoses, psychiatric medications dispensed, inpatient/ED utilization, PHQ-9 responses. **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]** — [Simon 2018](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17101167)
- Missing VA domains identified post hoc: criminal-legal involvement (not in original REACH VET), MST and IPV (added in 2.0). — [PMC12535588](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12535588/); [Nextgov 2024](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2024/10/va-updating-its-ai-suicide-risk-model-reach-more-women/400377/)
- Health information exchange data improve cross-organization capture for suicide-risk identification. — [PMC12038995](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038995)
- Clinical notes: VA studies use NLP of progress notes for suicidal thoughts history and "life events" (e.g., legal, financial, relationship stressors) to improve acute veteran suicide prediction; a 2026 VA study integrated discrete NLP models into personalized suicide risk prediction. — [medRxiv 2025, Life Events Extraction for Veteran Acute Suicide Prediction](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.03.25321612.full.pdf); [Protocol: theory-guided NLP of VA progress notes](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362923041_Suicide_theory-guided_natural_language_processing_of_clinical_progress_notes_to_improve_prediction_of_veteran_suicide_risk_protocol_for_a_mixed-method_study); [PubMed 41862442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41862442/)
- Utilization features can dominate (and confound) MH condition models. — [JMIR 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/63352)

**Recommended feature taxonomy (synthesized from the above sources)**

| Category | Examples (VA source) | Suicide model | Undiagnosed-condition model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demographics / military | age, sex, marital status, service era, service-connected disability %, MST screen, combat exposure | Core | Core (ADHD/autism age-sex patterns) |
| Diagnoses (ICD-10-CM → SNOMED) | prior self-harm (X71–X83, T14.91, R45.851), MH/SUD, chronic pain, TBI, sleep | Strongest | Comorbidity patterns (SUD, anxiety, depression, sleep) |
| Medications (RxNorm) | opioids, benzodiazepines, antidepressants, stimulants, atomoxetine, antipsychotics, lithium | Core | Off-label stimulant use, failed antidepressant trials |
| Utilization | ED, inpatient MH, missed appointments, no-shows, care fragmentation | Core | Strong but confounding (normalize) |
| Screening instruments | PHQ-9 item 9, C-SSRS, PC-PTSD-5, PCL-5, AUDIT-C, CTBIE | Strong | Silver labels / features |
| Labs / vitals | BAC, tox screens, weight change | Modest | Modest |
| Notes (NLP/LLM) | ideation mentions, life events, attention/organization complaints | Incremental | Potentially high (symptom language) |
| SDOH | homelessness flags, legal involvement (VJO/HCRV), IPV, financial strain | Incremental, equity-relevant | Incremental |

### Inferences
- Build the VA pipeline in CDW/VINCI for training (richest), but express features in OMOP concepts so the model can be externally validated in OHDSI networks and civilian community-care data; plan FHIR US Core/Bulk export to score community-care (MISSION Act) episodes.
- Encode time explicitly (look-back windows: 0–30, 31–180, 181–730 days; recency/counts/trajectories) — short-horizon signals drive VSAIL-style performance.
- For undiagnosed ADHD/autism, model features should exclude diagnostic-workup artefacts (referral orders, neuropsych testing CPT codes) that leak the label.

### Gaps
- OMOP/PCORnet/FHIR documentation pages could not be fetched; current version numbers (e.g., PCORnet CDM v7.0, US Core 7/8) should be confirmed.
- No source quantified the incremental AUC of SDOH or NLP features over structured data in VA suicide models in this session.

## Key Question 5: What are the methodological best practices (reporting, validation, label noise, imbalance, calibration, net benefit, thresholds, explainability, fairness, drift, performative feedback)?

### Takeaway
Use TRIPOD+AI for reporting and PROBAST+AI for risk-of-bias self-audit; validate temporally and externally with person-level metrics, calibration, and decision curves at capacity-relevant thresholds; treat under-diagnosed targets as positive-unlabeled problems with chart-review gold standards; audit subgroup discrimination *and* calibration (race, sex, age, rurality); and plan for drift and performative effects (intervention changes outcomes) via silent-mode validation, randomized/stepped rollouts, and intervention-aware retraining.

### Inferences
- **Recommended validation stack**: (1) internal temporal split (train years T, test T+1 monthly); (2) external validation in held-out VISNs and in civilian OMOP data; (3) silent prospective run ≥6–12 months; (4) stepped-wedge or cluster-randomized rollout (as Walsh did for CDS) to estimate causal impact; (5) person-level AUC, AUPRC, sensitivity/PPV at top 0.1/1/5%, calibration slope/intercept/ICI, decision curves at threshold probabilities matching intervention cost, all stratified by race, ethnicity, sex, age band, rurality, and utilization decile.
- **Under-diagnosed targets**: estimate class prior via chart-review sample of "unlabeled" patients; use PU-learning or noise-robust losses; evaluate against a prospectively screened gold standard (e.g., ASRS-v1.1 plus clinician interview), not ICD codes; report how much performance depends on utilization features.
- **Imbalance**: prefer cost-sensitive loss or no resampling plus post-hoc recalibration; resampling (SMOTE/undersampling) distorts calibration and must be followed by recalibration — standard practice consistent with Van Calster 2019 (calibration emphasis).
- **Explainability**: SHAP (global and per-patient) for clinician review lists; beware that SHAP on correlated EHR features is attribution, not causation; per-patient reason codes help coordinators prioritize outreach (REACH VET dashboard concept).
- **Performative loop**: log intervention exposure; retrain with intervention-adjusted labels (e.g., treat flagged-and-treated periods with IPW or exclude), keep a randomized holdout or use rollout variation to estimate counterfactual risk.

## Key Question 6: How are large language models being used with EHR data for phenotyping and risk prediction (2023–2026), and what are the risks?

### Takeaway
LLMs are useful for (a) extracting suicidality and life-event concepts from notes (encoder models fine-tuned on annotated notes reach F1 ≈0.8; locally hosted generative LLMs are being tested for privacy-preserving classification) and (b) drafting computable-phenotype logic, but evidence shows inconsistent accuracy (e.g., ~50% accuracy generating computable phenotypes from standardized vocabularies; criteria too broad/narrow), so outputs require expert review and validation against chart-review gold standards. No published, prospectively validated LLM-based suicide risk prediction model in VA was found.

### Cited Findings
- Fine-tuned encoder baseline: on 500 annotated psychiatric evaluation notes from a safety-net hospital, **RoBERTa multi-label F1 0.81** (multiple single-label 0.78) for suicidal ideation/attempt/exposure/NSSI phenotypes. — [arXiv 2409.18878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18878)
- Reasoning LLMs applied to the same 500-note task with explanations (2025 preprint). — [medRxiv 2025.03.27.25324783](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.27.25324783.full.pdf)
- Privacy-preserving, locally hosted Llama-2 classified suicidality in German psychiatric admission notes against psychiatrist labels (3 models × 5 prompt variants). — [PMC11669470](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11669470/); [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.06.24303763.full.pdf)
- Deep learning detection and prediction of suicidal behavior from clinical notes (*PLoS ONE* 2025). — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0331459)
- VA: life-events extraction from notes for acute veteran suicide prediction (2025 preprint); integration of discrete NLP models into personalized VA suicide risk prediction (2026). — [medRxiv 2025.02.03.25321612](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.03.25321612.full.pdf); [PubMed 41862442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41862442/)
- Dataset construction choices (how suicidality labels are built) strongly shape detection performance in clinical text (2026 preprint) — a label-validity warning for LLM evaluation. — [arXiv 2606.19637](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19637)
- LLM-generated phenotype algorithms: GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 outperformed Claude 2 and Bard on instruction following and SQL executability; LLMs identified relevant concepts but organized criteria poorly → algorithms **too restrictive or too broad**. *JAMIA* 2024;31(9):1994. doi:10.1093/jamia/ocae072. — [JAMIA](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/31/9/1994/7645319); [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.19.23300230v2.full)
- SHREC framework (2025): GPT-4 averaged **~50% accuracy** generating computable phenotypes from standardized vocabularies vs original definitions; substantial human oversight needed. — [PMC12904566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12904566/)
- GPT-4 matched or beat curated spaCy rules for Crohn's sub-phenotyping (**precision 0.87, recall 1.00** on one task). — [Communications Medicine 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-025-01337-0)
- GPT-4 accuracy **0.85** vs hybrid NLP **0.87** for high-throughput phenotyping of neurology physician notes. — [arXiv 2403.05920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.05920)
- Retrieval-augmented normalization improves LLM phenotype-concept mapping accuracy. — [PMC11913805](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11913805/)
- LLM-generated synthetic EHR text used to improve SDOH (eviction) detection (2025 preprint). — [arXiv 2507.07421](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07421)

### Inferences
- Best near-term role for LLMs in a VA pipeline: **note-level feature extraction** (suicidal ideation, firearm access, IPV, MST disclosure, legal/financial stressors, ADHD/autism symptom language) run inside VA's secure enclave with locally hosted or FedRAMP-authorized models, feeding a conventional calibrated risk model — rather than end-to-end LLM risk scores.
- LLM extraction must be validated like any phenotype: annotated gold-standard sample, per-concept precision/recall, subgroup error analysis (dialect, gender), and drift monitoring as note templates change.
- Risks: hallucinated evidence spans, prompt sensitivity, non-determinism across model versions (versioning/pinning needed), demographic bias in language interpretation, leakage of diagnostic text (e.g., "r/o ADHD") into "undiagnosed" prediction, and PHI exposure if external APIs are used.
- LLM-drafted phenotype definitions should be treated as first drafts for clinician-informatician review and tested against chart review (per JAMIA 2024 and SHREC findings).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed veteran-specific LLM suicidality-extraction study with reported F1 was found; Llama-2 German study F1 values not extracted.
- No published LLM or EHR foundation-model (e.g., transformer over coded sequences) study for **adult ADHD or autism** detection was found in this session.
- Head-to-head comparisons of LLM-extracted note features vs structured-only models for suicide risk (incremental AUC/PPV) were not found.
