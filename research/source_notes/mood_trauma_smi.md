## Q1. Veteran-specific effect sizes linking each condition to suicide death, attempts and NSSI

### Takeaway
In the largest VA national cohort (Ilgen et al. 2010, Arch Gen Psychiatry; about 3.3M VHA patients from FY1998–99, followed 7 years, 7,684 suicides), every psychiatric diagnosis examined raised suicide-death risk. Bipolar disorder carried the highest risk in men (HR 2.98), and substance use disorder (HR ≈6.6) then bipolar disorder (HR ≈6.3) were highest in women. Depression ranked second in men. PTSD and "other anxiety" carried the smallest diagnosis-specific HRs, and later VA work suggests PTSD alone, without comorbid SUD or depression, is a weaker independent predictor. Insomnia and nightmares are strong, independent correlates of suicide attempts in VHA data (OR 5.6 and 2.5 in a case-control study). For OSA the association is smaller but significant (OR 1.4; HR 1.2).

### Cited Findings

**Overall psychiatric diagnosis (VA national cohort)**
- Ilgen MA, Bohnert ASB, Ignacio RV, McCarthy JF, Valenstein MM, Kim HM, Blow FC. "Psychiatric diagnoses and risk of suicide in veterans." *Arch Gen Psychiatry* 2010;67(11):1152–1158. doi:10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2010.129. Design: retrospective cohort of all VHA patients in FY1998–1999, with psychiatric diagnoses from treatment records predicting National Death Index suicide over the next 7 years. 7,684 veterans died by suicide in follow-up. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919); [PubMed 21041616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21041616/)
- Any psychiatric diagnosis vs none: HR 2.50 in men and HR 5.18 in women. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919); a VA HSR&D brief describes this as about 160% higher overall risk and more than fivefold higher risk for women — [VA HSR&D Pub Brief](https://hsrd.research.va.gov/research/citations/PubBriefs/articles.cfm?RecordID=296)
- 46.8% of veterans who died by suicide had at least one psychiatric diagnosis recorded in VHA. — [ScienceDaily / EurekAlert release, Nov 2010](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/11/101101161825.htm)
- Rank order of diagnosis-specific risk:
  - **Men:** bipolar > depression > SUD > schizophrenia > other anxiety > PTSD.
  - **Women:** SUD > bipolar > schizophrenia > depression > PTSD > other anxiety.
  - CIs were wider for women because fewer women use VHA. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/731870); [Psychiatric News 2011](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/full/10.1176/pn.46.2.psychnews_46_2_002)
- Men with bipolar disorder: **HR 2.98 (95% CI 2.73–3.25)**. Women with SUD: **HR 6.62 (95% CI 4.72–9.29)**. Women with bipolar disorder: **HR 6.33 (95% CI 4.69–8.54)**. — [ResearchGate copy of abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47644897_Psychiatric_Diagnoses_and_Risk_of_Suicide_in_Veterans); [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919). These figures come from search extracts of abstract copies; the full-text table was not accessible, so confirm them against Table 2 of the paper.

**PTSD**
- Ilgen 2010: PTSD had the **lowest** diagnosis-specific HR among men and was 5th of 6 among women. In a depression cohort, comorbid PTSD was associated with *lower* suicide risk. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)
- VHA patients with PTSD: women with PTSD had lower suicide-death risk than men with PTSD, and comorbid SUD significantly raised suicide-death risk among veterans with PTSD. — ["Gender Differences in Suicide and Self-Directed Violence Risk," *Women's Health Issues* (2018/2019)](https://www.whijournal.com/article/S1049-3867(18)30489-4/pdf)
- In a VHA study of suicide risk evaluations (CSRE) and subsequent suicide, PTSD was associated with lower clinician-rated chronic suicide risk. — ["Suicide Risk Evaluations and Suicide in the Veterans Health Administration," *JAMA Netw Open* 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830585)
- Conner, Ilgen and colleagues analyzed two-way diagnostic combinations (for example PTSD + depression) in a VHA cohort, which shows that risk depends on comorbidity. — [PMC8372244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8372244/). Specific HRs could not be extracted; see Gaps.
- The National Center for PTSD has published a research review of the evidence: *PTSD Research Quarterly* V28N4, "PTSD and Death from Suicide." — [ptsd.va.gov](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/publications/rq_docs/V28N4.pdf)

**Major depressive disorder**
- Ilgen 2010: depression was 2nd-highest diagnosis-specific risk in men, below bipolar disorder and above every other diagnosis, and 4th in women. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)
- In VA inpatients, comorbid depression and alcohol use disorder predicted suicide attempt in the year after hospitalization. — [*J Affect Disord* 2016, S0165032715302147](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032715302147)

**PHQ-9 item 9 (suicidal/self-harm thoughts) as a risk marker**
- VA data (Louzon SA, Bossarte R, McCarthy JF, Katz IR. "Does suicidal ideation as measured by the PHQ-9 predict suicide among VA patients?" *Psychiatr Serv* 2016;67(5):517–522. doi:10.1176/appi.ps.201500149):
  - Sample: 447,245 VHA PHQ-9 administrations, Oct 2009–Sep 2010, with NDI suicide follow-up through Sep 2011.
  - Any item 9 endorsement: **HR 1.92 (95% CI 1.53–2.41)**.
  - "Several days": **HR 1.75 (1.24–2.46)**.
  - The association persisted after controlling for overall depression severity.
  - — [Psychiatric Services](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201500149)
- **Civilian** data (Simon GE, Rutter CM, Peterson D, et al. "Does response on the PHQ-9 depression questionnaire predict subsequent suicide attempt or suicide death?" *Psychiatr Serv* 2013;64(12):1195–1202. doi:10.1176/appi.ps.201200587; Group Health outpatients):
  - One-year probability of attempt or suicide death rose from about **0.4%** for "not at all" to about **4% (1 in 25)** for "nearly every day."
  - Item 9 remained a strong predictor after adjustment.
  - **39% of attempts and 36% of suicide deaths within 30 days occurred in people answering "not at all."**
  - — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256610695_Does_Response_on_the_PHQ-9_Depression_Questionnaire_Predict_Subsequent_Suicide_Attempt_or_Suicide_Death); [Psych News Alert, Sep 2013](https://alert.psychnews.org/2013/09/depression-questionnaire-item-helps.html)
- **Civilian** replication: in more than 1.2 million PHQs, item 9 strongly predicted attempt and suicide death over 2 years. — [Simon et al., *J Clin Psychiatry* 2016, PubMed 26930521](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26930521/)
- Military/VA screening research: more than 95% of patients who screen positive on the PHQ-9 suicide item do not go on to attempt or die by suicide. Adding Suicide Cognitions Scale items improved identification. — [CDMRP research highlight, Bryan 2021](https://cdmrp.health.mil/dmrdp/research_highlights/21bryan_highlight)

**Bipolar disorder**
- Ilgen 2010: highest risk diagnosis in men (HR 2.98, 2.73–3.25) and 2nd-highest in women (HR ≈6.33). — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)
- Among VHA patients with bipolar disorder, benzodiazepine receipt was associated with increased suicide risk. — ["Suicide Among Veterans Health Administration Patients With Bipolar Disorder: Evidence of Increased Risk With Benzodiazepine Receipt," *J Clin Psychiatry* 2025 (24m15424)](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/suicide-veterans-health-administration-patients-bipolar-disorder-evidence-increased-risk-benzodiazepine-Receipt-24m15424.pdf). Only the title was confirmed; the effect size was not extracted.

**Anxiety disorders (GAD, panic, "other anxiety")**
- Ilgen 2010: "other anxiety disorders" ranked 5th of 6 in men and 6th of 6 in women. Elevated risk was still significant, since all diagnoses examined were associated with elevated suicide risk. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919); [PubMed search extract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21041616/)

**Schizophrenia / psychotic disorders**
- Ilgen 2010: schizophrenia ranked 4th in men and 3rd in women. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)
- Sleep disorders are prevalent and under-recorded in VA health-record data for veterans with SMI. — ["Sleep disorders in veterans with serious mental illnesses: prevalence in VA health record data," PMC10476039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476039)

**Sleep disorders (insomnia, nightmares, OSA/sleep-related breathing disorders)**
- VHA case-control study of suicide attempt (Bishop TM, Walsh PG, Ashrafioun L, Lavigne JE, Pigeon WR; *Sleep Medicine* 2020; PubMed 31727433). Adjusted ORs for suicide attempt:
  - Insomnia **OR 5.62 (95% CI 5.39–5.86)**
  - Nightmares **OR 2.49 (2.23–2.77)**
  - Sleep-related breathing disorders **OR 1.37 (1.27–1.48)**
  - — [PubMed 31727433](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31727433/)
- Veterans discharged from VA residential mental health treatment (Bishop et al. 2023, *Frontiers in Sleep*, doi:10.3389/frsle.2023.1173650; VA Center of Excellence for Suicide Prevention, Canandaigua):
  - Any sleep disturbance (n=1,211) predicted shorter time to suicide attempt: **HR 1.16 (1.02–1.32)**.
  - In a model with age, insomnia, nightmare disorder and alcohol dependence, sleep-related breathing disorders: **HR 1.19 (1.01–1.38)**.
  - Insomnia and nightmare disorder were not independently significant in that model.
  - — [Frontiers in Sleep](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsle.2023.1173650/full)
- Mid-to-late-life veterans, propensity-matched cohort of about 2 million (Byers et al., conference abstract, not peer-reviewed full paper): any sleep disorder diagnosis (including apnea and insomnia) **HR 1.23 (1.20–1.27)** for any suicide attempt after full adjustment, including psychiatric disorders. The association was stronger among those with psychiatric disorders. — [PMC10736809](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10736809/)

**Non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI)**
- I found no veteran-specific HR/OR linking any of these diagnoses to NSSI (as distinct from suicide attempt) in this session. See Gaps.

### Inferences
- For a referral or flagging tool, the hierarchy implied by VA data for suicide **death** is roughly: bipolar ≥ MDD > SUD > schizophrenia > anxiety ≈ PTSD (men), with SUD and bipolar dominant in women. Comorbidity (especially +SUD, +sleep disorder, +pain/opioids) matters more than any single label.
- PTSD's modest *independent* HR does not mean PTSD is low-risk. Its risk is likely mediated through comorbid depression, SUD, insomnia/nightmares and firearm access, so PTSD should still trigger suicide screening.
- Insomnia's very large OR for **attempts** (5.6) is much larger than its HR for time-to-attempt in a residential cohort (1.16). This suggests strong confounding by severity, but insomnia remains a cheap, EHR-visible flag.
- Item 9 helps stratify risk but is insufficient alone: about 1/3 of near-term attempts and deaths follow a "not at all" response (civilian data). The VA therefore moved to the C-SSRS Screener (see Q3).

### Gaps
- Exact Ilgen 2010 HRs and CIs for depression, SUD (men), schizophrenia, PTSD and other anxiety were not retrievable (only rank order). Full-text Table 2 is needed. It is also unclear whether these HRs are age-adjusted only or mutually adjusted.
- Conner/Ilgen comorbidity HRs (PMC8372244) were not extracted (fetch blocked).
- Bullman & Kang (e.g., Vietnam-era PTSD and suicide cohorts) and Conner et al. (2014+) PTSD-specific VA estimates were not retrieved this session.
- No veteran-specific NSSI effect sizes were found. ICD-10-CM R45.88 (nonsuicidal self-harm, added FY2022) [prior knowledge, not re-verified this session] should enable future VA analyses.
- Veteran-specific effect sizes for GAD vs panic separately, and for nightmare disorder vs PTSD-related nightmares, were not found.
- Army STARRS / Millennium Cohort effect sizes (service-member rather than veteran) were not retrieved.
- A civilian MHRN study ("Diagnosed Mental Health Conditions and Risk of Suicide Mortality," *Psychiatr Serv* 2019, doi:10.1176/appi.ps.201800346) gives diagnosis-specific civilian comparators, but numbers were not extracted. — [Psychiatric Services](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201800346)

## Q2. Latest VA suicide statistics (2026 Annual Report, 2024 data; 2025 Annual Report, 2023 data)

### Takeaway
The 2026 National Veteran Suicide Prevention Annual Report was released 30 Sep 2026 and covers 2001–2024. It counts **6,488 veteran suicides in 2024 (about 17.7/day, down from 18.2/day in 2023)** at an unadjusted rate of **36.4 per 100,000**, versus 24.3/100k in 2001. Firearms remain the dominant method: the veteran firearm-suicide rate rose 68.7% from 2001 to 2024. Women veterans' rate was about **double (+99.9%)** that of non-veteran women, and **62% of decedents had no VHA care** in the year of death or prior year.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 Annual Report (data year 2024):**
  - Rate trajectory: 24.3/100k in 2001, 36.6/100k in 2023, **36.4/100k in 2024**.
  - The veteran **firearm** suicide rate rose **68.7%** from 2001 to 2024.
  - — [VA OMHSP, 2026 National Veteran Suicide Prevention Annual Report (PDF)](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/docs/data-sheets/2026/CMR_2026_National_Veteran_Suicide_Prevention_Annual_Report.pdf) (search extract)
- Release and scope (covers 2001–2024): — [VA News press release, "VA releases annual Veteran suicide prevention report, updated with 2024 data"](https://news.va.gov/press-room/va-releases-annual-veteran-suicide-prevention-report-updated-with-2024-data/)
- **6,488 veteran suicides in 2024, ~17.7/day, vs 18.2/day in 2023.** — [Stars and Stripes, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.stripes.com/veterans/2026-10-02/veteran-suicide-small-increase-2024-23028014.html); [Forbes, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nicoleroberts/2026/10/20/6488-veteran-lives-lost-but-gaps-and-unknowns-remain/); [Clermont Sun op-ed, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.clermontsun.com/2026/10/21/op-ed-report-documents-6488-veteran-suicides-in-2024)
  - **Conflict:** secondary outlets give the 2023 count as 6,662 (Stripes) vs 6,398 (another outlet). This may reflect revision of 2023 data between the 2025 and 2026 reports. Verify in the PDF.
- 2024 women veterans' suicide rate was **99.9% higher** than for women without military service. **62%** of veterans who died by suicide in 2024 had not received VHA care in that year or the year before. — [Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/veterans/2026-10-02/veteran-suicide-small-increase-2024-23028014.html); [VA News](https://news.va.gov/press-room/va-releases-annual-veteran-suicide-prevention-report-updated-with-2024-data/)
- Secondary sources only (not verified against PDF): 2024 age-adjusted rate **39.3/100k**; male **39.2/100k**; female **13.9/100k** (adjustment status unclear). — [Junction BH summary](https://junctionbh.com/national-veteran-suicide-prevention-report/); [Red Star Foundation](https://redstarfoundation.org/veteran-suicide/)
- **2025 Annual Report (data year 2023):**
  - Overall veteran rate **35.2/100k** (up from 34.7 in 2022).
  - Female veterans **13.9/100k** (from 13.7); male veterans **37.8/100k**.
  - Firearm suicide **25.8/100k** of the 35.2 total (about 73%), followed by suffocation.
  - — [VA 2025 Annual Report Part 2 (PDF)](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/docs/data-sheets/2025/2025_Annual_Report_Part_2_508.pdf); [Primary News Source copy](https://primarynewssource.org/sourcedocument/2025-national-veteran-suicide-prevention-annual-report-part-2-report-findings/)
  - **Conflict/note:** the 2026 report lists 2023 at 36.6/100k vs 35.2/100k in the 2025 report. This likely reflects a different rate basis (crude vs age/sex-adjusted) or data revision. Flag for the writer.
- Secondary claim (unverified): Priority Group 5 veterans aged 18–34 had a 2023 suicide rate of **85.4/100k**. — [MentalHealthStats blog](https://mhstats.org/blog/veteran-suicide-statistics/)
- Among recently separated veterans, a study of suicide methods found firearms predominated. — ["Analysis of Methods of Suicide Among US Military Veterans Recently Separated From Military Service," PubMed 35522285](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35522285)

### Inferences
- About 3/4 of veteran suicides involve firearms (2023: 25.8/35.2 ≈ 73%). Any EHR screening-referral pathway should therefore include **lethal-means (firearm) counseling** as a co-trigger.
- Because about 62% of decedents were outside VHA care, VA EHR-based detection reaches only a minority of eventual decedents. Community-care claims and non-VA EHRs (e.g., via HL7/FHIR) matter.

### Gaps
- The exact 2024 age/sex-adjusted veteran vs non-veteran rate ratio, age-band rates (18–34, 35–54, 55–74, 75+), the firearm % by sex for 2024, and the report's VHA-diagnosis-specific rates were not obtainable (PDF fetch blocked). The writer should pull these from the 2026 PDF.
- Whether the 2026 report revised 2023 figures (36.6 vs 35.2) is unconfirmed.

## Q3. EHR signals that precede attempts/suicide, screening instruments (LOINC, cutoffs) and VA screening workflows, with per-condition referral triggers

### Takeaway
EHR-visible precursors with veteran-specific evidence include:
- recent psychiatric inpatient discharge (297/100k person-years in the following year; risk highest in days 1–90)
- PHQ-9 item 9 endorsement (HR ≈1.9)
- new or recorded sleep-disorder diagnoses
- opioid discontinuation after long-term therapy
- benzodiazepine receipt in bipolar disorder
- comorbid SUD
- recent military separation

Since November 2020, VA universal screening (Risk ID) uses the **C-SSRS Screener** as the primary screen, replacing PHQ-9 item 9. A positive screen leads to the **Comprehensive Suicide Risk Evaluation (CSRE)**.

### Cited Findings

**VA Risk ID workflow**
- Risk ID began as a 3-stage process: **(1) PHQ-9 item 9 → (2) C-SSRS Screener if item 9 positive → (3) VHA Comprehensive Suicide Risk Evaluation (CSRE) if C-SSRS positive.** — [Bahraini N, et al. "Assessment of Rates of Suicide Risk Screening and Prevalence of Positive Screening Results Among US Veterans After Implementation of the VA Suicide Risk Identification Strategy," *JAMA Netw Open* 2020;3(10):e2022531](https://dx.doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.22531)
- **In November 2020 the C-SSRS Screener replaced item 9 as VA's primary universal screen**, with screening extended to all veterans seen in VHA. — [PMC10089346, "VA's implementation of universal screening and evaluation for the suicide risk identification program in November 2020"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10089346/)
- The Joint Commission does **not** consider item 9 alone a validated suicide-risk screen, but **does** consider the C-SSRS Screener validated. — [VA Risk ID FAQ v4.0 (NAVAO copy)](https://navao.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Risk-ID-FAQ-4.0.pdf)
- PHQ-9 item 9 tracks passive (r_φ=0.79) and nonspecific active ideation (r_φ=0.59) on the C-SSRS, but not intent, plan or behavior items. This was in trans women, a non-veteran sample. — [Practical Psychiatry abstract](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/practicalpsychiatry/abstract/10.1097/pra.0000000000000967~suicide-risk-screening-among-trans-women-screening?redirectionsource=fulltextview)
- VA uses **STORM** (Stratification Tool for Opioid Risk Mitigation) to flag patients at risk of overdose or suicide. — [NASEM materials on stopping opioid prescriptions](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/9fba0cbf-f249-4027-a572-56f7d4d80730)

**EHR precursor signals (veteran evidence)**
- **Psychiatric hospital discharge:**
  - Male veterans discharged from VHA acute psychiatric units, 2005–2010: 929 suicides in the year after discharge, **297/100,000 person-years**.
  - The rate rose from 234/100k (2005) to 340/100k (2008), then plateaued.
  - — [Britton PC, et al. "Suicide mortality among male veterans discharged from VHA acute psychiatric units from 2005 to 2010," *Soc Psychiatry Psychiatr Epidemiol* 2017;52:1081–1087, doi:10.1007/s00127-017-1377-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00127-017-1377-x)
  - Risk is highest in days 1–90 but stays elevated through days 91–365. — ["Shorter- and Longer-Term Risk for Suicide Among Male US Military Veterans in the Year After Discharge From Psychiatric Hospitalization," *J Clin Psychiatry*](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/risk-suicide-male-us-military-veterans-year-after-discharge-psychiatric-hospitalization/)
- A narrative review reports veterans are about **5×** more likely to die by suicide within 12 weeks of psychiatric discharge than the broader VHA treatment population, and about **54×** the general US population over the same period. — [PMC13207306, "Suicide Risk Following Psychiatric Hospitalization: A Narrative Review"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13207306/)
- **Service members** (DoD, 2001–2011): **568/100,000 person-years** in the first 12 weeks post-discharge. — [Luxton DD, et al. *Psychiatr Serv* 2013, doi:10.1176/appi.ps.201200413](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201200413)
- Administrative-data models predict suicide after VHA psychiatric hospitalization. — [PMC7219514](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7219514/). More recent work adds social determinants and NLP. — [PMC12698767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12698767)
- Timing of outpatient follow-up after discharge is associated with suicide risk. — [*JAMA Netw Open* 2023, "Follow-Up Timing After Discharge and Suicide Risk Among Patients Hospitalized With Psychiatric Illness"](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2810365) (effect size not extracted)
- **Opioid discontinuation:** stopping long-term opioid therapy was associated with increased risk of death from overdose or suicide in about 1.39M VHA patients (observational, not causal). — [Oliva EM, et al. "Associations between stopping prescriptions for opioids, length of opioid treatment, and overdose or suicide deaths in US veterans: observational evaluation," *BMJ* 2020;368:m283, doi:10.1136/bmj.m283](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m283)
  - At a rural VA, 36% of 150 tapered or discontinued patients died or had suicidal ideation within a year. This is a small sample. — [*Pain Med* 2024, doi:10.1093/pm/pnae013](https://doi.org/10.1093/pm/pnae013)
  - Target-trial emulations of opioid and benzodiazepine use and mortality in veterans (2007–2019). — [NASEM, "Veterans, Prescription Opioids and Benzodiazepines, and Mortality, 2007–2019"](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28584/chapter/14)
- **Benzodiazepine in bipolar disorder:** associated with increased suicide risk in VHA patients. — [J Clin Psychiatry 2025 (24m15424)](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/suicide-veterans-health-administration-patients-bipolar-disorder-evidence-increased-risk-benzodiazepine-Receipt-24m15424.pdf)
- **Sleep disorder diagnoses:**
  - Insomnia, nightmare and SRBD diagnoses are associated with suicide attempts (ORs 5.62 / 2.49 / 1.37). — [PubMed 31727433](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31727433/)
  - Sleep disturbance shortens time to attempt after residential discharge (HR 1.16). — [Frontiers in Sleep 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsle.2023.1173650/full)
- **Item 9 response:** HR 1.92 (VA). — [Louzon 2016](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201500149). The civilian 1-year gradient runs from 0.4% to 4%. — [Simon 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256610695_Does_Response_on_the_PHQ-9_Depression_Questionnaire_Predict_Subsequent_Suicide_Attempt_or_Suicide_Death)
- **Prior documented suicide attempt in service** and subsequent VHA use: suicide risk and VHA utilization among veterans with an attempt documented during service. — [*J Clin Psychiatry*, "Suicide Risk and VHA Utilization Among Those With a Documented Suicide Attempt in the US Military"](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/suicide-risk-veterans-health-administration-use-suicide-attempt-us-military/)

**Per-condition EHR triggers that should prompt referral for formal MH screening/evaluation**

The structured-data elements are taken from the evidence above. ICD-10-CM codes are standard code-set facts **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session; check the CDC ICD-10-CM browser https://icd10cmtool.cdc.gov/ for current FY descriptors]**.

- **Suicidality / self-harm (all conditions):**
  - R45.851 suicidal ideation; T14.91XA suicide attempt.
  - Poisoning codes with intentional self-harm (T36–T65, character "2", e.g., T42.4X2A benzodiazepine) and X71–X83 external-cause codes.
  - R45.88 nonsuicidal self-harm (NSSI); Z91.51 personal history of suicidal behavior; Z91.52 personal history of nonsuicidal self-harm.
  - Any PHQ-9 item 9 ≥1 (LOINC 44260-8); C-SSRS screener positive.
  - Evidence base: [Louzon 2016](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201500149); [Bahraini 2020](https://dx.doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.22531)
- **PTSD (F43.10 unspecified, F43.11 acute, F43.12 chronic):**
  - PC-PTSD-5 ≥3 (or ≥4) → PCL-5 / CAPS-5.
  - Nightmares (F51.5 nightmare disorder) and insomnia diagnoses.
  - Prazosin prescription (nightmare proxy) [inference].
  - Comorbid AUD/SUD (F10–F19), which raises suicide risk in PTSD per [WHI](https://www.whijournal.com/article/S1049-3867(18)30489-4/pdf).
  - Z65.5 exposure to war/hostilities; Z91.82 history of military deployment; TBI codes (S06.x, F07.81 postconcussional syndrome).
  - Chronic pain plus opioids.
- **MDD (F32.0–F32.5, F32.9, F32.A; recurrent F33.x):**
  - PHQ-2 ≥3 / PHQ-9 ≥10, with item 9 checked.
  - New antidepressant start, dose change or switch [inference].
  - Recent psychiatric discharge.
  - Comorbid AUD, given the depression + AUD prospective attempt risk post-inpatient ([JAD 2016](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032715302147)).
  - Insomnia diagnosis or hypnotic Rx.
- **Bipolar disorder (F31.x):**
  - Positive MDQ.
  - Antidepressant-emergent agitation or mania [inference].
  - Lithium, valproate or antipsychotic prescriptions with gaps (non-adherence) [inference].
  - Benzodiazepine co-prescription ([JCP 2025](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/suicide-veterans-health-administration-patients-bipolar-disorder-evidence-increased-risk-benzodiazepine-Receipt-24m15424.pdf)).
  - Multiple prior "MDD" diagnoses with poor antidepressant response [inference].
- **GAD (F41.1) / Panic (F41.0) / anxiety unspecified (F41.9):**
  - GAD-7 ≥10.
  - Repeated ED visits for chest pain, palpitations or dyspnea with negative cardiac workup [inference].
  - Long-term benzodiazepine Rx, particularly with opioids [inference; see the NASEM target-trial emulations](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28584/chapter/14).
- **Schizophrenia (F20.x) / schizoaffective (F25.x) / other psychosis (F22, F23, F28, F29):**
  - Long-acting injectable missed doses; antipsychotic refill gaps [inference].
  - Recent first-episode diagnosis or psychiatric discharge.
  - Homelessness codes (Z59.0x).
  - Comorbid sleep disorders, which are prevalent and under-recorded ([PMC10476039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476039)).
- **Sleep:**
  - Insomnia: G47.00, G47.01, G47.09; F51.01 primary insomnia; F51.0x.
  - Nightmare disorder: F51.5.
  - OSA: G47.33.
  - ISI ≥15; STOP-BANG ≥3; hypnotic or z-drug Rx; CPAP non-adherence [inference].
  - Evidence base: [PubMed 31727433](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31727433/); [Frontiers 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsle.2023.1173650/full)
- **Utilization and medication patterns (cross-cutting):**
  - Psychiatric discharge in the last 90–365 days ([Britton 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00127-017-1377-x)).
  - Opioid discontinuation after long-term use, especially within the last ~3 months ([Oliva 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m283)).
  - STORM / REACH VET flags.
  - Missed MH appointments or no-shows; ED visits for self-harm or intoxication [inference; no veteran effect size retrieved].

### Inferences
- A practical EHR rule for an MCP/flagging tool is to escalate to C-SSRS + CSRE whenever **any** of the following is present:
  - item 9 ≥1
  - psychiatric discharge in the last 90 days (highest priority) or last 12 months
  - opioid discontinuation after long-term therapy
  - new bipolar/MDD/psychosis diagnosis
  - insomnia/nightmare diagnosis co-occurring with any MH diagnosis
  - separation from service within the last 12 months
- Negative item 9 should **not** suppress referral when other triggers are present, given that about 36–39% of near-term events follow a "not at all" response (civilian data, Simon 2013).

### Gaps
- LOINC codes for PC-PTSD-5, PCL-5, C-SSRS (screener and full), MDQ, ISI and STOP-BANG were **not confirmed** this session. Verify on loinc.org before use. LOINC may lack codes for some of these (the GitHub draft notes missing codes for other instruments).
- No veteran-specific effect sizes were retrieved for missed appointments, ED visits or benzodiazepine initiation as precursors. The REACH VET predictive model (VA, launched 2017; flags the top 0.1% predicted risk per facility) [prior knowledge, not re-verified] has published evaluations (McCarthy et al. 2021, *JAMA Netw Open*) that were not retrieved here.
- The exact VHA Directive requirements were not verified this session: annual depression screening and PTSD screening cadence (commonly described as annual for the first 5 years post-separation, then every 5 years) [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. Also unverified: the C-SSRS screener positivity rules and the 2024 VA/DoD Suicide Risk CPG recommendations.
- Bahraini 2020 positivity rates (% positive on item 9, C-SSRS and CSRE) were not extracted.

## Q4. High-risk transitions for veterans

### Takeaway
The first year after leaving service is a high-risk window. Ravindran et al. (JAMA Netw Open 2020; n=1,868,970) found suicide risk **peaks 6–12 months post-separation** and declines only modestly over 6 years. Risk is higher for those with <2 years of service and those with **non-honorable discharges**. Post-psychiatric-discharge risk is very high, at about 297/100k person-years in the following year for male veterans and highest in the first 90 days.

### Cited Findings
- Ravindran C, Morley SW, Stephens BM, Stanley IH, Reger MA. "Association of Suicide Risk With Transition to Civilian Life Among US Military Service Members." *JAMA Netw Open* 2020;3(9):e2016261:
  - 1,868,970 separated service members (VA/DoD Identity Repository); Cox models of suicide within 6 years of separation.
  - — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2770538); [PMC7489860](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7489860/)
  - Suicide risk **peaked at 6–12 months post-separation** and decreased only modestly over the following years. Veterans with **<2 years of service** had elevated risk. — [NASEM, "The Landscape of Veteran Health Care and Suicide Risk" (2023)](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/27195/chapter/5); [Healio, 15 Sep 2020](https://www.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20200915/military-service-member-suicide-rates-increase-after-separation-and-vary-by-demographics)
- **Character of discharge:** among more than 3.6 million service members separating in 2002–2021, followed up to 5 years, those **without an honorable discharge** had significantly higher suicide risk than honorably discharged veterans. — [Reger, Ravindran et al., "Character of Discharge From the US Military and Suicide Mortality," *JAMA Netw Open* 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2834370); [US Medicine summary](https://www.usmedicine.com/clinical-topics/suicide/veterans-with-less-than-honorable-discharges-face-elevated-suicide-risk/)
- Mental health symptom trajectories relative to separation (Millennium Cohort): — [Porter et al., *Depress Anxiety* 2022, doi:10.1002/da.23246](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/da.23246) (effect sizes not extracted)
- Vocational, financial and social experiences in early post-separation predict suicidal ideation trajectories. — [ResearchGate, "Contribution of veterans' initial post-separation vocational, financial, and social experiences..."](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369258529_Contribution_of_veterans'_initial_post-separation_vocational_financial_and_social_experiences_to_their_suicidal_ideation_trajectories_following_military_service)
- **Post-psychiatric discharge:**
  - 297/100k person-years in the first year; rising 2005→2008. — [Britton 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00127-017-1377-x)
  - Days 1–90 carry the highest risk. — [J Clin Psychiatry](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/risk-suicide-male-us-military-veterans-year-after-discharge-psychiatric-hospitalization/)
  - About 5× the VHA treatment population within 12 weeks. — [PMC13207306](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13207306/)
  - Premature death, suicide and nonlethal self-harm after psychiatric discharge (civilian/other population). — [*JAMA Netw Open* 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2820374)
- **Discharge from residential MH treatment:** sleep disturbance shortened time to attempt (HR 1.16). — [Frontiers 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsle.2023.1173650/full)
- **Opioid treatment discontinuation** is a medication-related transition (see Q3). — [Oliva 2020 BMJ](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m283)
- **Not in VHA care:** 62% of 2024 decedents had no VHA care in the year of or before death. — [Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/veterans/2026-10-02/veteran-suicide-small-increase-2024-23028014.html)

### Inferences
- EHR transition flags should include:
  - date of separation (DD-214) <12 months, with the 6–12-month window highest
  - service <2 years
  - non-honorable discharge (OTH/BCD/DD; these veterans may have limited VHA eligibility, which explains the EHR blind spot)
  - psychiatric inpatient or residential discharge in the last 90 days
  - opioid stop
  - Z-codes for homelessness (Z59.0x), unemployment (Z56.0), relationship or divorce problems (Z63.0, Z63.5), and legal problems (Z65.3) [ICD-10 prior knowledge, not re-verified]

### Gaps
- Ravindran 2020's exact HRs by time since separation (vs the 6-year baseline), and the separation-year rate per 100k, were not extracted.
- No veteran-specific effect sizes were retrieved in this session for loss of housing, loss of job, or relationship breakdown as precipitants. VA Annual Reports and VA homelessness studies (e.g., Tsai et al.) should be consulted.

## Q5. Prevalence and under-diagnosis of each condition in veterans

### Takeaway
Recorded sleep-disorder diagnoses in VHA have risen steeply. Sleep-related breathing disorders went from **5.5% to 22.2%** of VHA patients (FY2012–FY2018) and insomnia from **7.4% to 11.8%**, which strongly suggests historical under-diagnosis. Veteran prevalence of PTSD, MDD, bipolar disorder, anxiety and psychosis could not be verified from primary sources in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Folmer RL, Smith CJ, Boudreau EA, et al. "Prevalence and management of sleep disorders in the Veterans Health Administration." *Sleep Med Rev* 2020;54:101358. doi:10.1016/j.smrv.2020.101358. FY2012→FY2018: SRBD diagnoses **5.5%→22.2%**; insomnia **7.4%→11.8%**. Among veterans with sleep disorders, PTSD rose from about **15% (2012) to 24% (2018)**. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343094455_Prevalence_and_Management_of_Sleep_Disorders_in_the_Veterans_Health_Administration); [Google Scholar DOI lookup](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?doi=10.1016%2Fj.smrv.2020.101358&amp=&pmid=32791487)
- National Veteran Sleep Disorder Study (VHA 2000–2010): **six-fold** relative increase in total sleep-disorder prevalence. PTSD tripled over the period and carried the highest sleep-disorder prevalence (16%) among comorbidities. — [Alexander M, et al., *Sleep* 2016, PubMed 27091538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27091538/)
- Rising diagnosis rates with greater VA attention suggest **historical under-diagnosis** of sleep disturbance in veterans. This is the review authors' inference. — [PMC9745770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9745770/)
- Sleep disorders in veterans with SMI are prevalent but under-recorded in VA health-record data. — [PMC10476039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476039)
- PTSD, pain and insomnia disorder commonly co-occur in post-9/11 veterans enrolling in VA care. — [PMC13417420](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13417420/)
- Synopsis of 109 studies (1995–2020) on sleep-disorder comorbidity in veterans with PTSD. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342282963_Co-Morbidity_of_Sleep_Disorders_in_Veterans_with_PTSD_An_Integrated_Synopsis_of_109_Studies_1995-2020)
- Sleep disorders and PTSD prevalence by age among Vietnam veterans. — [PMC10737874](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10737874/)
- Clinical link between OSA and PTSD. — [Sleep Research Society update, June 2020](https://www.sleepresearchsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/FP-Scientific-Update-June-2020.pdf)

### Inferences
- The fourfold rise in recorded SRBD over 6 years reflects detection, not incidence. Any veteran with PTSD, obesity, hypertension, TBI or insomnia who lacks an OSA workup is a reasonable STOP-BANG trigger.
- Item-level screens (PC-PTSD-5, PHQ-2) are done routinely in VHA. Under-diagnosis is therefore most likely for bipolar disorder (screened only reactively), sleep disorders, and conditions in veterans outside VHA (62% of suicide decedents).

### Gaps
- Not verified this session: veteran PTSD prevalence (National Center for PTSD commonly cites about 7% lifetime among all veterans, with higher figures in OEF/OIF and Gulf War cohorts [prior knowledge, not re-verified]), and MDD, bipolar, GAD/panic and schizophrenia prevalence among VHA users.
- No veteran-specific estimates of under-diagnosis rates (e.g., % of screen-positives without a diagnosis) were found for PTSD, MDD, bipolar or anxiety.
- Whether a 2025 VA/DoD CPG for Chronic Insomnia Disorder and OSA (updating the 2019 version) has been published could not be confirmed. The same applies to the specific recommendations of the PTSD 2023, MDD 2022, Bipolar 2023 and Suicide Risk 2024 CPGs (healthquality.va.gov not reached).

## Q6. Misdiagnosis and diagnostic overlap (bipolar vs ADHD vs BPD; PTSD vs TBI; depression masking other conditions)

### Takeaway
I found **no veteran-specific primary evidence** in this session quantifying misdiagnosis between bipolar disorder, ADHD and BPD, or between PTSD and TBI. The points below are clinically standard overlap patterns, labeled as inference or prior knowledge, plus the veteran evidence that PTSD's suicide risk is largely comorbidity-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- PTSD's diagnosis-specific suicide HR in VHA was the lowest among men, and comorbid PTSD was associated with lower risk in a depression cohort. PTSD + SUD increases risk. This implies that the *pattern* of comorbidity drives risk more than the PTSD label. — [Ilgen 2010, JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919); [WHI](https://www.whijournal.com/article/S1049-3867(18)30489-4/pdf)
- PTSD, pain and insomnia frequently co-occur in post-9/11 veterans, a "complex and common clinical presentation" that complicates attribution of symptoms. — [PMC13417420](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13417420/)
- Sleep disorders are under-recorded in SMI, so psychosis-related insomnia may be attributed to the SMI rather than coded separately. — [PMC10476039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476039)

### Inferences
- **Bipolar vs MDD/ADHD/BPD:**
  - Bipolar depression often presents first and is coded as MDD (F32/F33). Hypomania is under-reported, so an MDQ should be triggered by recurrent MDD with poor antidepressant response, antidepressant-induced activation, or family history.
  - Episodic distractibility, impulsivity and irritability overlap with ADHD (F90.x); persistent childhood-onset course favors ADHD.
  - Affective instability, impulsivity and self-harm overlap with BPD (F60.3); minutes-to-hours mood shifts tied to interpersonal triggers favor BPD over discrete multi-day episodes.
  - Civilian survey data (Hirschfeld et al. 2003, *J Clin Psychiatry*) reported that about 69% of people with bipolar disorder were initially misdiagnosed, most often as unipolar depression [prior knowledge, civilian, not re-verified this session].
- **PTSD vs mild TBI:** concentration and memory problems, irritability, sleep disturbance, headache and emotional dysregulation overlap. Post-concussive symptoms (F07.81, S06.x history) can be mislabeled as PTSD or vice versa. Both should be screened, e.g., VA TBI screen + PC-PTSD-5 [inference].
- **Depression masking neurodevelopmental or other conditions:** adult ADHD, OSA (fatigue, poor concentration, low mood) and insomnia can present as depression. OSA is plausibly a major "masking" condition given the fourfold rise in recorded SRBD ([Folmer 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343094455_Prevalence_and_Management_of_Sleep_Disorders_in_the_Veterans_Health_Administration)) [inference].
- **Anxiety/panic vs medical:** panic presenting as recurrent cardiac or respiratory ED visits [inference].

### Gaps
- No veteran-specific studies were retrieved quantifying bipolar↔ADHD↔BPD misclassification, PTSD↔TBI misattribution, or the diagnostic delay of bipolar disorder in VHA.
- Army STARRS and Millennium Cohort data on pre-enlistment ADHD/neurodevelopmental conditions and later suicidality were not retrieved this session.
