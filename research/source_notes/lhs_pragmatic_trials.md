## 1. Core LHS frameworks and definitions, and the VA as an LHS

### Takeaway
The LHS concept runs from the IOM's 2007 "Learning Healthcare System" workshop through the 2013 *Best Care at Lower Cost* definition (science, informatics, incentives and culture aligned so that knowledge is a by-product of care) to Friedman's operational learning cycle (Data-to-Knowledge, Knowledge-to-Performance, Performance-to-Data) and AHRQ's LHS researcher competencies (Forrest et al. 2018: 33 competencies in 7 domains, since revised to 38 in 8 domains). The VA is the canonical US example. HSR&D/QUERI supply embedded research and implementation capacity, PEPReC supplies randomized policy evaluation, Diffusion of Excellence spreads frontline innovations, and the Million Veteran Program supplies a genomic and EHR data asset. A screening-referral validation program should be framed as one turn of this cycle: model, intervention, embedded evaluation, then feedback into operations.

### Inferences
- A validation program for an adult ADHD/autism/PTSD/depression screening-referral tool maps onto Friedman's cycle as follows. D2K: model development and retrospective validation in CDW/VINCI data. K2P: CDS plus referral pathway, deployed with implementation strategies. P2D: embedded pragmatic trial outcomes and continuous monitoring fed back to the next model version.
- The VA-specific "evidence-based policy" route (QUERI/PEPReC partnership with an operational owner such as OMHSP) is the most realistic way to obtain randomized rollouts at national scale. STORM shows this is feasible. REACH VET shows what happens when rollout is not randomized: evaluation falls back to quasi-experimental triple-differences (see Q3).
- The AHRQ competency domains can structure the team: methods/biostatistics, informatics (CDS build in CPRS/VistA and the Oracle Health Federal EHR), ethics/regulatory, implementation science, and operational-partner engagement.

## 2. Appropriate pragmatic trial designs (PRECIS-2, cluster, stepped-wedge, encouragement, RDD, ITS, platform, SMART, A/B tests of CDS) and Living Textbook guidance

### Takeaway
For a model-triggered screening-referral intervention, the main options are: (a) a cluster-randomized or stepped-wedge rollout at the facility or clinic level, where the CDS is a system-level change and contamination is likely; (b) patient- or encounter-level randomization (A/B testing) of CDS variants such as interruptive vs. passive alerts or alert thresholds, as in VSAIL; (c) a regression-discontinuity design exploiting the model's risk threshold, as REACH VET's top-0.1% cut-off allows; and (d) SMART or adaptive designs for sequencing outreach intensity. PRECIS-2 should be used to document how pragmatic each design choice is. The NIH Pragmatic Trials Collaboratory Living Textbook gives operational guidance on stepped-wedge vs. parallel cluster designs, constrained randomization, consent and DSMB issues.

### Cited Findings
**PRECIS-2**
- PRECIS-2 (Loudon K, Treweek S, Sullivan F, Donnan P, Thorpe KE, Zwarenstein M. *BMJ* 2015;350:h2147) scores 9 domains from 1 (very explanatory) to 5 (very pragmatic): eligibility, recruitment, setting, organisation, flexibility (delivery), flexibility (adherence), follow-up, primary outcome and primary analysis. It is used at the design stage to match design to purpose. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1136/bmj.h2147](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h2147)
- The CONSORT extension for pragmatic trials (Zwarenstein M et al. *BMJ* 2008;337:a2390) and the CONSORT extension for cluster trials (Campbell MK et al. *BMJ* 2012;345:e5661) are the reporting standards. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1136/bmj.a2390](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.a2390); [doi:10.1136/bmj.e5661](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e5661)

**NIH Pragmatic Trials Collaboratory Living Textbook**
- The Living Textbook of Pragmatic Clinical Trials is a free, continually updated online resource. A handout gives a last-updated date of 31 Jan 2025. **[verified]** — [Living Textbook handout (DCRI)](https://dcricollab.dcri.duke.edu/sites/NIHKR/KR/NIH%20Collaboratory%20Living%20Textbook%20Handout.pdf); [rethinkingclinicaltrials.org](https://rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/)
- The "Experimental Designs and Randomization Schemes" chapter (statistical section updated 27 Mar 2024) covers parallel cluster designs, stepped-wedge designs, cluster-crossover designs, covariate-constrained randomization and pair matching. **[verified]** — [Living Textbook (rethinkingclinicaltrials.org)](https://rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/)
- Living Textbook guidance on stepped-wedge designs: all clusters start in control and cross over to the intervention in random order on a staggered schedule. Stepped-wedge CRTs "accommodate potentially more between-cluster heterogeneity" and "generally do not need as many clusters to achieve the same statistical power" than parallel CRTs. They are also justified "in the context of pragmatic trials where the interventions are presumed (often with little evidence) to be beneficial." Because they combine cluster-randomized and longitudinal features, their analysis is more complex. **[verified]** — [Living Textbook, Experimental Designs chapter](https://rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/)
- In cluster-crossover designs, by contrast, "not all clusters receive the intervention, unlike ... the stepped-wedge trial." **[verified]** — [Living Textbook](https://rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/)
- Stepped-wedge methods and reporting: Hemming K, Haines TP, Chilton PJ, Girling AJ, Lilford RJ, *BMJ* 2015;350:h391; CONSORT stepped-wedge extension, Hemming K et al., *BMJ* 2018;363:k1614. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1136/bmj.h391](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h391); [doi:10.1136/bmj.k1614](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k1614)

**Other designs**
- Rapid-cycle randomized testing of operational changes (an "A/B testing" LHS model) at NYU Langone. Randomized QI projects (e.g., EHR alerts, patient outreach) were embedded in operations and completed quickly, with null results used to stop ineffective programs (Horwitz LI, Kuznetsova M, Jones SA, *N Engl J Med* 2019;381:1175-1179). **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1056/NEJMsb1900856](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsb1900856)
- Regression-discontinuity designs in health care: treatment is assigned by a threshold on a continuous score (such as a risk-model percentile), and causal effects are estimated locally at the cut-off (Venkataramani AS, Bor J, Jena AB, *BMJ* 2016;352:i1216). **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1136/bmj.i1216](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i1216)
- REACH VET's program rule (top 0.1% of predicted risk triggers outreach) created the comparison group used in its evaluation: those in the top 0.3%-0.1% tier, below the eligibility threshold. This is effectively a threshold-based design analyzed with triple differences rather than a formal RDD. **[verified]** — [McCarthy et al., JAMA Netw Open 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2790743)
- Interrupted time series (segmented regression) for system-wide policy changes: Bernal JL, Cummins S, Gasparrini A, *Int J Epidemiol* 2017;46(1):348-355. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1093/ije/dyw098](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw098)
- Randomized encouragement designs, stepped-wedge, RDD and other alternatives to the individually randomized RCT for public-health interventions are reviewed in West SG, Duan N, Pequegnat W, et al., *Am J Public Health* 2008;98(8):1359-1366. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.2105/AJPH.2007.124446](https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2007.124446)
- SMART (sequential multiple assignment randomized trial) and MOST for building adaptive interventions, e.g., escalating from portal message to phone outreach to warm handoff among non-responders: Collins LM, Murphy SA, Strecher V, *Am J Prev Med* 2007;32(5 Suppl):S112-S118. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1016/j.amepre.2007.01.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amepre.2007.01.022)
- Adaptive platform trials (master protocol, multiple arms added or dropped by pre-specified rules): Adaptive Platform Trials Coalition, *Nat Rev Drug Discov* 2019;18(10):797-807. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1038/s41573-019-0034-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41573-019-0034-3)
- VA precedent for embedding randomization in routine care: the VA "point-of-care" clinical trial of insulin sliding-scale vs. weight-based dosing. It used EHR (CPRS)-embedded randomization and outcome capture (Fiore LD, Brophy M, Ferguson RE, et al., *Clin Trials* 2011;8(2):183-195). **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1177/1740774511398368](https://doi.org/10.1177/1740774511398368)
- The Vanderbilt VSAIL trial is a model for CDS A/B testing. Encounters with patients flagged by the VSAIL model in 3 neurology clinics were randomized to interruptive vs. non-interruptive CDS (561 patients, 596 encounters; NCT05312437). **[verified]** — [PMC11699529](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11699529/); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05312437](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05312437)
- PCORI Methodology Standards set out cross-cutting standards (including heterogeneity of treatment effects, complex interventions and data integrity) applicable to pragmatic comparative-effectiveness trials. **[std-cite]** — [PCORI Methodology Standards](https://www.pcori.org/research/about-our-research/research-methodology/pcori-methodology-standards)

### Inferences
- A recommended staged design sequence:
  1. Silent-mode (shadow) deployment to estimate calibration, PPV and the screen-positive volume per clinic, with no clinician exposure.
  2. Encounter- or patient-randomized A/B tests of CDS variants (alert format, threshold, wording, default orders), with process outcomes such as screen completion and referral placed.
  3. A stepped-wedge or parallel cluster-randomized trial across VA facilities or CBOCs for distal outcomes (diagnostic yield, treatment initiation, self-harm), using covariate-constrained randomization by facility size and baseline screening rates.
  4. An optional SMART embedded in step 3 for outreach intensity among non-responders.
- Choose parallel CRT over stepped-wedge when secular trends are likely, for example concurrent national campaigns or REACH VET 2.0 changes. Stepped-wedge confounds time with treatment and needs explicit time modeling (per Living Textbook and Hemming 2015).
- Model thresholds create natural RDD opportunities. If the tool refers at a percentile cut-off, a pre-registered RDD around the cut-off is a low-cost complement to randomized evidence. It estimates local effects only, for patients near the threshold.
- Randomized encouragement fits when the referral itself cannot be withheld. Randomize the nudge (e.g., the alert or outreach letter), not access to evaluation.

## 3. Real-world examples: pragmatic trials and rigorous evaluations of EHR risk-model-triggered and screening-referral interventions

### Takeaway
The evidence base is mixed. REACH VET (quasi-experimental) was associated with fewer documented suicide attempts and more care engagement, but no detectable change in suicide mortality. STORM (randomized stepped-wedge, VA-wide) showed lower all-cause mortality from mandated case review of model-flagged patients. VSAIL (randomized CDS) showed that interruptive alerts drive far more screening. The 3-arm SPOT trial (n≈18,882) found that a DBT-skills outreach increased self-harm and care management had no benefit, a cautionary result for "flag-and-outreach" interventions. The VA Caring Letters RCT (>100,000 Veterans Crisis Line callers) found no effect on suicide attempts. SPI+ in VA EDs (non-randomized) was associated with 45% fewer suicidal behaviors. VA Risk ID shows the feasibility and yield of universal screening at 140 centers. No randomized trials were found of EHR-triggered adult ADHD or autism screening-referral pathways.

### Cited Findings
**REACH VET (VA; predictive model triggers outreach)**
- McCarthy JF, Cooper SA, Dent KR, et al. "Evaluation of the Recovery Engagement and Coordination for Health-Veterans Enhanced Treatment Suicide Risk Modeling Clinical Program in the Veterans Health Administration." *JAMA Netw Open* 2021;4(10):e2129900. **[verified]** — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2790743); [PMC8524305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8524305)
  - Design: triple differences comparing 6-month pre/post changes for patients in the top 0.1% of predicted risk entering REACH VET (Mar 2017-Dec 2018) against the subthreshold top 0.3%-0.1% tier. Difference-in-differences was used for mortality subcohorts and parallel-trends checks. N=173,313, of whom 40,816 were REACH VET-eligible. **[verified]** — [PMC8524305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8524305)
  - Results: fewer documented suicide attempts (triple-difference ADT −0.05; 95% CI −0.06 to −0.03), more completed outpatient visits, fewer missed appointments, more new safety plans, and fewer MH admissions and ED visits. No difference was found in suicide or all-cause mortality in subcohort analyses. **[verified]** — [PMC8524305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8524305); [NIMH summary](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/news/science-news/2022/study-shows-reach-vet-program-effective-for-veterans-at-high-risk-for-suicide)
  - Limitation: suicide attempts came from site reports (SPAN / Suicide Behavior and Overdose Report), not a fully validated measure, and the design is observational. **[verified]** — [PMC8524305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8524305)
- Model origins: McCarthy JF, Bossarte RM, Katz IR, et al., "Predictive Modeling and Concentration of the Risk of Suicide," *Am J Public Health* 2015;105(9):1935-1942; Kessler RC et al., *Int J Methods Psychiatr Res* 2017;26(3):e1575. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.2105/AJPH.2015.302737](https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2015.302737); [doi:10.1002/mpr.1575](https://doi.org/10.1002/mpr.1575)
- REACH VET 2.0: VA rebuilt the model on a population of more than 7 million Veterans (per an ORNL description). In Oct 2024 VA announced plans to add military sexual trauma and intimate partner violence as predictors, after criticism that the original model under-identified women. **[verified]** — [ORNL KDI](https://kdi.ornl.gov/?p=896); [GovExec, Oct 2024](https://www.govexec.com/technology/2024/10/va-updating-its-ai-suicide-risk-model-reach-more-women/400411/)
- A Dec 2024 Council on Criminal Justice evaluation (2018 data) reported that the REACH VET model did not accurately identify justice-involved veterans who died by suicide, which raises subgroup-performance concerns. **[verified]** — [Council on Criminal Justice](https://counciloncj.org/can-suicide-be-predicted-for-justice-involved-veterans/)

**STORM (VA; randomized stepped-wedge evaluation of a predictive-analytics-targeted mandate)**
- Strombotne KL, Legler A, Minegishi T, et al. "Effect of a Predictive Analytics-Targeted Program in Patients on Opioids: a Stepped-Wedge Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial." *J Gen Intern Med* 2023;38(2):375-381. All 140 VA medical centers were randomized over 23 months (2018-2020) to a policy mandating case review of patients flagged at high risk by STORM, an algorithm using MH/SUD diagnoses, opioid dose and prior adverse events. Mandated review was associated with lower 4-month all-cause mortality (OR 0.78; 95% CI 0.65-0.94) and no detectable effect on serious adverse events. Mandated-review patients were about 5 times more likely to receive a case review. **[verified]** — [Springer, doi:10.1007/s11606-022-07617-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-022-07617-y)
- A secondary analysis in long-term opioid therapy patients (*Substance Abuse*, 2023) found lower odds of opioid discontinuation and lower all-cause mortality (AME −3.31 percentage points; 95% CI −5.63 to −1.00). **[verified via search snippet]** — [SAGE, doi:10.1177/08897077231198299](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/08897077231198299). This is contradicted in a different subgroup (newly diagnosed OUD) by a reported higher 90-day mortality (OR 1.74; 95% CI 1.06-2.87; *Addiction*, 2023). I could not retrieve that paper's citation, so see Gaps.

**VSAIL (Vanderbilt; randomized test of risk-model-guided CDS)**
- Walsh CG, Ripperger MA, Novak L, et al. "Risk Model-Guided Clinical Decision Support for Suicide Screening: A Randomized Clinical Trial." *JAMA Netw Open* 2025 (Jan). Pragmatic randomized trial in 3 VUMC neurology clinics; interruptive vs. non-interruptive CDS triggered by the VSAIL model; 561 patients and 596 encounters. **[verified]** — [PMC11699529](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11699529/); [Vanderbilt VALIANT summary](https://vanderbilt.edu/valiant/2025/01/28/risk-model-guided-clinical-decision-support-for-suicide-screening-a-randomized-clinical-trial); [medRxiv preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.24304318v1)
  - Results: a decision to screen occurred in 42% of interruptive-arm encounters vs. 4% of non-interruptive. Documented assessments given a decision to screen were 52% (63/121) interruptive vs. 92% (11/12) non-interruptive, but interruptive CDS still yielded more documented assessments overall. No suicidal ideation or attempts were reported during the study. Investigators flagged alert fatigue and false-positive stigma as trade-offs. **[verified]** — [VUMC News, Jan 2025](https://news.vumc.org/2025/01/03/ai-tested-for-alerting-clinicians-of-suicide-risk-at-three-vumc-clinics/); [PMC11699529](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11699529/)
- Prior prospective (silent) validation of VSAIL: Walsh CG et al., "Prospective Validation of an Electronic Health Record-Based, Real-Time Suicide Risk Model," *JAMA Netw Open* 2021;4(3):e211428. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.1428](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.1428)

**SPOT (MHRN / NIH Collaboratory; screening-triggered outreach, pragmatic RCT)**
- Simon GE, Shortreed SM, Rossom RC, et al. "Effect of Offering Care Management or Online Dialectical Behavior Therapy Skills Training vs Usual Care on Self-harm Among Adult Outpatients With Suicidal Ideation: A Randomized Clinical Trial." *JAMA* 2022;327(7):630-638. Four health systems; eligibility triggered by PHQ-9 item 9 responses in routine care; 3 arms (usual care, care management, online DBT-skills). **[verified]** — [PubMed 35166800](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35166800/); [PMC8848197](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8848197); [NCT02326883](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02326883)
  - Results over 18 months, fatal or nonfatal self-harm: care management vs. usual care HR 1.07 (97.5% CI 0.84-1.37); DBT-skills vs. usual care HR 1.29 (97.5% CI 1.02-1.64). Event counts were 172 (3.27%) care management, 206 (3.92%) skills, 162 (3.27%) usual care. The authors concluded the findings "do not support implementation." **[verified]** — [PubMed 35166800](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35166800/). A project summary lists usual care at 3.1%, which conflicts with the abstract's 3.27%; the abstract is preferred. — [HealthPartners summary](https://www.healthpartners.com/knowledgeexchange/display/document-rn27397)
  - Total randomized: 18,882 (from the JAMA abstract). **[std-cite]** — [JAMA, doi:10.1001/jama.2022.0423](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.0423)

**Caring Letters / Caring Contacts**
- Reger MA et al. "Caring Letters Sent by a Clinician or Peer to At-Risk Veterans: A Randomized Clinical Trial." *JAMA Netw Open* 2024 (Apr 29). More than 102,000 Veterans Crisis Line callers were randomized to letters signed by a clinician vs. a peer. Caring-letters receipt was not associated with suicide attempt incidence or all-cause mortality (exploratory), but did increase VHA mental health engagement. Suicide mortality was not examined. A planned comparison with a pre-launch cohort is registered (ISRCTN27551361), but no results were found. **[verified]** — [PubMed 38683611](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38683611/); [UW News](https://newsroom.uw.edu/news-releases/caring-letters-show-mixed-results-for-veterans-in-crisis); [ISRCTN27551361](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN27551361)
- Military Caring Contacts via text: Comtois KA, Kerbrat AH, DeCou CR, et al., *JAMA Psychiatry* 2019;76(5):474-483. In soldiers and Marines, the RCT reported lower suicidal ideation and suicide attempts with caring texts added to standard care. **[std-cite; effect sizes not re-verified]** — [doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.4530](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.4530)

**Safety Planning Intervention (SPI+) in VA EDs**
- Stanley B, Brown GK, Brenner LA, et al. "Comparison of the Safety Planning Intervention With Follow-up vs Usual Care of Suicidal Patients Treated in the Emergency Department." *JAMA Psychiatry* 2018;75(9):894-900. Cohort comparison (non-randomized) of 5 VA intervention EDs vs. 4 control EDs, about 1,640 patients. SPI+ was associated with about 45% fewer suicidal behaviors over 6 months (OR ≈0.56) and roughly doubled odds of outpatient MH treatment engagement. **[std-cite; figures from prior knowledge of abstract, not re-verified]** — [doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.1776](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.1776)

**VA Suicide Risk Identification Strategy (Risk ID): universal screening rollout**
- Bahraini NH, Brenner LA, et al. "Assessment of Rates of Suicide Risk Screening and Prevalence of Positive Screening Results Among US Veterans After Implementation of the Veterans Affairs Suicide Risk Identification Strategy." *JAMA Netw Open* 2020;3(10):e2022531. Risk ID has three stages: PHQ-9 item 9 primary screen, then the C-SSRS Screener, then the VHA Comprehensive Suicide Risk Evaluation (CSRE). Data cover the first year (FY2019) at 140 VAMCs: 4,101,685 Veterans with ambulatory visits and 1,044,056 with ED/urgent care visits. Positive rates were 3.5% primary / 0.4% secondary in ambulatory care and 3.6% / 2.1% in ED/urgent care. The authors note most veterans who die by suicide do not receive VA care. **[verified]** — [PMC7578771](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7578771); [Healio summary](https://cm.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20201021/va-strategy-may-effectively-identify-veterans-at-risk-for-suicide)

**Other screening-to-intervention design precedent**
- ED-SAFE (non-VA EDs): Miller IW et al., *JAMA Psychiatry* 2017;74(6):563-570. Three-phase interrupted time series (treatment as usual, then universal screening, then screening plus intervention). The intervention phase was associated with reduced suicide attempts vs. TAU. **[std-cite; effect sizes not re-verified]** — [doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0678](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0678)

**Adult ADHD / autism screening trials**
- The closest randomized evidence found is a 2×2 factorial RCT in a primary care clinic in Edmonton, Canada (Jul-Oct 2024; NCT06530758). It tested ASRS layout variants and found the standard ASRS did not increase screen-positive rates. The authors noted that high positive-screen rates highlight the need for better adult ADHD screening tools in primary care. **[verified]** — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2025, doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1646293](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1646293/full)
- The AAFP National Research Network lists an "Adult ADHD Screening" practice-based study (design and results not retrieved). **[verified existence only]** — [AAFP NRN](https://www.aafp.org/family-physician/patient-care/nrn/studies/all/adult-adhd.html)
- No randomized trial was found that tests an EHR-triggered adult ADHD or autism screening prompt vs. usual care on referral, diagnosis or downstream suicide outcomes (search returned only pilots and QI projects). **[verified as a negative search result]** — [search summary sources: Ann Fam Med 2024;22(6):568](https://www.annfammed.org/content/22/6/568); [UVM ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.uvm.edu/fmclerk/1149)

### Inferences
- The strongest VA template for a randomized, operations-embedded evaluation of a model-flag-plus-mandated-action program is STORM: a national stepped-wedge in which randomization was over the policy mandate, not over access to the model. A screening-referral tool for undiagnosed ADHD/autism/PTSD/depression could copy this design, randomizing facilities to "flag plus mandated screening/referral workflow" vs. "flag available but not mandated."
- SPOT shows that well-intentioned outreach triggered by a risk signal can cause harm or do nothing. Any outreach component (e.g., a portal message telling a veteran they may have undiagnosed ADHD or autism) therefore needs a randomized comparison with self-harm as a monitored safety outcome, not just effectiveness. This argues against uncontrolled national rollout.
- VSAIL shows that CDS format is itself a large-effect "intervention component" (42% vs. 4%). Process-level A/B tests are cheap, fast and well powered, and should precede the costlier outcome trial.
- REACH VET's null result on mortality, alongside positive process and attempt results, reflects the power limitation for suicide death (see Q4). Expect the same pattern for a new tool.
- Risk ID yield figures (3.5% → 0.4% in ambulatory care) give a benchmark for funnel attrition when planning a multi-stage screening-referral cascade.

## 4. Outcomes, metrics and powering for rare outcomes

### Takeaway
Use a hierarchical outcome set. Primary outcomes should be proximal and well powered: screening completion, referral placed and completed, diagnostic yield, time to diagnosis and treatment initiation. Self-harm, suicide attempts, ED visits and suicide death (via VA/DoD Mortality Data Repository / National Death Index linkage) should be secondary or safety outcomes, powered only in very large cluster designs. Clinician burden and alert metrics, and subgroup equity (calibration and sensitivity by race, sex, age, rurality), should be built in. Suicide death at the population veteran rate (35.2 per 100,000 in 2023) needs about 1 million per arm to detect a 20% relative reduction, so mortality endpoints are infeasible outside national programs.

### Cited Findings
- Veteran suicide epidemiology (2025 National Veteran Suicide Prevention Annual Report, 2023 data, released Feb 2026): 6,398 veteran suicides in 2023 (44 fewer than 2022); rate 35.2 per 100,000 (up from 34.7 because the veteran population shrank); male 37.8 and female 13.9 per 100,000. **[verified]** — [VA News](https://news.va.gov/145433/2025-national-veteran-suicide-prevention-report/); [American Legion, Feb 2026](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/be-the-one/2026/february/number-of-veterans-dying-by-suicide-dropped-slightly-from-2022-to-2023-but-the-rate-of-suicide-grew). A later VA report covering 2024 data revises some 2023 counts upward; do not mix figures across reports. **[verified via search note]**
- In REACH VET, suicide attempts came from SPAN / Suicide Behavior and Overdose Report site reports, and mortality subcohort analyses detected no difference. This illustrates the dependence on VA-specific outcome sources and the limited power for death. **[verified]** — [PMC8524305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8524305)
- SPOT used fatal or nonfatal self-harm over 18 months as its primary outcome, ascertained from health-system records plus state death records. Event rates were about 3.3%-3.9% in an ideation-positive population of about 18,882. **[verified rates]** — [PubMed 35166800](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35166800/)
- VSAIL used process outcomes (decision to screen, documented assessment) and monitored suicidal ideation and attempts as safety events. This made a small trial (596 encounters) informative. **[verified]** — [PMC11699529](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11699529/)
- STORM's primary outcomes were all-cause mortality and serious adverse events within 4 months, with case review receipt as a process (fidelity) measure. **[verified]** — [Strombotne 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-022-07617-y)
- Caring Letters used suicide attempts as primary outcome, with VHA MH engagement as secondary and all-cause mortality as exploratory. **[verified]** — [PubMed 38683611](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38683611/)
- Prediction-model PPV for suicide death is very low across published models (systematic review and simulation: Belsher BE et al., *JAMA Psychiatry* 2019;76(6):642-651). Interventions triggered by such models therefore reach mostly people who would not have died by suicide, which supports choosing outcomes beyond death. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.0174](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.0174)
- Subgroup performance of EHR suicide-risk models differs by race and ethnicity: lower sensitivity for Black and American Indian/Alaska Native patients and those without recorded race (Coley RY, Johnson E, Simon GE, Cruz M, Shortreed SM, *JAMA Psychiatry* 2021;78(7):726-734). **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0493](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0493)
- Alert fatigue rises with workload and repeated alerts. Ancker JS et al., *BMC Med Inform Decis Mak* 2017;17:36 provides metrics such as override and acceptance rates and repeated-alert exposure. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1186/s12911-017-0430-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-017-0430-8)
- Implementation outcome taxonomy (acceptability, adoption, appropriateness, feasibility, fidelity, implementation cost, penetration, sustainability): Proctor E et al., *Adm Policy Ment Health* 2011;38(2):65-76. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1007/s10488-010-0319-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10488-010-0319-7)
- Risk ID screen-positive rates (3.5% / 0.4% ambulatory; 3.6% / 2.1% ED) give funnel benchmarks for cascade planning. **[verified]** — [PMC7578771](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7578771)

### Inferences
**Recommended outcome hierarchy for a screening-referral tool**
1. Process: proportion of flagged patients screened (e.g., ASRS, AQ-10, PC-PTSD-5, PHQ-9), screen-positive rate, referral placed, referral completed (consult completed in VA or Community Care), time from flag to evaluation.
2. Diagnostic: new diagnoses per 1,000 flagged (diagnostic yield), confirmation rate among referred (≈ PPV of the cascade), time to diagnosis.
3. Treatment: initiation of guideline-concordant treatment within 90 days (e.g., psychotherapy visits, stimulant or SSRI fills), MH visit counts.
4. Safety and distal outcomes: suicide attempts / self-harm (SPAN/SBOR plus ICD-10 X60-X84, T-codes with intent), MH admissions, ED visits, suicide death via VA/DoD Mortality Data Repository (NDI-linked). Also all-cause mortality, as STORM used.
5. Clinician and system: alert firing rate per clinician-day, override rate, time-in-chart, clinician-reported burden (e.g., NASA-TLX or a short survey), referral-queue wait time (capacity spillover).
6. Equity: model calibration and sensitivity, and intervention effects, by race/ethnicity, sex (women veterans), age, rurality, MST history and justice involvement. These subgroups are prompted by the REACH VET 2.0 and CCJ critiques and by Coley 2021.

**Power illustrations (researcher's calculations, individual randomization, 2-sided α=0.05, 80% power)**
- Suicide death at 35.2/100k/yr, 20% relative reduction: about 1,003,000 per arm. Infeasible except as a national program evaluation.
- Suicide death in a high-risk tier with 1%/yr incidence, 20% RRR: about 35,000 per arm.
- Self-harm at SPOT-like 3.27% over 18 months, 20% RRR: about 10,500 per arm. SPOT had about 6,300 per arm and so was powered for larger effects.
- Referral completion 10% → 15%: about 680 per arm.
- New diagnosis yield 0.5% → 1.0%: about 4,700 per arm.
- Cluster designs inflate these by the design effect 1+(m−1)·ICC. For example, m=500 per clinic with ICC=0.01 gives a DE of about 6.0.

**Strategies for rare outcomes**
- Composite outcomes (self-harm plus attempt plus suicide death, as SPOT did).
- Enrichment of high-risk tiers (REACH VET or STORM approach).
- National stepped-wedge with long follow-up.
- Bayesian analysis with informative or skeptical priors.
- Non-inferiority framing for safety (no increase in self-harm).
- Treating mortality as a monitored safety outcome rather than the primary endpoint.
- Plan for NDI/MDR lag: death data typically arrive many months after year end, so final mortality analyses lag trial completion.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve published ICC values for suicide-attempt or referral outcomes in VA facility-level clusters. These are needed for real sample-size calculations; the Living Textbook or prior VA cluster trials (e.g., STORM) may report them.
- I did not verify the current NDI/MDR data-release lag for VA researchers.
- No validated "diagnostic yield" metric for adult ADHD or autism in VA was found.

## 5. Ethics and regulation (waivers of consent, VA IRB and ORD rules, research vs. QI, DSMB for suicide trials, FDA CDS guidance, ONC HTI-1/HTI-5, equity)

### Takeaway
Minimal-risk pragmatic trials of CDS and referral workflows can usually proceed under a waiver or alteration of consent (45 CFR 46.116(f) / 38 CFR 16, with VA-specific add-ons in VHA Directive 1200.05). SPOT shows that the "impracticability" criterion and DSMB arrangements are the hardest negotiation points for suicide-related trials. VA's Program Guide 1200.21 governs research vs. operations: once an activity is designed to produce generalizable knowledge, it is research and needs IRB oversight. Regulation of the software itself shifted in 2026. FDA reissued its CDS guidance (6 Jan, re-issued 29 Jan 2026), removing the 2022 "time-critical" gloss on criterion 3. ONC's HTI-5 final rule (announced 26 Oct 2026) removed HTI-1's predictive-DSI source-attribute ("model card") and risk-management certification requirements. Transparency and equity safeguards for a VA tool must therefore come mainly from VA's own AI governance and trial protocol, not from ONC certification.

### Cited Findings
**Consent waivers (Common Rule and VA)**
- 45 CFR 46.116(f)(3) allows an IRB to waive or alter consent if all of the following hold: (i) the research involves no more than minimal risk; (ii) it could not practicably be carried out without the waiver or alteration; (iii) if it uses identifiable private information or biospecimens, it could not practicably be carried out without using them in identifiable form; (iv) the waiver will not adversely affect subjects' rights and welfare; and (v) when appropriate, subjects will be given additional pertinent information after participation. **[std-cite]** — [eCFR 45 CFR 46.116](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-A/section-46.116). VA codifies the Common Rule at 38 CFR Part 16. — [eCFR 38 CFR Part 16](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-16)
- VHA Directive 1200.05, "Requirements for the Protection of Human Subjects in Research," was issued 7 Jan 2019 (replacing the 2014 Handbook), amended 8 Jan 2021, and technically amended 13 Jul 2023 (Amendment #3, e.g., adding a 38 U.S.C. § 7330D reference to paragraph 28). The 2019 version aligned waiver of documentation of consent with the revised Common Rule. It also added a VA-specific additional requirement for waivers of informed consent for minimal-risk research under the 2018 Requirements (paragraphs 17.g-h). **[verified]** — [VA ORD Amendment 3 description](https://www.research.va.gov/resources/policies/guidance/Description-Amendment3-VHA-Directive-1200-05.pdf); [VA ORD Amendment 2 description](https://www.research.va.gov/resources/policies/guidance/Description-Amendment2-VHA-Directive-1200-05.pdf); [Summary of changes (archived)](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20220515152338/https://www.research.va.gov/resources/policies/guidance/SummaryChanges-VHADirective1200-05.pdf)

**Research vs. operations/QI in VA**
- VHA Program Guide 1200.21, "VHA Operations Activities That May Constitute Research" (ORD, 9 Jan 2019). A VHA operations activity is not research if it is designed and implemented for internal VA purposes and is not designed to produce information that expands the knowledge base of a scientific discipline. When operations data are combined with additional data for generalizable analysis, the extra collection and the combined analysis are research, while the original operational uses stay non-research. Non-research publications outside VA require a documented non-research determination (attestation by the author and by an operational leader). **[verified]** — [VHA Program Guide 1200.21 (PDF)](https://www.research.va.gov/resources/policies/ProgramGuide-1200-21-VHA-Operations-Activities.pdf); [VA ORD non-research determination guidance](https://www.research.va.gov/resources/policies/guidance/Non-Research-Determinations-for-Publications-Outside-VA.pdf); [VAPHS guidance example](https://www.va.gov/files/2023-09/VAPHS%20Guidance%20on%20Operational%20Activities%20versus%20Research_final_clean%20%2810%29.pdf)

**LHS and cluster-trial ethics frameworks**
- Faden RR, Kass NE, Goodman SN, Pronovost P, Tunis S, Beauchamp TL. "An ethics framework for a learning health care system." *Hastings Cent Rep* 2013;43(s1):S16-S27. It proposes an obligation to learn alongside respect for patients, with oversight calibrated to risk and burden rather than a sharp research/practice line. **[std-cite]** — [doi:10.1002/hast.134](https://doi.org/10.1002/hast.134)

**SPOT as an ethics and DSMB case study**
- SPOT used full waivers of consent to identify eligible patients from records, to randomize to usual care vs. outreach, and to ascertain outcomes from records. Offering the intervention used an alteration/partial waiver with abbreviated online consent. - HHS Section 1557 final rule (May 2024) added 45 CFR 92.210. ### Inferences
**Regulatory pathway for a VA screening-referral validation program**
1. Retrospective model development on CDW/VINCI is research under 1200.05 (IRB, usually a waiver of HIPAA authorization and consent).
2. Silent-mode prospective validation may be operations under 1200.21 if led by an operational partner for internal purposes. If designed for publication or generalizable knowledge, it is research.
3. Randomized CDS or referral trials designed to produce generalizable knowledge are research. They need IRB review, a consent waiver justified under 46.116(f)(3) and 1200.05 ¶17, ClinicalTrials.gov registration and a DSMB.

**DSMB plan for suicide-adjacent trials (lessons from SPOT)**
- Pre-specify that self-harm events are expected outcomes, not stopping triggers.
- Use aggregate, unblinded periodic review of self-harm rates by arm with group-sequential or Bayesian harm boundaries.
- Keep an individual-level clinical safety response (e.g., Risk ID/CSRE pathways, Veterans Crisis Line) separate from trial monitoring.
- Negotiate these terms with the DSMB early to avoid SPOT-type delays.

**Software regulation**
- A clinician-facing tool that shows a risk score and recommends screening or referral, with the basis visible to the clinician (criterion 4), is likely non-device CDS under the 2026 FDA guidance.
- Patient-facing outreach or automated triage without clinician review would fall outside the CDS carve-out and needs separate FDA analysis.
- VA-built tools are not "certified health IT developer" products, so HTI-1/HTI-5 bind VA's EHR vendor (Oracle Health) rather than VA's in-house models.
- After HTI-5, model-card-style transparency for a VA tool should be voluntary and protocol-driven: publish a TRIPOD+AI-style model card and use the VA Trustworthy AI principles and OMB AI requirements.

**Section 1557 caveat**
- Whether 45 CFR 92.210 binds VA's own programs is unclear. The HHS rule covers HHS-funded and HHS-administered programs, and VA is a separate executive agency. The report writer should not assert that it applies to VA without legal confirmation, though it is a useful normative benchmark.

## 6. Implementation science frameworks and the post-deployment monitoring loop

### Takeaway
Use a hybrid effectiveness-implementation study. Type 1 or Type 2 fits a new screening-referral tool, and Type 3 fits scaling an established one, as Curran 2012 and the 2022 "Reflections" update describe. Evaluate with RE-AIM (reach and equity of reach is critical for model-triggered programs) and diagnose barriers with CFIR 2.0. Pair this with a governed post-deployment loop: drift and calibration monitoring, scheduled recalibration (REACH VET → REACH VET 2.0 is the VA precedent), subgroup fairness audits, and governance under the VA Trustworthy AI Framework and the Oct 2025 VA AI strategy.

### Cited Findings
**Hybrid designs**

### Inferences
**Recommended framing**
- Use a Hybrid Type 2 study: randomize facilities (STORM-style stepped-wedge) to test clinical effectiveness (referral completion, diagnostic yield, treatment initiation, with self-harm as a safety outcome) and an implementation strategy (e.g., QUERI-style facilitation plus audit and feedback vs. CDS alone) at the same time.

**RE-AIM operationalization**
- Reach: share of eligible veterans flagged and screened, with demographic representativeness vs. the eligible population.
- Effectiveness: as in Q4.
- Adoption: share of clinics and clinicians acting on alerts.
- Implementation: fidelity to the referral protocol, alert override rate, cost.
- Maintenance: sustained screening at 12-24 months, and model performance stability.

**Use of CFIR 2.0**
- Use CFIR 2.0 for pre-implementation site interviews and to explain heterogeneity of facility-level effects in the stepped-wedge.

**Post-deployment monitoring loop**
- Monthly monitoring of alert volume, PPV and calibration-in-the-large (observed vs. expected), stratified by subgroup.
- Pre-specified recalibration triggers, such as calibration slope outside 0.8-1.2 or a subgroup sensitivity gap above a threshold.
- An annual full refit with versioning.
- A governance board including OMHSP, the operational owner, NAII-aligned AI oversight, ethics and veteran representatives.
- A "model-change freeze" or pre-specified version handling during the trial so that model updates do not confound the trial.
- Particular attention to dataset shift during facility transitions to the Oracle Health Federal EHR.
