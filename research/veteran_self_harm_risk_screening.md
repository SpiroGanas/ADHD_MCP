# Turning Veteran Health Records Into Screening Referrals

An EHR-reading tool can find veterans who should be sent for formal mental health screening, because nearly every condition that raises veteran self-harm risk leaves a codable trace: bipolar disorder (suicide **HR 2.98** in male VHA patients), substance use disorders (crude suicide rate **171.4 per 100,000** with sedative use disorder), personality disorders (**156.5 vs 46.7** suicides per 100,000 person-years), traumatic brain injury (adjusted **HR 1.71**), insomnia (**OR 5.62** for attempts), high-dose or discontinued opioids, military sexual trauma (**HR 2.27** in women), housing instability (**aHR 1.75**) and the first year after leaving service. ADHD and autism, the project's focus, carry some of the largest relative risks in the literature, roughly **2–4 times the odds of suicide attempts and 6–7.5 times the odds of suicide death** in civilian registries, yet no veteran-specific effect size exists for either, and DoD accession rules that disqualify both give recruits every reason to leave them undocumented. That makes them classic under-diagnosed, high-risk targets, and ADHD is also modifiable: in a VA cohort, months on stimulants carried **43% lower odds of suicide death**. Prediction is feasible (a Swedish adult-ADHD EHR model reached **AUC 0.79** six months before diagnosis) but no adult autism EHR model exists, labels are incomplete and noisy, and positive predictive value will be low, so models must be built as positive-unlabeled problems, judged by net benefit at clinician-capacity thresholds, and tested in a STORM-style randomized stepped-wedge rollout rather than REACH VET's after-the-fact quasi-experiment, with the SPOT trial's finding that well-meant outreach increased self-harm as a standing warning. In this learning health system the MCP server is the governed, read-only interface that assembles FHIR evidence, applies versioned rules and models, returns an evidence-linked "consider referral for screening" recommendation to a clinician (never a diagnosis), and logs every recommendation, override and downstream outcome so that each turn of the learning cycle can be evaluated. Many figures below rest on abstracts or secondary sources because full texts were blocked during research, and they are marked accordingly.

## Reading the evidence: three confidence markers qualify every figure

During research in November 2026 the session's network policy blocked full-text access to PubMed, PMC, JAMA Network, the VA mental health site, hl7.org and modelcontextprotocol.io, so most numbers come from abstracts, journal landing pages, search extracts and secondary coverage. A figure with no marker came from the primary paper's own abstract or page. **"(secondary)"** marks a figure taken from a citing paper, press release or news story; **"(verify)"** marks a figure that is conflicting, truncated or single-sourced; and **"(unverified)"** marks a standard reference fact, such as an ICD-10-CM code, LOINC code or instrument cutoff, that is well established but was not re-confirmed against an authoritative source during research. Every effect size is labeled by population (veteran, service member or civilian), because for the two focus conditions the veteran-specific evidence base is essentially empty. All ICD-10-CM codes must be validated against the FY2027 code set that took effect on 1 October 2026 ([CDC ICD-10-CM browser](https://icd10cmtool.cdc.gov/)); only Z91.51, Z91.82 and Z91.85 were confirmed against CMS tables during research ([CMS FY2027 tables](https://www.cms.gov/icd10m/FY2027-fr-v44-fullcode-cms/fullcode_cms/P0996.html); [CMS FY2024 tables](https://www.cms.gov/icd10m/FY2024-version41-fullcode-cms/fullcode_cms/P0969.html)). Throughout, a "referral indicator" is a reason to send a veteran to a clinician for formal screening. Nothing in this report describes a diagnostic rule, and the tool it describes recommends referral for screening; it does not diagnose.

## Part 1: Fourteen risk domains leave EHR fingerprints a referral tool can read

The stakes are measured in the 2026 National Veteran Suicide Prevention Annual Report, released 30 September 2026, which counted **6,488 veteran suicides in 2024, about 17.7 per day**, at an unadjusted rate of **36.4 per 100,000**, up from 24.3 in 2001 ([VA 2026 Annual Report](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/docs/data-sheets/2026/CMR_2026_National_Veteran_Suicide_Prevention_Annual_Report.pdf); [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/veterans/2026-10-02/veteran-suicide-small-increase-2024-23028014.html)). The veteran firearm suicide rate rose **68.7%** between 2001 and 2024, women veterans died by suicide at a rate **99.9% higher** than women who never served, and **62% of decedents had received no VHA care** in the year of death or the year before ([VA News](https://news.va.gov/press-room/va-releases-annual-veteran-suicide-prevention-report-updated-with-2024-data/); [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/veterans/2026-10-02/veteran-suicide-small-increase-2024-23028014.html)). Firearms were involved in **73.3% of veteran suicides in 2023**, against 52.9% among non-veteran adults ([VA 2025 Annual Report, Part 2](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/docs/data-sheets/2025/2025_Annual_Report_Part_2_508.pdf)). Two design facts follow. A tool that reads only VA records can reach at most a minority of eventual decedents, so it should read community-care and non-VA FHIR data wherever it lawfully can, and lethal-means safety counseling belongs alongside any referral it triggers. The 2023 figures differ between reports (6,398 deaths and 35.2 per 100,000 in the 2025 report; a revised count and 36.6 per 100,000 in coverage of the 2026 report), so figures should not be mixed across reports (verify).

The largest VA cohort linking diagnoses to suicide death followed about 3.3 million VHA patients from FY1998–1999 for seven years and observed 7,684 suicides. Any psychiatric diagnosis carried an **HR of 2.50 in men and 5.18 in women**, and **46.8% of decedents had a psychiatric diagnosis recorded in VHA** ([Ilgen 2010](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/11/101101161825.htm)). Diagnosis-specific risk ran bipolar disorder > depression > SUD > schizophrenia > other anxiety > PTSD in men, and SUD > bipolar disorder > schizophrenia > depression > PTSD > other anxiety in women ([Ilgen 2010](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)). That ordering, together with repeated evidence that comorbid clusters such as PTSD with SUD, TBI with PTSD, or insomnia with any psychiatric diagnosis carry more risk than any single label, argues for a tool that scores combinations rather than diagnoses in isolation. Each subsection below gives the strength of association with self-harm or suicide and a referral-indicator table of EHR-observable symptoms, behaviors, comorbidities, codes, coded screening instruments, and medication and utilization signals. ADHD and autism receive the most depth, and a consolidated signal-to-condition table closes Part 1.

### Cross-cutting self-harm signals gate every condition-specific referral

Prior self-harm is the strongest predictor in every published suicide model, and VA already runs a screening pathway that a referral tool must respect rather than duplicate. Since November 2020, VA's universal Risk ID program has used the **C-SSRS Screener as the primary suicide screen**, routing positives to the **Comprehensive Suicide Risk Evaluation (CSRE)** ([PMC10089346](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10089346/)); the Joint Commission treats the C-SSRS Screener as validated but not PHQ-9 item 9 on its own ([VA Risk ID FAQ](https://navao.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Risk-ID-FAQ-4.0.pdf)). In VA data, any endorsement of PHQ-9 item 9 carried an **HR of 1.92 (1.53–2.41)** for suicide death across 447,245 administrations, persisting after adjustment for depression severity ([Louzon 2016](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201500149)). In civilian data, however, **39% of attempts and 36% of suicide deaths within 30 days followed a "not at all" answer** ([Simon 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256610695_Does_Response_on_the_PHQ-9_Depression_Questionnaire_Predict_Subsequent_Suicide_Attempt_or_Suicide_Death)), so a negative item 9 must never suppress a referral. The weeks after psychiatric discharge are the highest-risk window the EHR can see: male veterans discharged from VHA acute psychiatric units died by suicide at **297 per 100,000 person-years** in the following year, with risk concentrated in days 1–90 ([Britton 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00127-017-1377-x); [J Clin Psychiatry](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/risk-suicide-male-us-military-veterans-year-after-discharge-psychiatric-hospitalization/)), and service members died at 568 per 100,000 person-years in the first 12 weeks ([Luxton 2013](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201200413)). The tool's first rule should therefore be a safety gate: any signal in the table below routes the veteran to Risk ID and lethal-means counseling before any ADHD, autism or other screening referral is offered, and the neurodevelopmental referral becomes an adjunct, not a substitute.

### ADHD: a modifiable risk hidden by accession rules

ADHD is common in the military despite rules meant to screen it out. Army STARRS found ADHD among the most prevalent disorders in soldiers, at higher rates than in civilians, with 30-day prevalence of about **7.0% in non-deployed soldiers** (secondary) ([Howlett 2018 manuscript](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/am-pdf/10.1002/jts.22347); [Kessler 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4057988/)) and weighted pre-deployment prevalence of **6.1%** in brigade combat teams bound for Afghanistan ([Howlett 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6386189)). For comparison, **6.0% of US adults** reported a current ADHD diagnosis in late 2023, more than half of them diagnosed at 18 or older (civilian) ([MMWR 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11466376/)). Under DoDI 6130.03 Volume 1, ADHD is reportedly disqualifying for enlistment if the applicant had an IEP, 504 Plan or work accommodations after the 14th birthday, a comorbid mental disorder, ADHD medication in the previous 24 months, or documented adverse academic or occupational performance (secondary; a reported pilot allowing enlistment after 12 months off medication conflicts with the 24-month rule (verify)) ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/join-the-military/eligibility-requirements/joining-the-military-with-anxiety-or-adhd); [MilitaryPrepHub](https://www.militaryprephub.com/blog/can-you-join-military-with-adhd.html)). Those rules penalize exactly the documentation an EHR would need, giving applicants strong reasons to stop medication and avoid evaluation before enlisting. Because ADHD is not a retention standard, first diagnosis often happens in service or at VA, and VHA patients with an ADHD diagnosis rose almost **60%, from about 70,000 to about 111,300, between FY2018 and FY2022**, with stimulant prescriptions up 53% (secondary) ([Military.com on the VA OIG report](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2024/04/05/veteran-prescriptions-addictive-adhd-drugs-are-not-being-properly-vetted-and-tracked-watchdog-says.html)). The VA Inspector General found the documentation behind those diagnoses and prescriptions was often unstandardized and poorly supported ([VA OIG 2024](https://www.vaoig.gov/reports/national-healthcare-review/deficiencies-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-diagnostic)). Two consequences shape the tool: a missing F90 code is weak evidence that ADHD is absent, and the F90 codes that do exist are noisy labels that cannot train a model without chart validation.

The association with suicidal behavior is consistent and large, but every estimate is civilian. A meta-analysis of 57 studies across age groups pooled **ORs of 2.37 (1.64–3.43) for suicide attempts, 3.53 (2.94–4.25) for ideation, 4.54 (2.46–8.37) for plans and 6.69 for completed suicide**; the completed-suicide interval, reported as 3.24–17.37, is asymmetric and may be mis-transcribed (verify) ([Septier 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763418309412)). In a Swedish matched cohort of 51,707 people with ADHD, the attempt **OR was 3.62 (3.29–3.98)** and the completed-suicide OR 5.91 after adjustment for comorbid psychiatric disorders (secondary) ([Ljung 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263431493_Common_Etiological_Factors_of_Attention-DeficitHyperactivity_Disorder_and_Suicidal_Behavior_A_Population-Based_Study_in_Sweden)). A Danish national cohort of about 2.9 million people found suicidal behavior at an **IRR of 4.7 (4.3–5.1)**, with crude incidence of 415.6 versus 75.5 per 100,000 person-years ([BJPsych 2019](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/suicidal-behaviour-among-persons-with-attentiondeficit-hyperactivity-disorder/6CECF48A64E415C871D233B2607114ED)), and a meta-analysis restricted to longitudinal studies gave an attempt OR of 3.34 (1.68–6.65) ([Garas 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12177204/)). Adult evidence on non-suicidal self-injury is thin, with a pooled OR of 1.85 (0.56–6.08) that is not significant ([PMC13797376](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13797376)). Excess mortality in ADHD is mostly unnatural: in Denmark mortality was 5.85 versus 2.21 per 10,000 person-years, accidents caused 42 of the 79 deaths with a known cause, and rate ratios were higher in women and in people diagnosed as adults (secondary) ([ScienceDaily on Dalsgaard 2015](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/02/150225205834.htm)); in Sweden unintentional injury and suicide led the causes of death, much of it explained by later-onset psychiatric comorbidity ([Sun 2019](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2739304)). Army STARRS adds military context without an ADHD-specific estimate: **47–58% of soldiers' lifetime suicidal behaviors began before enlistment**, and intermittent explosive disorder, which clusters with ADHD, predicted first post-enlistment attempts at an OR of about 3.7–3.8 ([Nock 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4100464)). No VA or DoD study comparing suicide outcomes in veterans with and without ADHD was found.

What makes ADHD the highest-leverage referral target is that identification connects to treatment with protective associations. Among VA patients with an active ADHD diagnosis in 2016–2019, **months with an active stimulant prescription carried an OR of 0.57 (0.36–0.88) for suicide death** compared with months without (veteran) ([PMC10897683](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897683/)). A Swedish target-trial emulation of 148,581 people aged 6–64 with newly diagnosed ADHD found that starting medication within three months was followed by roughly **17% fewer first episodes of suicidal behavior**, 15% less substance misuse, 12% fewer transport accidents and 13% less criminality over two years, with larger effects for stimulants and for people with prior events (percentages from press coverage; exact IRRs need checking against the BMJ text) ([Zhang 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12344785/); [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/drug-discovery/news/adhd-medications-cut-risk-of-self-harm-by-17-and-crime-by-13-403612)). Adults with ADHD had a motor-vehicle-crash OR of about 1.45–1.49, falling to about 0.58–0.62 in months on medication within the same person (secondary) ([Chang 2017 commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10270426/)). These are within-person and emulated-trial designs rather than randomized evidence, and confounding by indication remains possible, but they give an ADHD referral a plausible causal path to lower self-harm that the trial program in Part 2 should measure directly through treatment initiation.

Comorbidity (depression, SUD, conduct and personality disorders) mediates much but not all of the excess risk, emotional dysregulation is the leading candidate mediator ([BMC Psychiatry 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-023-05099-8)), and thwarted belongingness and perceived burdensomeness mediate the link between ADHD traits and ideation in young adults ([Humanit Soc Sci Commun 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03578-1)). A military pathway runs through trauma: pre-deployment ADHD predicted post-deployment PTSD at an **AOR of 2.13 (1.51–3.00)**, rising to 2.50 among soldiers without prior PTSD ([Howlett 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6386189)), which makes a chain from ADHD to PTSD to suicide plausible, though untested. The same overlap complicates detection. In post-9/11 veterans the ASRS v1.1 may not separate ADHD symptoms from PTSD symptoms ([Military Medicine 2025](https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/190/5-6/e1106/7911917)); in a VA polytrauma clinic, 73 combat veterans met ADHD criteria and reported more attention problems but not more overall distress than those without ADHD ([PMC6363329](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6363329/)); and chronic mild TBI produces ADHD-like deficits while ADHD itself raises injury risk ([Am J Med 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002934325007028); [umbrella review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-024-00356-5)). The defensible discriminator is evidence of onset in childhood and before service or injury (school accommodations, childhood prescriptions, accession records, collateral history), which structured interviews such as the DIVA-5 are designed to elicit.

In the record, undiagnosed adult ADHD tends to show up as its consequences. The best adult EHR model to date, built on Swedish regional data, ranked **F15.8 (other stimulant-related disorders) and Y90.3 (blood alcohol evidence) among its strongest predictors** of an ADHD diagnosis six months later ([Hamed 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/early-detection-of-adults-adhd-using-electronic-health-records-a-machine-learning-study/C84C0CA747621B55238E6ED384120176)). Women are diagnosed later and often first labeled with depression or anxiety, with the longest delays, more than seven years on average, among women with comorbid anxiety (secondary) ([Int J Womens Health review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2147/IJWH.S511297); [Sci Rep 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12218314/)), and some late-diagnosed women regard an earlier BPD diagnosis as a misdiagnosis ([Asia Pac J Soc Work](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/18387357.2023.2268756)). Insomnia affects about 67% of people with ADHD against 29% of controls, and delayed sleep phase is more common (secondary) ([IJWH review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2147/IJWH.S511297)). For the screening step, the 6-item DSM-5 **ASRS-5 at a cutoff of 14** showed sensitivity 91.4%, specificity 96.0% and AUC 0.94 in the general population but specificity of only 74.0% in an ADHD clinic (secondary) ([ASRS-5 summary](https://lifebylogic.com/glossary/asrs-screener/)), and the older ASRS v1.1 reached sensitivity 0.84 and specificity 0.66 among 1,138 SUD patients ([van de Glind](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4083506/)). The ASRS v1.1 symptom checklist has a LOINC panel, PhenX **62750-5** ([Findacode](https://www.findacode.com/loinc/62750-5--phenx-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-symptoms-protocol-adult-121501.html)), but no code for the 6-item screener was verified, and no evidence was found that VA stores ASRS scores as structured data. With specificity this low in comorbid populations, the EHR flag should set the pre-test probability and the screener should confirm, not the reverse.

The evidence supports a starting heuristic rather than a validated rule: refer when one high-weight signal co-occurs with two or more modifiers, or when two or more high-weight classes appear within about three years, then calibrate against chart-reviewed cases. Injury, crash and legal features skew male, so women are likely to be under-flagged by an unadjusted score; sex-specific thresholds or a sex-stratified model are warranted, consistent with later diagnosis in women and their higher relative risk.

### Autism: the highest relative suicide risk with the least veteran evidence

Adult autism is common and mostly unrecognized, and nobody has counted it in veterans. About **2.21% of US adults (about 5.4 million)** were modeled to be autistic in 2017, counting diagnosed and undiagnosed people (civilian) ([Dietz 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32390121/)), and a reanalysis of English primary-care data concluded that **more than 89% of autistic adults aged 40 or older are undiagnosed** (secondary) ([King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/up-to-90-of-middle-aged-and-older-autistic-adults-are-undiagnosed-in-the-uk-new-review-finds)). No published estimate of autism prevalence in US veterans or service members was found, and a clinician's August 2026 column states there are no robust estimates and no longitudinal studies of suicide risk in autistic veterans, reporting anecdotally that such veterans are often first labeled with PTSD, depression, GAD, ADHD or TBI (secondary, opinion) ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-forgotten-women/202608/the-clockwork-men)). Autism is disqualifying for enlistment, with possible waivers (secondary) ([MilitaryPrepHub](https://www.militaryprephub.com/blog/can-you-join-military-with-autism.html)), and Board of Veterans' Appeals decisions show veterans seeking service connection for it ([BVA A23032475](https://www.va.gov/vetapp23/Files11/A23032475.txt)). The implication is that autistic veterans who served did so largely undiagnosed and masking, which is precisely the group the evidence below identifies as highest risk.

The relative risks are the largest of any condition in this report, and all come from civilian data. In a Swedish matched cohort, autism carried a **suicide OR of 7.55 (6.04–9.44)**, the largest of any cause of death, rising to **9.40 (7.43–11.9) without intellectual disability** and **13.05 (8.73–19.5) in autistic women**; all-cause mortality OR was 2.56 ([Hirvikoski 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11833514/)). A Danish cohort of about 6.56 million people found **more than threefold rates of both suicide attempt and suicide**, and the attempt rate ratio **increased with age at first autism diagnosis** ([Kõlves 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2774853)). A meta-analysis of 31 studies gave adults a **self-harm OR of 3.97 (3.11–5.01) and a suicidality OR of 3.84 (2.78–5.30)** ([Blanchard 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785235)), and another of 36 studies covering 48,186 autistic people without intellectual disability found pooled prevalence of 34.2% for ideation, 21.9% for plans and **24.3% for attempts or suicidal behavior** ([Newell 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10018918/)). In US data, 1.7% of 2.3 million Epic Cosmos patients with autism codes had a suicide attempt, with women carrying a disproportionate and earlier share and men staying elevated into midlife ([Baker 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1940193/full)). In Kaiser Permanente Northern California, 1.79% of autistic adults attempted suicide against 0.32% of controls, and **only 14 of the 27 autistic attempters had a depression diagnosis** ([Croen 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362361315577517)). That last finding is the single most important design input for autism: a flag anchored on depression or a PHQ-9 positive will miss about half of the highest-risk autistic adults.

The mechanisms point to late recognition and masking. Camouflaging autistic traits significantly predicted lifetime suicidality ([Cassidy 2018](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/356ff321-0f9b-4204-a8c4-9f105a0cca65)), and the association between autistic traits and suicidal thoughts was mediated by camouflaging, defeat and entrapment ([Cassidy 2023](https://www.suicideinfo.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Suicide-Life-Threat-Behav-2023-Cassidy-Is-camouflaging-autistic-traits-associated-with-defeat-entrapment-and.pdf)). Among adults newly diagnosed at a UK specialist clinic, 66% reported lifetime suicidal ideation and 35% had planned or attempted suicide (secondary) ([JADD 2020 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-020-04643-9)). Evidence on whether autistic women carry higher suicide risk is mixed: two large studies found higher attempt risk in women while absolute suicide rates look similar ([Kirby 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11102323/)). If military service rewards conformity and the suppression of visible difference, as seems plausible, autistic service members would be expected to camouflage heavily and be recognized late, if ever, which would place them in the highest-risk stratum; that expectation is an inference with no veteran data behind it. In the record, autistic adults show higher rates of every major psychiatric diagnosis (depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, OCD, schizophrenia) and of nearly all medical conditions, including seizures, obesity, diabetes and hypertension ([Croen 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362361315577517)), and personality-disorder labels appear among autism suicide risk factors ([systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322576962_Systematic_Review_of_Suicide_in_Autism_Spectrum_Disorder_Current_Trends_and_Implications)). The characteristic signature is therefore a long trail of psychiatric diagnoses that change over time and never quite fit.

Screening instruments are weaker for autism than for ADHD, and masking makes self-report less reliable. The **AQ-10 at a cutoff of 6** reached sensitivity 0.88, specificity 0.91 and PPV 0.85 in its development sample and is recommended by NICE for deciding whether a full assessment is needed (secondary) ([AIDE Canada](https://aidecanada.ca/resources/learn/asd-id-core-knowledge/screening-tests-and-tools-for-autism-in-adulthood-examples-and-cautions)), but in a Dutch referral clinic the RAADS-R had the highest sensitivity (73%) and the AQ short forms the highest specificity (70–72%) (secondary) ([Sizoo 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279251183_Predictability_of_Self-report_Questionnaires_RAADS-R-NL_AQ-28_and_AQ-10_in_the_Assessment_of_Autism_Spectrum_Disorders_in_Adults)). The **RAADS-14 at a cutoff of 14** had sensitivity 97% but specificity only 46–64% in adult psychiatric outpatients ([Eriksson 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3907126/)); vendor claims of RAADS-R sensitivity above 97% conflict with clinic data in which an above-cutoff score carried only a 34.7% chance of an autism diagnosis, and a 2024 psychometric study judged the RAADS-R inadequate as a stand-alone screen ([PubMed 38305196](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38305196/)). The CAT-Q measures camouflaging and is not a diagnostic screen ([ScienceWorks](https://www.scienceworkshealth.com/post/adult-autism-screening-scores-what-aq-10-raads-14-and-cat-q-can-and-cannot-tell-you)). No LOINC codes for adult autism instruments were verified, so scores will need local codes or a QuestionnaireResponse representation. A negative AQ-10 should not override a strong EHR pattern, and the diagnostic step belongs to a clinician using tools such as the ADOS-2 Module 4 or ADI-R with a clinical interview.

### PTSD: a modest independent risk that amplifies every comorbidity

PTSD is the label most associated with veteran mental health, yet its independent link to suicide death is the weakest of the major diagnoses. In the VA national cohort it carried the **lowest diagnosis-specific hazard among men and ranked fifth of six among women**, and comorbid PTSD was associated with *lower* risk within a depression cohort ([Ilgen 2010](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)); among VHA patients with PTSD, comorbid SUD significantly raised suicide-death risk ([Women's Health Issues](https://www.whijournal.com/article/S1049-3867(18)30489-4/pdf)), and clinicians rated chronic risk lower for veterans with PTSD in a 2025 VHA study of suicide risk evaluations ([JAMA Netw Open 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830585)). Comorbidity changes the picture sharply: in about 6.7 million VHA users, **11.63% of veterans with comorbid TBI and PTSD had an intentional self-harm diagnosis versus 1.92% with neither**, an adjusted OR of **4.26 (4.15–4.38)** among high utilizers ([Rawat 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10407646/)). PTSD should therefore be treated as a hub: its suicide risk travels through SUD, depression, insomnia, nightmares, pain and firearm access, and it is also the label under which undiagnosed ADHD (via the pre-deployment pathway above) and autism (anecdotally) are likely to hide. The PC-PTSD-5 has verified LOINC codes, panel **102010-6**, score **102017-1** (0–5) and trauma-exposure item **102011-4** ([LOINC 102010-6](https://cdn.loinc.org/102010-6/panel); [LOINC 102017-1](https://cdn.loinc.org/102017-1)), with VA validation identifying **3 as the optimally sensitive cutoff and 4 as the optimally efficient one** ([PMC11306811](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11306811/)). The PCL-5 runs 0–80 ([PMC12479293](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12479293/)), with a provisional diagnosis cutoff of about 31–33 (unverified). For model labels, the Million Veteran Program PTSD algorithm reached **AUC 0.95 and PPV 0.95** against 500 chart reviews ([Harrington 2019](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Validation-of-an-Electronic-Medical-Record-Based-in-Harrington-Quaden/4641923a6ab5ae437c94f10e7742f4a4491ae89e)).

### Depression: the highest-volume signal, but item 9 alone misses a third

Depression was the second-highest diagnosis-specific suicide risk among male VHA patients and fourth among women ([Ilgen 2010](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)), and comorbid depression with alcohol use disorder predicted suicide attempt in the year after VA psychiatric hospitalization ([J Affect Disord 2016](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032715302147)). The PHQ-9 is both the most available signal and the most misleading one. Item 9 carries a VA **HR of 1.92**, and "several days" alone an HR of 1.75 (1.24–2.46) ([Louzon 2016](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.201500149)); in civilian outpatients the one-year probability of attempt or suicide death rose from about **0.4% for "not at all" to about 4% for "nearly every day"** ([Simon 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256610695_Does_Response_on_the_PHQ-9_Depression_Questionnaire_Predict_Subsequent_Suicide_Attempt_or_Suicide_Death)). Yet more than 95% of patients who screen positive on item 9 do not go on to attempt or die by suicide ([CDMRP, Bryan](https://cdmrp.health.mil/dmrdp/research_highlights/21bryan_highlight)), and a third of near-term events follow a negative answer. Depression also acts as a mask: in women it is often the first label given to unrecognized ADHD ([IJWH review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2147/IJWH.S511297)), it accompanies but does not explain half of autistic attempts ([Croen 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362361315577517)), and recurrent depression with poor antidepressant response is the classic presentation of undiagnosed bipolar disorder (inference). PHQ-9 codes are verified: panel **44249-1**, total **44261-6** (0–27) and item 9 **44260-8**, with severity bands of 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, 15–19 moderately severe and 20–27 severe ([LOINC 44249-1](https://cdn.loinc.org/44249-1); [LOINC 44261-6](https://cdn.loinc.org/44261-6)). The PHQ-2 panel 55757-9 and total 55758-7, with a positive cutoff of 3, are unverified.

### Bipolar disorder: the top diagnosis-specific risk in male veterans

Bipolar disorder carried the **highest diagnosis-specific suicide hazard among male VHA patients, HR 2.98 (2.73–3.25)**, and the second-highest among women, **HR 6.33 (4.69–8.54)**; both figures come from abstract copies and should be checked against the paper's Table 2 (verify) ([Ilgen 2010 abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47644897_Psychiatric_Diagnoses_and_Risk_of_Suicide_in_Veterans)). Among VHA patients with bipolar disorder, benzodiazepine receipt was associated with increased suicide risk, though the effect size was not extracted ([J Clin Psychiatry 2025](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/suicide-veterans-health-administration-patients-bipolar-disorder-evidence-increased-risk-benzodiazepine-Receipt-24m15424.pdf)). Bipolar disorder is the condition most likely to be under-diagnosed among veterans already in care, because VA screens routinely for depression and PTSD but only reactively for bipolarity, and because bipolar depression typically presents first and is coded as unipolar depression (inference). Its overlap with ADHD and BPD is the central differential problem for this tool: episodic distractibility and irritability resemble ADHD, but a persistent, childhood-onset course favors ADHD, while mood shifts lasting minutes to hours and tied to interpersonal triggers favor BPD over discrete multi-day episodes (inference). Electronic phenotypes are reasonably precise, with **PPV 0.80 for a coded algorithm and 0.85 for an NLP algorithm** against structured interviews ([Castro 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274401943_Validation_of_Electronic_Health_Record_Phenotyping_of_Bipolar_Disorder_Cases_and_Controls)). The Mood Disorder Questionnaire is usually scored positive at 7 or more of 13 items with co-occurrence and moderate or serious impairment (unverified), and no LOINC code was confirmed.

### Anxiety disorders: weak alone, informative as masks

"Other anxiety disorders" ranked fifth of six diagnoses in male VHA patients and sixth of six in women, though still associated with elevated suicide risk ([Ilgen 2010](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)), and no veteran-specific estimates separating GAD from panic disorder were found. Anxiety earns its place in the tool less as a suicide signal in its own right than as a modifier and a mask. Women with ADHD and comorbid anxiety have the longest diagnostic delays (secondary) ([IJWH review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2147/IJWH.S511297)), social anxiety and isolation are part of the autism pathway through thwarted belongingness ([Humanit Soc Sci Commun 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03578-1)), and long-term benzodiazepine prescribing, particularly with opioids, is the subject of VA target-trial emulations of mortality ([National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28584/chapter/14)). The GAD-7 is conventionally positive at 10 or more, with panel 69737-5 and total 70274-6 (all unverified).

### Psychotic disorders: risk concentrated around discharge and disengagement

Schizophrenia ranked fourth among diagnosis-specific suicide risks in male VHA patients and third in women ([Ilgen 2010](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/210919)); exact hazard ratios were not retrievable and are a gap. The actionable signals are temporal rather than diagnostic: the post-discharge window, gaps in antipsychotic treatment, a first-episode diagnosis and homelessness, which concentrates risk in high-psychiatric-burden subgroups of homeless veterans ([PubMed 35905795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35905795/)). Sleep disorders are prevalent but under-recorded among VA patients with serious mental illness ([PMC10476039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476039)). Autistic adults also carry higher rates of schizophrenia diagnoses ([Croen 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362361315577517)), so a schizophrenia-spectrum label alongside lifelong social-communication differences is a reason to consider autism assessment.

### Sleep disorders: insomnia carries a fivefold attempt signal and is badly under-coded

Sleep is the cheapest strong signal in the record. In a VHA case-control study, adjusted ORs for suicide attempt were **5.62 (5.39–5.86) for insomnia, 2.49 (2.23–2.77) for nightmares and 1.37 (1.27–1.48) for sleep-related breathing disorders** ([Bishop 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31727433/)). Among veterans discharged from VA residential mental health treatment, any sleep disturbance shortened time to attempt (**HR 1.16, 1.02–1.32**), and sleep-related breathing disorder remained significant (HR 1.19) while insomnia and nightmares did not in the adjusted model ([Bishop 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsle.2023.1173650/full)); a conference abstract on about 2 million mid-to-late-life veterans reported an HR of 1.23 (1.20–1.27) for any sleep disorder after full adjustment ([Byers](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10736809/)). The gap between an OR of 5.6 in the case-control design and an HR near 1.2 in adjusted cohorts says much of insomnia's raw association reflects illness severity, but as a flag the severity it marks is exactly what matters. Recorded diagnoses have climbed steeply, with sleep-related breathing disorders rising from **5.5% to 22.2%** of VHA patients and insomnia from **7.4% to 11.8%** between FY2012 and FY2018 ([Folmer 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343094455_Prevalence_and_Management_of_Sleep_Disorders_in_the_Veterans_Health_Administration)), after a sixfold rise in 2000–2010 ([Alexander 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27091538/)); a fourfold increase in six years reflects detection rather than incidence, which means many veterans still carry undiagnosed sleep disorders. Sleep also links to the focus conditions: insomnia and delayed sleep phase are common in adult ADHD (secondary) ([IJWH review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2147/IJWH.S511297)), and obstructive sleep apnea's fatigue and poor concentration can masquerade as depression or attention problems (inference). Conventional cutoffs are an Insomnia Severity Index of 15 or more for clinical insomnia and a STOP-BANG of 3 or more for intermediate-to-high OSA risk (both unverified; no LOINC codes confirmed).

### Traumatic brain injury: a durable 1.5–2x suicide hazard that mimics ADHD

The TBI evidence is the most consistently veteran-specific in this report. VHA patients with a TBI history were **1.55 times (1.24–1.92) as likely to die by suicide** ([Brenner 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21734509/)); in about 1.4 million VHA users from FY2006–2015 the hazard was **2.19 unadjusted and 1.71 (1.56–1.87) adjusted**, and moderate or severe TBI was associated with firearm as the method among decedents (**OR 2.39, 1.48–3.87**) (journal attribution to J Head Trauma Rehabil needs confirming (verify)) ([Hostetter 2019 coverage](https://medschool.cuanschutz.edu/physical-medicine-and-rehabilitation/newsdetail/education/newly-published-study-from-pm-r-faculty)). Post-9/11 veterans with TBI had suicide rates **56% higher** than those without (MRR 1.56, 1.46–1.67), at 90.81 versus 56.65 per 100,000 person-years in 2020 ([Howard 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2808953)), peaking near 100 per 100,000 against 61 without TBI ([Howard 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2838445)), and even mild TBI raised the likelihood of death by suicide, accident or homicide ([Howard 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8837911/)). In 860,892 post-deployment soldiers, military TBI was associated with suicide both directly and indirectly through new-onset PTSD, alcohol use disorder and mood disorders ([Brenner 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2807787)). VA's TBI Clinical Reminder, given to OEF/OIF/OND veterans without a prior TBI diagnosis, asks about a qualifying event, immediate symptoms, new or worsening symptoms and current symptoms, and a positive screen triggers the Comprehensive TBI Evaluation ([VHA Directive 2010-012](https://www.va.gov/optometry/docs/vha_directive_2010-012_screening_and_evaluation_of_possible_tbi_in_oef-oif_veterans.pdf)); the screen is sensitive (about 94%) but only moderately specific (about 59%) ([PMC4055523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4055523)). For labels, a semi-supervised VA phenotype (TBI-PheCAP) reached **AUC 0.92 and PPV 98% at 90% specificity** ([Brain Injury 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699052.2024.2373920)), and the Comprehensive TBI Evaluation was the best-balanced single source ([PMC13477427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13477427/)). TBI is also the condition most easily confused with ADHD: chronic mild TBI produces ADHD-like cognitive and behavioral problems, and stimulant use that began before a TBI diagnosis may reflect pre-existing symptoms ([Am J Med 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002934325007028)), while in civilian children pre-injury ADHD was over-represented (16% versus 10.8% population incidence) (secondary) ([pediatric meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353202539_Association_of_Attention-DeficitHyperactivity_Disorder_Diagnoses_With_Pediatric_Traumatic_Brain_Injury_A_Meta-analysis)). No veteran-validated method separates the two; documented childhood onset is the only defensible discriminator.

### Substance use disorders: the strongest signal in women and a leading ADHD predictor

Every substance use disorder is associated with veteran suicide, and the associations are stronger in women. In VHA data, **unadjusted HRs ran from 1.35 (cocaine) to 4.74 (sedatives) in men and from 3.89 (cannabis) to 11.36 (sedatives) in women** ([Bohnert 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28301070/)); the suicide rate was **75.6 per 100,000 with any SUD against 34.7 for VHA users overall**, and **171.4 per 100,000 with sedative use disorder** ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/03/170316092949.htm)). After further adjustment including tobacco use disorder, any-SUD HRs were about 1.5 in men and 1.9 in women ([Bohnert reply, Addiction 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.13937)), and in the older national cohort SUD was the highest diagnosis-specific risk for women at **HR 6.62 (4.72–9.29)** (verify against full text) ([Ilgen 2010 abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47644897_Psychiatric_Diagnoses_and_Risk_of_Suicide_in_Veterans)). Treatment matters: among more than 60,000 VA patients receiving medication for opioid use disorder, **buprenorphine was the only medication associated with lower suicide mortality in adjusted models, by about 65%**, while methadone was associated only in unadjusted models and naltrexone not at all ([Watts 2022](https://ajp.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.21070700)). SUD codes also carry information about ADHD: stimulant-related and alcohol-related codes were among the strongest predictors of a later adult ADHD diagnosis ([Hamed 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/early-detection-of-adults-adhd-using-electronic-health-records-a-machine-learning-study/C84C0CA747621B55238E6ED384120176)), the ASRS was validated specifically in treatment-seeking SUD patients ([van de Glind](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4083506/)), and a VA Evidence Synthesis Program report reviewed ADHD with SUD in veterans ([VA ESP 2024](https://www.hsrd.research.va.gov/publications/esp/adult-adhd.pdf)). Because coded OUD misses many veterans, NLP of notes for problematic opioid use adds cases ([arXiv 2401.12996](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12996)). These are the most legally protected signals in the record (see Part 3 on 38 U.S.C. 7332 and 42 CFR Part 2). AUDIT-C codes are verified, panel **72109-2** and total **75626-2** (0–12), positive at **4 or more in men and 3 or more in women** ([Findacode](https://www.findacode.com/loinc/75626-2--total-score-audit-c.html); [US Core AUDIT-C](https://hl7.org/fhir/us/core/Questionnaire-AUDIT-C.html)); a score of 8 or more suggesting likely AUD and a DAST-10 positive at 3 or more are conventional (unverified), and no DAST-10 LOINC code was found.

### Borderline and other personality disorders: tripled suicide rates hidden by under-coding

In 5.5 million VHA patients followed through 2017, veterans with any personality disorder died by suicide at **156.5 per 100,000 person-years versus 46.7 without**, with **Cluster B (including borderline and antisocial) highest at 178.5**, PD-NOS at 152.6 and Cluster C at 121.4; the association persisted after adjustment for other mental health diagnoses, and **personality disorder prevalence in VHA was lower than in community samples**, which signals under-diagnosis ([Nelson 2022](https://ifp.nyu.edu/?p=1655582)). In a small sample of 124 veterans, BPD was associated with non-suicidal self-injury disorder (**aOR 7.67**), and NSSI, not BPD, was the independent correlate of suicide attempts ([PMC9004586](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9004586/)); another veteran cohort found NSSI to be the sole independent correlate of lifetime attempts (aOR 4.9), and civilian BPD samples average about 80% lifetime ideation, 52% lifetime attempts and 6% suicide deaths ([PMC13441801](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13441801/)). No BPD-specific veteran hazard ratio was found. BPD sits at the center of the differential problem: adult ADHD–BPD comorbidity is estimated at **18–34%**, partly an artifact of shared impulsivity and emotional dysregulation ([PMC9567630](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9567630/)); BPD and ICD-11 complex PTSD share affect dysregulation, negative self-concept and relational difficulty, and a clinical guide recommends that shared symptoms count toward both while the differential is open ([BJPsych 2023](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/abs/distinguishing-between-icd11-complex-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-and-borderline-personality-disorder-clinical-guide-and-recommendations-for-future-research/ECE472B009FE51D8D11C315EC0475068)); and late-diagnosed autistic and ADHD women report earlier BPD labels ([Asia Pac J Soc Work](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/18387357.2023.2268756)). A BPD code in a trauma-exposed veteran should therefore prompt a structured differential assessment rather than be taken at face value: childhood-onset history points to ADHD, sustained mood episodes to bipolar disorder, chronic interpersonal instability with self-harm to BPD, and trauma-linked disturbances in self-organization to complex PTSD (inference).

### Eating disorders: one in three women veterans screen positive, one in a thousand is coded

Eating disorders are the clearest example of a condition the EHR almost never records. In a national survey, **32.8% of women and 18.8% of men veterans screened positive for a probable DSM-5 eating disorder** (with a screen whose validity in veterans is questioned), while VHA EHR data show **1-year and 5-year diagnosis prevalence of about 0.1% and 0.2%** among post-9/11 veterans, attributed to the absence of routine screening; interview-based VHA studies give 3–4.7% ([review citing Masheb 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11802485)). More than 78% of over 45,000 participants in VA's MOVE! weight-management program reported binge-eating behavior ([DoD Psychological Health Center of Excellence](https://health.mil/Military-Health-Topics/Centers-of-Excellence/Psychological-Health-Center-of-Excellence/Clinicians-Corner-Blog/Special-Populations-and-Topics/Binge-Eating-in-the-Military)). VA's April 2026 fact sheet on women veterans states that eating disorders are associated with greater risk of NSSI, suicidal ideation and suicide attempts, may be associated with suicide death, and should be assessed ([VA OMHSP fact sheet](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/suicide_prevention/docs/FSTP-Suicide-Among-Women-Veterans.pdf)). No veteran study of suicide mortality in eating disorders was found, and the ADHD–binge-eating link was not quantified in the material reviewed. Because codes are nearly absent, the tool must rely on proxies and notes, and women who screen positive for MST, IPV or PTSD are the highest-yield group for eating-disorder screening (inference).

### Chronic pain and opioid exposure: dose, discontinuation and taper speed all carry risk

Several pain diagnoses independently predict veteran suicide after psychiatric adjustment: **psychogenic pain (HR 1.58), migraine (HR 1.34) and back pain (HR 1.13, 99% CI 1.03–1.24)**, while arthritis and neuropathy did not ([Ilgen 2013 coverage](https://m.medicalxpress.com/news/2013-05-pain-conditions-linked-suicide.html)), and specific pain conditions remained associated with attempts in a VHA case-control study ([Boska 2021](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4N2PNgY4)). Among 123,946 VA chronic pain patients on opioids, suicide hazard rose with dose to **HR 2.15 (1.64–2.81) at 100 mg morphine-equivalent per day or more** versus 20 or less ([Ilgen 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4939394)). Stopping opioids was associated with overdose or suicide death in **1,394,102 VHA patients**, with risk rising with prior treatment length from an HR of 1.67 (30 days or less) to **6.77 (more than 400 days)** (verify; HRs from a secondary summary) ([Oliva 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m283); [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356313490_The_Opioid_Safety_Initiative_and_Veteran_Suicides)), though the National Academies rated that study at critical risk of immortal-time bias ([National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28584/chapter/14)). Among 509 VHA patients whose clinicians discontinued long-term opioids, 9.2% had suicidal ideation and 2.4% suicidal self-directed violence within 12 months ([Demidenko 2017](https://read.qxmd.com/read/28807135/suicidal-ideation-and-suicidal-self-directed-violence-following-clinician-initiated-prescription-opioid-discontinuation-among-long-term-opioid-users)). Civilian data show tapering itself carries risk: among 113,618 patients on stable high-dose therapy, a taper of 15% or more was associated with overdose (**aIRR 1.68**) and mental health crisis (**aIRR 2.28**), each 10% increase in taper velocity raised those risks further, and elevated risk persisted 12–24 months ([Agnoli 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8335575); [JAMA 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2793293)). VA already runs STORM, an EHR model that predicts overdose or suicide-related serious adverse events in patients on opioids ([STORM protocol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6339438/)), so the tool should read STORM tiers rather than recompute them. The PEG pain scale has verified LOINC codes, panel **91148-7** and mean score **91147-9** ([LOINC 91148-7](https://loinc.org/91148-7/panel)).

### Psychosocial and military risk factors: the signals that live in notes

Military and social exposures carry suicide hazards comparable to psychiatric diagnoses, and most of them are poorly coded. **Military sexual trauma** was associated with suicide at **HR 1.69 (1.45–1.97) in men and 2.27 (1.76–2.94) in women** among 6.35 million VHA patients screened in FY2007–2011, with screen-positive prevalence of 1.1% in men and 21.2% in women; MST may "negate the protective effect of female gender" ([Kimerling 2016](https://sprc.org/?p=13217)). VA has screened universally for MST since 1999 with a two-item clinical reminder ([Kimerling 2008](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/ps.2008.59.6.635)). **Housing instability** carried adjusted suicide hazards of **1.75 for unstably housed veterans and 1.80 for those at imminent risk** among about 5.85 million VHA patients screened, and use of at least one VHA homeless program was associated with **20% lower suicide hazard** (78% lower with four or more programs, observational) ([VA HSR&D bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USVHACENTER/bulletins/2788f15); [HSR&D brief](https://www.hsrd.research.va.gov/research/citations/pubbriefs/articles.cfm?RecordID=1072)); an older study reported a suicide HR of about 2.7 for homeless veterans (verify) ([Schinka 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033354918755709)). **Separation from service** carries its own hazard: among 1,868,970 separated service members, suicide risk **peaked 6–12 months after separation** and declined only modestly over six years, with higher hazards for men (3.13), shorter service (1.26), Marines (1.55) and Army (1.48) ([Ravindran 2020](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2770538)); in about 3.9 million service members, separation was associated with suicide (**HR 1.63, 1.50–1.77**) whether or not the person deployed, while deployment itself was not (HR 0.96) ([Reger 2015 coverage](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/04/150401115738.htm)), and non-honorable discharge raised risk among 3.6 million separations ([Reger 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2834370)).

**Firearm access** is the most lethal factor and the least coded. About **44.9% of veterans own firearms** against roughly 20% of non-veterans ([Cleveland 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5735043/)), and **one-third of veteran owners store at least one gun loaded and unlocked** ([Simonetti 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29658142/)); the 2024 VA/DoD suicide guideline covers lethal-means safety alongside screening and predictive analytics ([Health.mil](https://health.mil/News/Dvids-Articles/2024/06/07/news473280)). No ICD-10-CM code records access, so the tool must read Comprehensive Suicide Risk Evaluation and safety-plan documentation or note text. **Intimate partner violence**: women veterans screening positive on VHA's E-HITS screen had **3.80 times (2.10–6.90) the odds of suicidal ideation or behavior** (comparison group unconfirmed (verify)) ([JAMA Netw Open 2020](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2764599)), and lifetime IPV is 33.0% among women veterans against 23.8% among civilian women ([JGIM](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11606-013-2534-x.pdf)). **Legal problems and other social determinants** are where note text matters most: in a nested case-control study of 8,821 veteran suicide decedents and 35,284 controls, NLP-extracted **legal problems (aOR 2.62, 2.38–2.89), violence (2.34) and social problems (1.94)** were independently associated with suicide death after adjustment for psychiatric, substance, pain and other factors ([Mitra 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10018322/)). Justice-involved veterans die at 2–5 times the rate of comparison populations ([Health & Justice 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40352-026-00456-7)), yet the evidence is not uniform: a 2014–2019 VHA cohort found the type of social determinant did not predict suicide mortality after adjustment ([Pandit 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ajad.70158)). Lower social support explained much of the variance in high chronic suicide risk in a national veteran survey, in which only 35.5% of veterans with current suicidal ideation were in mental health treatment ([NHRVS 2019–2020](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/mental/other-mental-disorders/prevalence-correlates-treatment-suicidal-behavior-us-military-veterans-results-2019-2020-national-health-resilience-veterans-study/)).

Data reliability determines how these factors enter the tool. In VHA's operational homelessness definition, **ICD-10 codes alone captured only 41–62% of homeless veterans** that stop codes, the Homeless Screening Clinical Reminder and HOMES records identified ([PLOS ONE 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0279973)), and NLP-extracted determinants added independent predictive information beyond structured codes ([Mitra 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10018322/)), improving post-discharge suicide prediction in about 2.99 million veterans ([npj Mental Health Research 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00120-2)). A Z-code-only rule will under-identify these veterans.

## Part 2: Models must be built for missing labels, rare outcomes and their own success

Part 1 describes what the record contains; Part 2 describes how a Learning Health System team can turn it into validated per-condition referral models. Two prediction targets should be kept distinct. The first, and the project's novel contribution, is a **referral model** for each condition: the probability that a veteran without the diagnosis has a condition that formal evaluation would confirm. The second is a **suicide-risk layer** that sets priority and triggers the safety gate. VA already operates the second in REACH VET and STORM, so the project should consume their outputs rather than rebuild them, and spend its methodological effort on the first, where the evidence base is thinnest and the label problem hardest. In Friedman's learning-cycle terms ([Friedman 2017](https://doi.org/10.15265/IY-2017-004)), this part covers data-to-knowledge and the trial program that closes performance-to-data.

### Phenotyping and predicting the undiagnosed are different problems

The literature conflates two tasks. **Phenotyping** identifies already-diagnosed cases with high precision and is mature: VA's Million Veteran Program PTSD algorithm reached AUC 0.95 and PPV 0.95 ([Harrington 2019](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Validation-of-an-Electronic-Medical-Record-Based-in-Harrington-Quaden/4641923a6ab5ae437c94f10e7742f4a4491ae89e)), TBI-PheCAP AUC 0.92 ([Brain Injury 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699052.2024.2373920)), bipolar NLP phenotypes PPV 0.85 ([Castro 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274401943_Validation_of_Electronic_Health_Record_Phenotyping_of_Bipolar_Disorder_Cases_and_Controls)), and a pediatric eMERGE autism algorithm PPV 0.84–0.885, though its machine-learning version fell to 0.66 on external testing ([Lingren 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0159621)). **Predicting future or undiagnosed cases** is harder. The best adult ADHD model, trained on 2,973 Swedish adults with ADHD against 4,447 adults referred to psychologists without it, reached **AUC 0.79 (0.76–0.81) with sensitivity 0.80 six months before diagnosis**, decaying to 0.76 at 12 months and 0.75 at 18 months, using only clinical codes and demographics ([Hamed 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/early-detection-of-adults-adhd-using-electronic-health-records-a-machine-learning-study/C84C0CA747621B55238E6ED384120176)). A review of EHR models for neurodevelopmental disorders found **AUC above 0.75 but sensitivity below 75% in most studies**, with the best results from tree-based or deep models using subject-level medical history ([J Neurodev Disord 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11566279/)). No adult autism detection model exists, and VA's phenotype library (CIPHER) is the place to check for local definitions ([VA CIPHER](https://phenomics.va.ornl.gov/web/cipher/search?search=autism)). Phenotype algorithms should therefore build labels and exclusions, while prediction models train on data strictly before the first diagnosis, using an index-date design like Hamed's.

### Labels when most cases are undiagnosed: a missing code means unlabeled, not negative

The estimand for a referral model is counterfactual: whether a veteran would be confirmed on formal evaluation if evaluated. Most veterans are never evaluated, so the observed label (a first F90.x or F84.x code after the index date) is a positive-unlabeled outcome whose labeling mechanism depends on access, sex, race, utilization and clinician attention. That violates the "selected completely at random" assumption behind the simplest positive-unlabeled methods, so the team should estimate the class prior and labeling propensity explicitly, drawing on the positive-unlabeled learning literature, which covers both settings ([Bekker and Davis 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-020-05877-5)). The most valuable single investment is a **prospectively screened gold-standard sample**: a random sample of unlabeled veterans, stratified by model score so that high-score strata are over-sampled and later re-weighted, who complete the ASRS-5 and a DIVA-5 interview, or the AQ-10 or RAADS-14 followed by clinician assessment with ADOS-2 Module 4. That one sample yields the class prior, a calibration target, an unbiased evaluation set and the diagnostic-yield baseline the trial needs. For autism it is also the only route to a first estimate of prevalence in veterans. Silver-standard methods stretch scarce chart reviews further: noisy-label phenotyping ([XPRESS](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocw028)), anchor-and-learn ([Halpern 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocw011)), semi-supervised PheCAP ([Zhang 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41596-019-0227-6)), PheNorm normalization of surrogate counts by healthcare utilization ([Yu 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocx111)) and semi-supervised ROC analysis for evaluation with limited labels ([ssROC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.01709)); all of these DOIs were cited from prior knowledge and not re-fetched during research.

Three biases need explicit handling. **Noisy positives**: given the Inspector General's findings, VHA F90.x codes should be combined with a medication fill and specialist encounter and then chart-validated (the composite is a common phenotype pattern, unverified for adults). **Utilization leakage**: EHR PTSD models with AUC 0.85–0.90 turned out to be driven largely by visit frequency ([JMIR 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/63352)), because diagnosis requires contact, and a cost-based proxy label famously encoded racial bias in a population-health algorithm ([Obermeyer 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax2342)); controls should be matched on utilization (Hamed used psychologist-referred controls), utilization features normalized, and performance reported by utilization decile. **Label leakage**: referral orders, neuropsychological testing codes and note text such as "r/o ADHD" must be excluded from features because they are the diagnostic process itself. For the suicide-risk layer, outcomes should be composite (ideation, attempt, suicide death, overdose and other external-cause death), ascertained from intent-coded ICD-10-CM records, VA's suicide behavior and overdose reports and the VA/DoD Mortality Data Repository linked to the National Death Index, with undetermined-intent poisonings treated as possible self-harm. These labels are already contaminated by intervention: REACH VET 2.0 was trained on 2018 data, after the original program began flagging and treating the highest-risk veterans in 2017 ([Peluso 2026](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/3375750-predictive-analytics-direct-clinical-attention-complex-patients-elevated-suicide-risk-enhancement-veterans-health-administration-reach-vet-model)), so any retraining should weight or restrict by logged intervention exposure.

### Data sources: develop in CDW, express in OMOP, deploy through FHIR

The VA Corporate Data Warehouse, accessed through VINCI, is the richest development substrate, and VINCI has offered an OMOP-transformed instance to researchers since July 2017 ([VA OMOP characterization](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6811349); [VINCI](https://www.hsrd.research.va.gov/for_researchers/vinci/)). Expressing features as OMOP concepts (SNOMED CT, RxNorm, LOINC) lets the same model be validated externally in OHDSI and PCORnet networks and on community-care data ([OHDSI CDM](https://ohdsi.github.io/CommonDataModel/); [PCORnet](https://pcornet.org/data/)), and a portable structured-only approach transferred across five heterogeneous health systems in prior suicide work ([Barak-Corren 2020](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2763237)). The Million Veteran Program adds validated phenotypes and survey data ([MVP](https://www.research.va.gov/mvp/)), and health-information-exchange data improve cross-organization capture for veterans treated in the community ([PMC12038995](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038995)). At deployment the model reads FHIR (US Core profiles and Bulk Data export), which matters because VA now runs two back ends: VistA at most sites and the Oracle Health Federal EHR at sites that went live from April 2026 (Part 3). Military history fields (separation date, character of discharge, branch, length of service) need a DoD data feed; which elements of the DoD-to-VA record exchange are available as structured data was not researched and is a gap.

### Feature engineering: time windows, combinations and note-derived signals

Time should be explicit. Look-back windows of 0–30, 31–180 and 181–730 days, with counts, recency and trajectories, capture both the acute signals that drive short-horizon discrimination and the slow accumulation that characterizes undiagnosed neurodevelopmental conditions. The combination heuristics from Part 1 (one high-weight signal plus two modifiers; two high-weight classes within three years) should enter as engineered interaction features, and onset ordering should be encoded relative to anchor events, such as a first ADHD code or stimulant dated after a TBI, or a new alcohol use disorder after separation. Medication features carry much of the signal: stimulant fills including non-VA fills, count of distinct antidepressants, opioid MME trajectory, taper velocity, benzodiazepine overlap, buprenorphine gaps and naloxone dispensing.

| Category | Examples | Referral model | Suicide-risk layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demographics and military | Age, sex, marital status, era, service-connected rating, separation date, discharge character, MST and combat exposure | Core; sex interactions for ADHD and autism | Core |
| Diagnoses | Prior self-harm, MH and SUD codes, pain, TBI, sleep | Comorbidity patterns and onset order | Strongest predictors |
| Medications | Stimulants, atomoxetine, antidepressants, opioids, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics, lithium | Stimulant fills, antidepressant switches | Core |
| Utilization | ED injury visits, MH admissions, no-shows | Strong but confounded; normalize | Core |
| Screening instruments | PHQ-9 item 9, C-SSRS, PC-PTSD-5, AUDIT-C, TBI screen | Silver labels and features | Strong |
| Notes (NLP or LLM) | Childhood history, attention and sensory language, ideation, life events | Potentially high | Incremental |
| Social determinants | Housing, legal, IPV, financial strain | Incremental | Incremental, equity-relevant |

Notes are where under-coded domains live. NLP-extracted social determinants were independently associated with suicide death in veterans ([Mitra 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10018322/)) and improved post-discharge prediction at 7–180 days ([npj Mental Health Research 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00120-2)), and VA groups are extracting life events (legal, financial, relationship) for acute risk prediction ([medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.03.25321612.full.pdf)). Large language models are best used for note-level extraction inside the VA enclave, feeding a conventional calibrated model, rather than for end-to-end risk scores. A fine-tuned RoBERTa reached F1 0.81 on multi-label suicidality phenotypes from 500 annotated notes ([arXiv 2409.18878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18878)), and a locally hosted Llama-2 was tested for privacy-preserving suicidality classification ([PMC11669470](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11669470/)), but GPT-4 averaged only about **50% accuracy generating computable phenotypes** from standardized vocabularies ([SHREC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12904566/)) and LLM-drafted algorithms were too broad or too narrow ([JAMIA 2024](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/31/9/1994/7645319)). The extraction step needs its own annotated gold standard, per-concept precision and recall, subgroup error analysis, pinned model versions and drift monitoring as note templates change; how suicidality labels are constructed strongly shapes measured performance ([arXiv 2606.19637](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19637)). No published LLM or EHR foundation-model study of adult ADHD or autism detection was found.

### Modeling approaches: start with penalized regression and make complexity earn its place

The VA precedent is instructive. REACH VET's operational model is a **penalized logistic regression with 61 predictors**, chosen because it matched the sensitivity of a 381-predictor proof of concept at the target thresholds ([Kessler 2017, via citing paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00143-9)); MHRN used penalized (LASSO) regression across about 20 million visits (unverified) ([Simon 2018](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17101167)); and Vanderbilt's VSAIL used random forests ([Walsh 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2777425)). A sensible sequence is a penalized logistic baseline, then gradient-boosted trees, then sequence or foundation models only if they improve calibrated net benefit on the gold-standard sample. The architecture should be hybrid: deterministic, auditable rules (expressed in CQL, Part 3) handle the suicide-safety gate, exclusions and hard triggers, while learned models rank veterans within eligible pools. Per-condition models can share a feature store, and multi-task learning across ADHD, autism and related outcomes is worth testing given shared signals. Sex should enter through interactions or stratification, because the female presentation of both focus conditions differs. Class imbalance is best handled with cost-sensitive losses or no resampling at all; resampling distorts calibration and must be followed by recalibration ([Van Calster 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7)). Per-patient SHAP reason codes give clinicians the basis for each recommendation, which is also what keeps the tool on the non-device side of FDA's CDS criteria (Part 3), though SHAP on correlated EHR features is attribution, not causation. Horizon matters: VSAIL discrimination reportedly rose from about 0.84 at 720 days to about 0.92 at 7 days before an attempt (verify) ([Walsh 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702617691560)), so a referral model (months-to-years horizon) and the suicide layer (days-to-weeks) should not share one horizon. One caution from the suicide literature favors the project: machine-learning suicide models largely re-weight known risk factors and do not find patients lacking them ([PubMed 36044603](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/36044603)), whereas a referral model for undiagnosed ADHD or autism targets a distinct, under-recognized mechanism and could add information that existing suicide models lack.

### Validation and calibration: temporal, external, silent and person-level

Three VA and civilian exemplars define the validation stack. REACH VET 2.0 was trained on 2018 data and tested on twelve monthly 2019 cohorts against the incumbent model, achieving **C 0.76 versus 0.69** and capturing **5.6 versus 3.6 suicide deaths per monthly top-tier list** ([Peluso 2026](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/3375750-predictive-analytics-direct-clinical-attention-complex-patients-elevated-suicide-risk-enhancement-veterans-health-administration-reach-vet-model)). VSAIL ran silently in real time on **77,973 patients** before any alert fired, revealing setting-specific performance that required recalibration ([Walsh 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2777425)), and was later validated in US Navy primary care ([VUMC News](https://news.vumc.org/2023/11/17/study-validates-use-of-vumc-suicide-risk-model-in-navy-primary-care/)). Barak-Corren's model detected 38% of first attempts at 90% specificity across 3.7 million patients in five systems, about two years ahead ([Barak-Corren 2020](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2763237)). The recommended stack is an internal temporal split (train on years T, test monthly on T+1), external validation in held-out VISNs and civilian OMOP data, a silent prospective run of at least 6–12 months, and then randomized rollout. Evaluation should be person-level, because visit-level evaluation lets frequent attenders dominate the metrics ([informative cluster size](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351843122_Clinical_risk_prediction_models_and_informative_cluster_size_Assessing_the_performance_of_a_suicide_risk_prediction_algorithm)), and should report AUROC, AUPRC, sensitivity and PPV at the top 0.1%, 1% and 5%, calibration slope, intercept and integrated calibration index ([Van Calster 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7)), and decision curves at threshold probabilities matched to intervention cost ([Vickers and Elkin 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/0272989X06295361)), all stratified by race, ethnicity, sex, age band, rurality and utilization decile. For referral models the reference standard must be the prospectively screened sample, not ICD codes. Reporting should follow **TRIPOD+AI** for development and validation ([Collins 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078378)), **PROBAST+AI** for risk-of-bias self-audit ([Moons 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-082505)), **DECIDE-AI** for early live evaluation ([Vasey 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-01772-9)) and **SPIRIT-AI and CONSORT-AI** for trial protocols and reports ([SPIRIT-AI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-020-1037-7); [CONSORT-AI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-020-1034-x)); these citations come from prior knowledge and their item counts should be confirmed.

### The low-PPV problem: size lists to capacity and pair them with cheap, safe actions

Rare outcomes guarantee low positive predictive value however good discrimination becomes. A systematic review found most suicide models had **AUC of 0.80 or more but PPV for suicide death of 0.01 or less** ([Belsher 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30865249/)). REACH VET's top 0.1% captures only about **2–3% of VA suicides** ([Kessler 2017, via citing paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00143-9)), and its PPV for the combined attempt-or-death outcome is about **5.2%**, with an average of 324.8 flagged veterans per month attempting or dying by suicide ([PMC12535588](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12535588/)). MHRN's top 5% of mental-health-visit scores captured **43% of 90-day attempts and 48% of suicide deaths**, but only 5.4% of those patients attempted and 0.26% died ([Simon 2018](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17101167)). Army STARRS concentrated 52.9% of post-hospitalization suicides in the 5% of hospitalizations with the highest predicted risk, at 3,824 per 100,000 person-years ([Kessler 2015](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/1935484)). The field has answered in two ways. Kessler and colleagues argue for judging models by the net benefit of the paired intervention rather than PPV ([Kessler 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-019-0531-0)), cost-effectiveness modeling suggests current accuracy suffices for low-cost interventions but not costly ones ([Ross 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0089)), and MHRN investigators note that some attempt models match the PPV of accepted clinical tools ([Simon, Shortreed and Coley](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/article-abstract/2737195)). Operationally, REACH VET adopted a **rank-and-review** design: a fixed top 0.1% per facility per month, sized to clinician bandwidth and paired with low-harm actions ([Psychiatric Services](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/full/10.1176/appi.ps.202100629)).

For this project the relevant PPV is the **cascade PPV** (EHR flag, then screener, then confirmed diagnosis), and the cost of a false positive at the first step is a questionnaire, not an intervention. Bayes' rule shows why the EHR flag must enrich before the screener confirms. Using the ASRS-5's clinic-sample operating characteristics (sensitivity 0.919, specificity 0.74; secondary), a positive screen implies about a **16% probability of ADHD if the flagged tier's prevalence is 5%, about 28% at 10% and about 47% at 20%** (illustrative calculation by the report author). Each referral consumes a scarce adult evaluation, so the flagged list should be sized to evaluation capacity per clinic, thresholds chosen by decision-curve analysis at the cost ratio of an unnecessary evaluation versus a missed diagnosis, and the expected funnel planned against VA's own Risk ID yields, where 3.5% of ambulatory veterans screened positive on the primary screen and 0.4% on the secondary ([Bahraini 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7578771)).

### Fairness: audit discrimination and calibration, not just inputs

Subgroup failures are documented in the models this project would sit beside. An MHRN suicide-death model performed well for White, Hispanic and Asian patients but poorly for **Black, American Indian/Alaska Native and unrecorded-race patients**, with AUCs reported at about 0.56–0.64 against 0.83 for non-Hispanic White patients (range from a secondary review) ([Coley 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2778923)); a VA analysis examined machine-learning suicide prediction by race and ethnicity ([Epidemiol Psychiatr Sci](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/epidemiology-and-psychiatric-sciences/article/using-machine-learning-to-predict-suicide-risk-among-veterans-variation-by-race-and-ethnicity/3447D64654D931E76F19BE2D0B80413E)). A 2024 investigation found the original REACH VET weighted being a white or divorced man more heavily than risk factors that mainly affect women and omitted MST and IPV ([Fuller Project](https://fullerproject.org/story/artificial-intelligence-veteran-suicide-prevention-algorithm-favors-men/)); REACH VET 2.0 added MST and IPV and removed race and ethnicity as inputs ([Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2024/10/va-updating-its-ai-suicide-risk-model-reach-more-women/400377/)), and an evaluation found the original model did not accurately identify justice-involved veterans who died by suicide ([Council on Criminal Justice](https://counciloncj.org/can-suicide-be-predicted-for-justice-involved-veterans/)). Removing protected attributes does not guarantee group calibration, so audits must cover discrimination, calibration and intervention effects by race and ethnicity, sex, age, rurality, MST history, justice involvement and utilization decile. For this project the sharpest fairness risk is sex: ADHD and autism features drawn from injuries, crashes and legal involvement skew male, while women are diagnosed later, carry the highest autism relative risk and were the group REACH VET under-served. HHS's Section 1557 rule requires covered entities to identify and mitigate discrimination risk in patient-care decision support tools ([Federal Register 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/06/2024-08711/nondiscrimination-in-health-programs-and-activities)); whether it binds VA's own programs is unclear, but it is a useful benchmark alongside VA's Trustworthy AI principle of "fair and equitable" ([VA Trustworthy AI](https://www.mdedge.com/content/implementing-trustworthy-ai-va-high-reliability-health-care-organizations/page/0/1)).

### Four precedents define what to copy and what to avoid

The rigorous evaluations of model-triggered programs give a mixed and instructive record.

| Program | Design | Population and scale | Key result | Lesson for this project |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| REACH VET (VA) | Quasi-experimental triple differences; top 0.1% vs sub-threshold 0.3–0.1% tier | N=173,313 (40,816 eligible) | More visits and safety plans, fewer MH admissions and ED visits, **fewer documented attempts (ADT −0.05)**; no mortality difference ([McCarthy 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2790743)); mortality HRs 1.03–1.04 to 24 months ([Dent 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2836124)) | Without randomization, evaluation is reconstructed after the fact; expect process gains without detectable mortality change |
| STORM (VA) | **Randomized stepped-wedge** of a case-review mandate, all 140 VA medical centers, 2018–2020 | Patients on opioids flagged high-risk | Mandated review cut 4-month **all-cause mortality (OR 0.78, 0.65–0.94)**; reviews about 5 times as likely; no effect on serious adverse events ([Strombotne 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-022-07617-y)) | Randomize the mandate, not access to the model; VA operations can run national randomized rollouts |
| VSAIL (Vanderbilt) | Encounter-randomized interruptive vs non-interruptive CDS | 561 patients, 596 encounters, 3 neurology clinics | Decision to screen **42% vs 4%** (OR 17.70); documented assessments 22% vs 4% ([Walsh 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2828654)) | CDS format is a large-effect component; cheap A/B tests should precede outcome trials |
| SPOT (MHRN) | Three-arm pragmatic RCT triggered by PHQ-9 item 9 | 18,882 outpatients, 4 systems | Care management HR 1.07 (0.84–1.37); **online DBT-skills outreach HR 1.29 (1.02–1.64), more self-harm** ([Simon 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35166800/)) | Signal-triggered outreach can harm; self-harm must be a monitored safety outcome |

Other evaluations sharpen the picture. VA's Caring Letters trial randomized more than 102,000 Veterans Crisis Line callers to clinician- or peer-signed letters and found no association with suicide attempts but increased mental health engagement ([UW News](https://newsroom.uw.edu/news-releases/caring-letters-show-mixed-results-for-veterans-in-crisis)); the non-randomized SPI+ evaluation in VA emergency departments reported about 45% fewer suicidal behaviors (verify) ([Stanley 2018](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.1776)); and Risk ID's first year covered 4.1 million veterans with ambulatory visits across 140 medical centers ([Bahraini 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7578771)). The closest randomized evidence for adult ADHD screening is a small factorial trial of ASRS layouts in Canadian primary care, which found the standard ASRS did not raise screen-positive rates ([Frontiers in Psychiatry 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1646293/full)); **no randomized trial of an EHR-triggered adult ADHD or autism screening-referral pathway was found**, so this project would be first.

### A staged validation program from silent mode to a national stepped-wedge

The program should run as a Hybrid Type 2 effectiveness-implementation study, testing the referral pathway's clinical effects and its implementation strategy together ([Curran 2012](https://doi.org/10.1097/MLR.0b013e3182408812); [Curran 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frhs.2022.1053496/full)), with each design choice scored on PRECIS-2's nine domains ([Loudon 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h2147)). **Stage 0** is retrospective development and validation in VINCI. **Stage 1** is silent-mode deployment for 6–12 months, scoring veterans in real time without clinician exposure to estimate calibration, flag volume per clinic, cascade PPV in the gold-standard sample and subgroup performance. **Stage 2** is a series of encounter- or patient-randomized A/B tests of CDS variants (card format, threshold, wording, default orders), powered on process outcomes as VSAIL was and in the spirit of NYU Langone's rapid-cycle randomized operations ([Horwitz 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsb1900856)). **Stage 3** is a cluster-randomized trial across VA medical centers or community-based outpatient clinics, copying STORM by randomizing facilities to "flag plus mandated screening and referral workflow" versus "flag available, not mandated," with covariate-constrained randomization on facility size and baseline screening rates. A stepped-wedge suits a pathway leadership intends to roll out everywhere and needs fewer clusters, but it confounds time with treatment, so a parallel cluster design is preferable if secular trends are likely, such as REACH VET 2.0 changes or national campaigns ([NIH Living Textbook](https://rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/); [Hemming 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h391)). **Stage 4** is an optional SMART embedded in Stage 3 to test outreach intensity among non-responders, for example portal message, then phone call, then warm handoff ([Collins 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amepre.2007.01.022)). Two cheap complements exploit the design itself: a pre-registered regression discontinuity around the model's percentile cutoff, which REACH VET's threshold made possible but which its evaluators analyzed with triple differences instead ([Venkataramani 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i1216)), and a randomized encouragement design that randomizes the nudge rather than access to evaluation when withholding referral is unacceptable ([West 2008](https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2007.124446)). The staggered Oracle Health go-lives offer possible strata for a natural experiment (speculative). Throughout the trial the model version should be frozen, or version changes pre-specified, so that updates do not confound the comparison.

### Outcomes and power: proximal endpoints primary, self-harm as safety

Suicide death cannot be a feasible primary endpoint outside a national program. The outcome hierarchy should run from **process** (share of flagged veterans screened, screen-positive rate, referral placed and completed, time from flag to evaluation), to **diagnostic** (new diagnoses per 1,000 flagged, confirmation rate among those referred), to **treatment** (guideline-concordant treatment within 90 days, such as stimulant or other ADHD medication fills, psychotherapy visits or MOUD), to **safety and distal outcomes** (self-harm, attempts, MH admissions, ED visits, suicide and all-cause mortality as STORM used), to **clinician and system burden** (alert rate per clinician-day, override rate, queue wait times) and **equity** (all of the above by subgroup). Treatment initiation deserves special status for ADHD, because it is the mediator through which the observational stimulant associations (OR 0.57 in VA) would operate. Illustrative sample sizes, calculated by the research team for individual randomization at two-sided alpha 0.05 and 80% power (not from a published source), show the problem:

| Outcome and assumption | Approximate n per arm |
|---|---|
| Suicide death at 35.2 per 100,000 per year, 20% relative reduction | ~1,003,000 |
| Suicide death in a high-risk tier at 1% per year, 20% reduction | ~35,000 |
| Self-harm at SPOT's 3.27% over 18 months, 20% reduction | ~10,500 |
| New-diagnosis yield 0.5% to 1.0% | ~4,700 |
| Referral completion 10% to 15% | ~680 |

Cluster designs inflate these by the design effect 1 + (m − 1) × ICC, about 6.0 for 500 patients per clinic at an ICC of 0.01; published VA ICCs for referral or self-harm outcomes were not found and should be estimated from STORM or Stage 1 data. Strategies for rare outcomes include composite endpoints (as SPOT used), enrichment of high-risk tiers, Bayesian analysis with skeptical priors, non-inferiority framing for safety (no increase in self-harm) and long national follow-up, with analyses scheduled around the multi-month lag of death data.

### Ethics and oversight: waivers, the research–operations line and SPOT's DSMB lesson

Minimal-risk pragmatic trials of CDS and referral workflows can usually proceed under a waiver or alteration of consent if the IRB finds the five conditions of 45 CFR 46.116(f)(3) met ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-A/section-46.116)), as codified for VA at 38 CFR Part 16 with VA-specific additions in VHA Directive 1200.05, last technically amended in July 2023 ([38 CFR 16](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-16); [VA ORD](https://www.research.va.gov/resources/policies/guidance/Description-Amendment3-VHA-Directive-1200-05.pdf)). VHA Program Guide 1200.21 draws the research–operations line: an activity designed for internal VA purposes is operations, but once it is designed to produce generalizable knowledge it is research needing IRB oversight, and combining operational data with additional data for generalizable analysis makes the combination research ([Program Guide 1200.21](https://www.research.va.gov/resources/policies/ProgramGuide-1200-21-VHA-Operations-Activities.pdf)). Retrospective development is research; a silent-mode run led by an operational partner for internal purposes can be operations; randomized trials designed for publication are research and need registration and a data and safety monitoring board. SPOT is the cautionary case: persuading the IRB that the research "could not practicably" proceed without a waiver was harder than the minimal-risk determination, and the default DSMB rule of pausing after each suicide attempt "does not work for this type of trial," causing significant delay and expense ([SPOT ethics discussion](https://dcricollab.dcri.duke.edu/sites/NIHKR/KR/SPOT%20Ethics%20and%20Regulatory%20Discussion.pdf); [Simon interview](https://dcricollab.dcri.duke.edu/sites/NIHKR/KR/Simon%20Interview%20SPOT%206-11-15.pdf)). The monitoring plan should therefore pre-specify self-harm events as expected outcomes, review aggregate rates by arm against group-sequential or Bayesian harm boundaries, keep individual clinical safety response (Risk ID, CSRE, the Veterans Crisis Line) separate from trial monitoring, and be negotiated early. The Ottawa Statement on cluster trials and the Faden learning-health-system ethics framework support oversight calibrated to risk ([Weijer 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001346); [Faden 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/hast.134)). Any patient-facing component, such as a message suggesting a veteran might have ADHD or autism, needs a randomized comparison with self-harm monitored, because SPOT showed that outreach triggered by a risk signal can do harm.

### Implementation science and post-deployment monitoring close the loop

RE-AIM gives the evaluation frame, with reach measured as the share of eligible veterans flagged and screened and its representativeness by subgroup, adoption as the share of clinics and clinicians acting on cards, implementation as protocol fidelity, override rate and cost, and maintenance as sustained screening and stable model performance at 12–24 months ([Glasgow 2019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2019.00064)). CFIR 2.0, developed by VA-affiliated investigators, structures pre-implementation interviews and explains facility-level heterogeneity ([Damschroder 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-022-01245-0)); the ERIC compilation supplies strategies such as facilitation and audit and feedback ([Powell 2015](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-015-0209-1)); and alert-fatigue metrics such as override rates and repeated-alert exposure belong in the burden outcomes ([Ancker 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-017-0430-8)). The partnership route is established: QUERI's implementation roadmap and PEPReC, which partnered with VA's Office of Mental Health and Suicide Prevention on the STORM randomization, provide the operational pathway to a national randomized rollout ([Kilbourne 2019](https://doi.org/10.1097/MLR.0000000000001144); [PEPReC](https://www.peprec.research.va.gov/)).

After deployment the model becomes part of the system it predicts. Calibration drifts as populations, coding and practice change ([Davis 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocx030)); dataset shift from EHR changes is a known cause ([Finlayson 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2104626)), which makes the VistA-to-Oracle Health transition a specific threat; and a widely deployed proprietary model performed far worse in external validation than its developer claimed ([Wong 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2021.2626)). Successful interventions also change the outcome-predictor relationships that retraining relies on, so models become "victims of their own success" ([Lenert 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocz145); [Perdomo 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06673)). The monitoring plan should track flag volume, cascade PPV and calibration-in-the-large monthly by subgroup; trigger recalibration when the calibration slope leaves a pre-set band such as 0.8–1.2 or a subgroup sensitivity gap exceeds a threshold; refit annually with versioning; log every intervention exposure so retraining can weight for it; and keep a small randomized holdout or exploit rollout variation to estimate counterfactual risk. Governance should sit with a board including the Office of Mental Health and Suicide Prevention, the operational owner, AI oversight, ethics and veteran representatives, consistent with VA's practice of continuously monitoring high-impact AI use cases and discontinuing non-compliant ones ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2026/06/23/va-inventory-report-reveals-367-ai-systems-operating-in-healthcare-benefits-and-services/)) and with OMB M-25-21's minimum practices for high-impact AI ([OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)).

## Part 3: An MCP server turns each referral into a logged turn of the learning cycle

The Model Context Protocol is not the model, the training pipeline or the clinical decision. Its role in this project is narrower and more valuable: it is the single governed interface through which AI clients and clinical workflows read FHIR evidence, apply versioned rules and models, and receive an evidence-linked recommendation, with every call logged in a form that the evaluation program in Part 2 can use. The tool it exposes **recommends referral for formal screening; it does not diagnose**, and that boundary should be enforced in the output schema, the card wording, the model card and the intended-use statement alike.

### MCP in late 2026 is a stateless, OAuth-protected tool protocol

MCP is an open JSON-RPC protocol, introduced by Anthropic in November 2024, through which servers expose three primitives to AI hosts: **tools** (callable functions), **resources** (readable context) and **prompts** (templates) ([MCP anniversary post](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2025-11-25-first-mcp-anniversary/)). Since 9 December 2025 it has been governed under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, with roughly 10,000 active servers and client support in ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and VS Code at the time of donation ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2025-12-09-mcp-joins-agentic-ai-foundation/)). The current release, **2026-07-28**, made the protocol stateless: it removed the `initialize` handshake and protocol-level sessions, added a required `server/discover` call that returns server identity, versions and capabilities, added **OpenTelemetry trace-context propagation in `_meta`**, deterministic tool ordering and cacheable results, moved long-running Tasks to an official extension, and replaced server-initiated elicitation and sampling with Multi Round-Trip Requests ([2026-07-28 changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/main/docs/specification/2026-07-28/changelog.mdx); [MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)). Roots, sampling and logging are deprecated, as are the old HTTP+SSE transport and OAuth Dynamic Client Registration in favor of Client ID Metadata Documents; elicitation continues through Multi Round-Trip Requests. Structured tool output, introduced in 2025-06-18, lets a tool declare an `outputSchema` and return machine-checkable `structuredContent` ([2025-06-18 changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/changelog)). Authorization is OAuth 2.1: the MCP server is a resource server that publishes RFC 9728 protected-resource metadata, clients send RFC 8707 resource indicators, and **servers must validate that tokens were issued for them**; token passthrough is explicitly forbidden, and proxies in front of third-party APIs must obtain per-client consent to avoid confused-deputy attacks ([MCP authorization](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/authorization); [MCP security best practices](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/security_best_practices)). OWASP advises treating the entire tool schema as a prompt-injection surface, pinning reviewed tool definitions by hash and validating every input ([OWASP MCP cheat sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/MCP_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html)). The normative 2026-07-28 authorization text was not read directly, so exact requirements should be confirmed against the spec. The server should target 2026-07-28 while remaining compatible with 2025-11-25 clients.

### The server's job in each phase of the learning cycle

VA frames its learning health system as a three-phase cycle adapted from Friedman: data to knowledge, knowledge to performance, performance to data ([VA LHS and QUERI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8332471)). The MCP server instruments all three.

| Phase | What the server does | Standards | What it records |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data to knowledge | Provides the same evidence-assembly logic to cohort builders that it uses at the point of care; long-running cohort exports run as MCP Tasks or outside MCP via SMART Backend Services and Bulk export to VINCI | SMART Backend Services, Bulk Data 2.0, OMOP | Cohort definitions and library versions used for training |
| Knowledge to performance | Runs the safety gate, CQL rules and per-condition models; returns a RiskAssessment-shaped recommendation and a proposed ServiceRequest to an AI assistant or a CDS Hooks card | MCP tools with `outputSchema`; CQL and PlanDefinition; CDS Hooks 2.0.1 | Inputs (resource IDs and versions), rule and model versions, output |
| Performance to data | Captures what clinicians did with each recommendation and what happened next | CDS Hooks Feedback; Provenance; AuditEvent; OpenTelemetry | Accept or override with reason; referral status; screen completed; diagnosis; treatment; safety events |

Three properties make MCP a better fit for this loop than a bespoke API. One interface serves several clients: a clinician-facing assistant in the EHR, a coordinator review agent working a REACH VET-style list, and a researcher agent querying de-identified cohorts can all call the same versioned tools, so the logic that produces a recommendation is identical wherever it is used. Using the same evidence-assembly code for training features and live scoring also guards against training-serving skew (a design recommendation, not a published finding). Per-request trace context, server identity through `server/discover` and structured outputs give natural hooks for the audit trail that a pragmatic trial needs. The evidence base is honest about the limits: a February 2026 scoping review of MCP and agent-to-agent systems in healthcare included 13 studies and **found none with prospective clinical validation in real patients** ([JMIR Preprints](https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/93344/submitted)), and no published example of an MCP server inside a formal QUERI or learning-health-system program was found. This project would be generating that evidence, not relying on it.

### Proposed architecture: deterministic rules first, models second, clinicians always last

The design principle is that the language model never navigates raw FHIR or decides eligibility. Benchmarks show why. On a FHIR-compliant virtual EHR with 300 physician-written tasks, the best of 12 models succeeded **69.67%** of the time ([MedAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14654v2)); agents struggled with FHIR's graph-like structure on 2,931 questions over MIMIC-IV-FHIR ([FHIR-AgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19319v2)); errors on a real hospital EHR came mostly from wrong tool arguments or misread tool output ([EHR-MCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15957v1)); and in more than 127,000 fault-injected episodes, unguarded agents **failed 32.2–67.8% of the time on tasks they otherwise solved**, while a tool-layer guard that verified read evidence improved safe success by 13.7–46.6 percentage points ([MedToolFault](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09942)). The server should therefore do the FHIR joins, flattening, rule evaluation and evidence verification itself, and hand the AI client a pre-assembled, verified summary.

```
  Clinician AI assistant        EHR (CDS Hooks patient-view)      Coordinator / research agents
        |  MCP, OAuth 2.1               |  CDS Hooks 2.0.1                  |  MCP

```

The FHIR abstraction layer is a practical necessity, not a nicety. VA paused Oracle Health deployments in April 2023 and resumed at four Michigan medical centers on **11 April 2026**, the first of 13 planned 2026 go-lives, with 26 more planned for FY2027 ([Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/modernization/2026/04/va-resumes-ehr-rollouts-four-michigan-medical-sites/412807/); [MeriTalk](https://meritalk.com/articles/va-plans-to-roll-out-ehr-system-to-13-sites-in-2026/)); site counts have shifted and should be treated as evolving. Through at least 2028 the server must read VistA-derived FHIR through VA's Lighthouse APIs (the Patient Health API at `https://api.va.gov/services/fhir/v0/r4` and the provider-facing Clinical Health API, FHIR 4.0.1) and Oracle Health Millennium FHIR at Federal EHR sites ([PHAPI CapabilityStatement](https://department-of-veterans-affairs.github.io/va-fhir-cop-mapset-ig/CapabilityStatement-PHAPI.html); [CHAPI CapabilityStatement](https://department-of-veterans-affairs.github.io/va-fhir-cop-mapset-ig/CapabilityStatement-CHAPI.html)). Whether VA's screening instruments surface through these APIs as LOINC-coded Observations, rather than as VistA health factors that need mapping, was not confirmed and is the first integration question to answer.

### Tool, resource and prompt design: read-only, task-specific and evidence-returning

Open-source FHIR MCP servers exist, but they are starting points rather than solutions. WSO2's Apache-licensed server exposes generic search, read, create, update and delete tools with SMART authorization ([WSO2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wso2/fhir-mcp-server/HEAD/README.md)); fhirHydrant adds SMART Backend Services and native audit events ([fhirHydrant](https://mcpservers.org/servers/faulkj/fhirhydrant)); Medplum ships MCP inside its server ([Medplum](https://www.medplum.com/docs/ai/mcp)); and AWS publishes a HealthLake MCP server ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/building-healthcare-ai-agents-with-open-source-aws-healthlake-mcp-server)). As of October 2026 neither Epic nor Oracle Health had published an official MCP server for third-party agents, according to a vendor connector page ([ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/developer/connectors/epic)). Exposing generic write tools to an LLM invites exactly the unsafe mutations the fault-injection study measured, so this server should expose only purpose-built, read-only tools, plus one tool that drafts, but never commits, a proposed order.

| Tool | Purpose | FHIR reads | Structured output |
|---|---|---|---|
| `check_safety_gate` | Detect self-harm signals before any referral | Condition, Observation (93373-9, 93374-7, 44260-8), Encounter, Flag | Gate status with evidence; if positive, directs to Risk ID, CSRE and lethal-means counseling and suppresses other referrals until addressed |
| `get_screening_history` | Instrument scores over time | Observation, QuestionnaireResponse by LOINC | Score timeline (PHQ-9, PC-PTSD-5, AUDIT-C, C-SSRS, ASRS) |
| `get_mh_problem_list` | Diagnoses with onset order | Condition (problem-list and encounter-diagnosis) | Normalized codes, first-onset dates, diagnosis changes over time |
| `get_medication_timeline` | Drug-class exposures | MedicationRequest, MedicationDispense, MedicationStatement | Stimulant fills, antidepressant switch count, MME trajectory, tapers, benzodiazepine overlap, MOUD gaps |
| `get_utilization_summary` | Acute care and engagement | Encounter | ED injury visits, discharges in 90/365 days, no-shows, utilization-normalized counts |
| `get_psychosocial_context` | Military and social determinants | Condition (Z-codes), Observation (SDOH and VA screens), Patient | Separation recency, housing, legal, MST and IPV screen status where available |
| `find_note_evidence` | Concept extraction from notes inside the boundary | DocumentReference | Concept hits with note IDs and span hashes rather than raw text |
| `check_existing_care` | Exclusions and de-duplication | ServiceRequest, CarePlan, Condition | Existing referrals, F90.x or F84.x, evaluations in progress |
| `assess_referral_need` | Run rules and models for one or all conditions | Calls the tools above | RiskAssessment-shaped recommendation per condition |
| `draft_referral_proposal` | Prepare an order for clinician review | None | ServiceRequest with `intent = proposal`; never written without clinician signature |

Resources should carry the versioned knowledge the tools apply: instrument metadata (items, scoring, cutoffs, LOINC codes and licensing; PHQ is copyright Pfizer and the C-SSRS copyright Dr. Kelly Posner, which matters for redistributing item text ([LOINC 44249-1](https://cdn.loinc.org/44249-1); [LOINC 93373-9](https://cdn.loinc.org/93373-9/panel))), versioned value sets of signal codes, CQL libraries and model cards. Prompts should be limited to clinician-facing templates that explain a recommendation from its evidence. Deterministic pre-filters belong in FHIR searches and CQL, such as querying Observations by LOINC code before any model runs, which lowers cost, removes hallucination risk from eligibility logic and makes it auditable. Because sampling is deprecated, any LLM reasoning runs either in the client or in a server-side model the server calls under explicit governance. Multi Round-Trip Requests can ask the clinician to confirm a choice (for example, which evaluation pathway to propose), but servers must not use elicitation to request sensitive information ([2025-06-18 changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/changelog)). A representative structured output looks like this (identifiers illustrative):

US Core 8.0.x aligns with USCDI v5, and US Core **9.0.0 (STU9)** is reported as the current published release ([US Core change log](https://hl7.org/fhir/us/core/changes.html)); US Core represents screening instruments as Observation panels with member item Observations, as its PHQ-9 example shows ([US Core PHQ-9 example](https://hl7.org/fhir/us/core/STU8.0.1/Observation-PHQ9-panel-example-44249-1.xml.html)). USCDI v6 (July 2025) carries a Health Status Assessments class that includes mental and cognitive status, alcohol use, substance use and SDOH assessment ([USCDI v6](https://isp.healthit.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/USCDI-Version-6-July-2025.pdf)). The Gravity Project's SDOH Clinical Care IG 2.3.0 tracks referrals with Task plus ServiceRequest, with completed assessments returned in `Task.output` ([SDOH Clinical Care IG](https://hl7.org/fhir/us/sdoh-clinicalcare/exchange_workflow.html)), which is a ready pattern for closing the loop from referral to screening result. The HL7 **AI Transparency on FHIR** IG (January 2026 ballot) requires a `meta.security` label on every AI-influenced resource as the authoritative indicator, with optional Provenance recording the model, inputs and human verifier, and model details in a Device resource plus a model-card DocumentReference ([AI Transparency on FHIR](https://hl7.org/fhir/uv/aitransparency/2026Jan/index.html)). Element-level detail for RiskAssessment, Provenance and AuditEvent was not retrieved and should be taken from the base R4 specification.

### SMART on FHIR and CDS Hooks put the server inside clinical workflow

The server wears two OAuth hats. Toward MCP clients it is a resource server that validates audience-bound tokens; toward FHIR it is a SMART client with its own credentials, so it must use token exchange or its own grant rather than forwarding client tokens, which the MCP spec forbids. Three SMART patterns map to the server's three modes. Interactive, clinician-context use relies on **SMART App Launch 2.2** with user-level granular scopes such as `user/Observation.rs`; server-to-server scoring and monitoring use **SMART Backend Services** with `system/` scopes; and population extraction for training and evaluation uses **Bulk Data 2.0** group-level `$export`, which Oracle Health supports ([SMART Backend Services](https://hl7.org/fhir/smart-app-launch/backend-services.html); [SMART scopes](https://hl7.org/fhir/smart-app-launch/scopes-and-launch-context.html); [Oracle Bulk Data](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/health/health-ai-application-suite/aibda/bulk_data_access.html)). Whether VA's Lighthouse OAuth exposes the same scope grammar was reported only by an aggregator and needs confirmation.

CDS Hooks is how recommendations reach clinicians who are not using an AI assistant. A `patient-view` service backed by the same engine returns a card such as "Consider referral for adult ADHD screening (ASRS-5): stimulant-related disorder code, three ED injury visits in 24 months, childhood IEP noted. This is not a diagnosis," with an accept/reject suggestion that creates a proposed ServiceRequest and a link to a SMART app showing the full evidence. **CDS Hooks 2.0.1** adds the **Feedback** mechanism that returns accept and override outcomes with override reasons, plus a required card `selectionBehavior` ([CDS Hooks](https://cds-hooks.hl7.org/); [STU2 changes](https://cds-hooks.hl7.org/STU2/changes.html)); that feedback stream is the performance-to-data link that REACH VET's evaluators had to reconstruct from administrative data. Rule logic should be published as CPG PlanDefinitions with CQL libraries, because `$apply` returns "a proposed set of activities" that the caller decides whether to carry out ([CPG $apply](https://hl7.org/fhir/uv/cpg/STU2/OperationDefinition-cpg-plandefinition-apply.html)), which matches the tool's recommend-only role. VSAIL's trial is the warning about delivery format: interruptive cards produced ten times as many screening decisions as passive ones but raised alert-fatigue and false-positive-stigma concerns ([VUMC News](https://news.vumc.org/2025/01/03/ai-tested-for-alerting-clinicians-of-suicide-risk-at-three-vumc-clinics/)), so card format belongs in the Stage 2 A/B tests, not in a design decision made in advance.

### Provenance and audit logging make every recommendation evaluable

Every tool call should produce a log record containing the MCP request ID and OpenTelemetry trace ID, the server version from `server/discover`, the tool name, the rule and model versions, the input resource IDs with their FHIR `meta.versionId`, the prompt-template hash and LLM model ID if a language model was used, the output RiskAssessment ID, the clinician's action from CDS Hooks feedback (accepted or overridden, with reason) and the downstream outcomes as they arrive: ServiceRequest status, screening completed, diagnosis made, treatment started and any safety event. Stored as FHIR AuditEvent and Provenance resources with AI Transparency labels in a research store, this record serves four purposes from Part 2 at once: it ascertains trial process outcomes without manual abstraction, feeds monthly calibration and fairness dashboards, records intervention exposure so retraining can correct for performative effects, and supplies the change-control history for model updates in the style of an FDA predetermined change control plan ([FDA PCCP guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/marketing-submission-recommendations-predetermined-change-control-plan-artificial)). The logs themselves are protected data: they should store resource references and hashes rather than raw sensitive content, and any prompt containing protected content must stay inside the VA boundary. No VA policy on retention of LLM prompts and outputs was found.

### Privacy: HIPAA, 38 U.S.C. 7332 and 42 CFR Part 2 shape what the server may read and repeat

The signals that best predict undiagnosed ADHD are among the most legally protected in the record. Under **38 U.S.C. 7332**, VA records identifying a patient that relate to **drug abuse, alcoholism or alcohol abuse, HIV infection or sickle cell anemia** are confidential; disclosure generally requires written consent with specific elements, and recipients are restricted from redisclosure ([38 U.S.C. 7332](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7332)). The **42 CFR Part 2** final rule reached its compliance date on **16 February 2026**; HHS's Office for Civil Rights enforces it under the HIPAA civil-penalty framework, and HIPAA breach notification now applies to Part 2 records ([HHS Part 2 fact sheet](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/fact-sheet-42-cfr-part-2-final-rule/)). HIPAA's general obligations apply to everything else the server touches, and research use of the logs and Bulk exports requires IRB or privacy-board approval, usually with a waiver of authorization. Five design rules follow. Protected data should be processed only within the VA treatment context for which it was collected. The server needs segmentation logic that filters by FHIR `meta.security` labels or by code, although how VA tags 7332 data in FHIR was not found. Protected content must never be echoed into prompts, logs or outputs that leave the authorized boundary, which in practice means locally hosted or otherwise authorized models. Community-care and health-information-exchange data carry their own consent requirements; a proposed rule to allow VA disclosure to exchange partners on the partner's attestation of consent was not confirmed as final. Finally, outputs to clinicians should cite the existence and date of a protected signal through resource references rather than reproduce protected detail when the viewer's access is uncertain.

### Regulatory position: non-device CDS as long as the clinician can see the basis

FDA's clinical decision support guidance, revised on 6 January 2026 and reissued on 29 January 2026, keeps the four statutory criteria for non-device CDS: the software does not analyze medical images or signals; it displays or analyzes medical information; it supports or provides recommendations to a health care professional; and it enables that professional to independently review the basis so that they do not rely primarily on the recommendation ([FDA CDS guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [FDA criteria page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/your-clinical-decision-support-software-it-medical-device)). The 2026 revision removed the 2022 position that time-critical support fails criterion 3, placed certain "singular recommendations" under enforcement discretion, stayed limited to clinician-facing software and remained silent on AI specifically ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-automation-bias-and-clinical-practice-fda-makes-incremental-updates-to-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance)). A clinician-facing tool that recommends referral for screening and shows the specific Observations, codes, dates and note references behind it fits that carve-out. Three design choices would push it toward device status and should be avoided or separately analyzed: patient-facing outreach or automated triage without clinician review, autonomous order placement, and opaque language-model risk scores whose basis the clinician cannot inspect. If the tool ever became a device, FDA's final guidance on predetermined change control plans for AI-enabled devices (December 2024) and its January 2025 draft on AI lifecycle management would govern updates ([FDA PCCP guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/marketing-submission-recommendations-predetermined-change-control-plan-artificial); [FDA AI lifecycle draft](https://fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/artificial-intelligence-enabled-device-software-functions-lifecycle-management-and-marketing)).

ONC's rules have moved in the opposite direction from transparency mandates. HTI-1 (January 2024) created the decision-support-intervention criterion at 45 CFR 170.315(b)(11), requiring certified health IT to support **31 source attributes for predictive interventions** and intervention risk management ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/09/2023-28857/health-data-technology-and-interoperability-certification-program-updates-algorithm-transparency-and); [ONC DSI resource guide](https://www.healthit.gov/sites/default/files/page/2024-05/DSI-Criterion-Resource-Guide_508.pdf)). The HTI-5 deregulatory rule, proposed on 29 December 2025 ([HealthIT.gov](https://healthit.gov/HTI5)), was **finalized and announced on 26 October 2026**, removing the source-attribute and predictive risk-management requirements and 14 certification criteria in all rather than the 34 proposed, with effective dates reported as 28 December 2026 for most provisions and 1 January 2027 for some; sources conflict on the date for the decision-support criterion, which should be checked against Federal Register document 2026-21943 (verify; based on secondary legal and trade coverage) ([AHA News](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-10-26-onc-finalizes-certain-deregulatory-actions-related-health-data-technology-interoperability); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/hti5-promotes-deregulatory-certification-actions); [McGuireWoods](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2026/10/hhs-finalizes-hti-5-narrowing-health-it-certification-and-deferring-information-blocking-changes/)). In any case these rules bind certified health IT developers such as VA's EHR vendor, not VA's in-house models, so transparency for this tool must be voluntary: a TRIPOD+AI-style model card, the 31 HTI-1 predictive attributes used as a documentation checklist, and the AI Transparency on FHIR IG for machine-readable labels. VA's own requirements apply directly: its Trustworthy AI Framework (purposeful; effective and safe; secure and private; fair and equitable; transparent and explainable; accountable and monitored), its AI use-case inventory, which listed **367 use cases in January 2026, 253 in VHA and 68% high-impact**, and OMB M-25-21's minimum practices for high-impact AI ([VA Trustworthy AI](https://www.mdedge.com/content/implementing-trustworthy-ai-va-high-reliability-health-care-organizations/page/0/1); [Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2026/06/23/va-inventory-report-reveals-367-ai-systems-operating-in-healthcare-benefits-and-services/); [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)). A tool that flags veterans for mental health referral should expect to be designated high-impact and planned accordingly, with human review before any outreach, as VA officials say all its AI suicide-prevention efforts include ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/management/2025/09/ai-based-suicide-prevention-efforts-va-always-have-human-involvement-official-says/408144/)).

## Evidence limitations and items to verify

Two kinds of limitation qualify this report. The first is structural: the evidence the project most needs does not exist yet. There is **no veteran-specific estimate of the association between ADHD or autism and suicide outcomes**, no estimate of autism prevalence in US veterans or service members, no adult autism EHR detection model, no veteran-specific effect sizes for non-suicidal self-injury, eating-disorder suicide mortality, BPD as distinct from Cluster B, social isolation or financial strain, and no randomized trial of an EHR-triggered adult ADHD or autism screening-referral pathway. The second is evidentiary: full texts were blocked during research, so many figures rest on abstracts, search extracts or secondary coverage, and the research streams occasionally disagreed (for example, one stream found the HTI-5 final rule through secondary coverage while another, searching earlier sources, did not). ## Conclusion

The evidence changes what this project should claim to be. For ADHD, the referral tool is best understood as a treatment-access intervention: identification matters because a VA cohort and a large target-trial emulation both associate ADHD medication with less suicidal behavior, so the trial should treat medication initiation as the mediating outcome and self-harm as the safety outcome, and the EHR flag earns its place only if it enriches pre-test probability enough to make a modest screener informative. For autism the honest first deliverable is evidence rather than deployment. The prospectively screened gold-standard sample that the models need would also produce the first estimate of autism prevalence among US veterans, and the finding that half of autistic adults who attempted suicide had no depression diagnosis marks out what a neurodevelopmental referral tool can add that existing suicide models, which largely re-weight familiar risk factors, cannot: a route to veterans whose risk is invisible to depression-anchored screening.

The MCP server's distinctive contribution is not that a language model can read charts, which benchmarks show it still does unreliably, but that it can turn every recommendation into a versioned, attributable and logged event. REACH VET has gone through two quasi-experimental evaluations without resolving whether it changes mortality, partly because the link from flag to action to outcome had to be reconstructed after the fact; a server that records the evidence, the model version, the clinician's response and the downstream result by design, inside a STORM-style randomized rollout, makes the performance-to-data leg of the learning cycle a property of the software rather than a later research project. With ONC's HTI-5 rule removing model-card mandates in late 2026, that transparency now has to come from the project itself, and the AI Transparency on FHIR guide plus TRIPOD+AI reporting are ready substitutes. SPOT's harm finding settles the remaining design question: the safest unit of intervention is a clinician-facing recommendation to screen, randomized at the facility level, never an automated message telling a veteran what they might have.
